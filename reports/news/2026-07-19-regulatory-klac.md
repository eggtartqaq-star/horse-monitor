# Regulatory Brief — KLAC (KLA Corporation)

**Analyst:** The dictator (Dept. 4 — Regulatory) · **Date:** 2026-07-19 · **Status of name:** under `/analyze` review. Companions: `reports/news/2026-07-19-regulatory-mrvl.md`, `reports/news/2026-07-18-regulatory-mu.md`.

All probabilities below are analyst estimates, not sourced figures. This is investment-impact research, not legal advice.

---

## Net regulatory read: **net HEADWIND**, but a materially different shape than a fabless name

KLA is a **directly regulated party** under BIS semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment (SME) controls — its inspection/metrology/process-control tools are the *thing being controlled*, not merely a chip that happens to ship to China. That inverts the MRVL/MU frame: KLA does not benefit from the "own-a-US-fab" tariff/CHIPS offset the way MU does, and it cannot relocate ship-to the way a fabless chipmaker can, because the restriction attaches to the customer (Chinese fab) and the tool's capability, not to KLA's supply chain. Three live pressures stack against a structurally strong franchise: (1) BIS SME rules + Entity List denials that have already compressed China from ~41–43% of revenue (FY2024) toward mid-20s%; (2) China's **50% domestic-equipment mandate** eroding the served-available market from the demand side; (3) **Section 232 Phase 2**, where Commerce has *recommended* tariffs explicitly covering **semiconductor manufacturing equipment** — a category that hits KLA's imported subsystems as an importer. Offsetting tailwind: KLA's process-control moat is the **hardest SME segment for China to localize**, and the US/global leading-edge fab buildout (CHIPS-subsidized) is a multi-year demand driver KLA over-indexes to via advanced-packaging process control (guided ~$1B in 2026).

## 1. US export controls (BIS SME) — KLA is the directly regulated party

**Development & source.** The Dec 2, 2024 BIS package (two IFRs, effective Dec 31, 2024) revised the SME control set — ECCNs **3B001/3B002** and associated software/technology 3D/3E — eliminating 3B090 and folding new categories of controlled equipment into 3B001/3B002, targeting tools used to fabricate logic at **16/14nm or below and non-planar (FinFET/GAA) architectures**, applied to Macau and Country Group D:5 (China). The package also added 140+ Entity List designations (Baker McKenzie, WilmerHale, Covington, Wiley, Holland & Knight, CSIS on the Dec 2024 rule). In 2026, BIS reportedly ordered equipment makers to halt shipments to a major Chinese fab (investingLive, 2026-04-28) — enforcement, not just rulemaking.

**Exposed mechanism for KLA.** KLA's crown jewels — wafer/reticle **inspection** and **metrology** (process control) — are increasingly captured by the 3B001/3B002 revisions and by end-user (Entity List) denials that prohibit sale regardless of tool spec. Unlike a fabless chip that can be re-routed to a non-China customer, an inspection tool destined for a Chinese leading-edge fab is simply a lost sale; there is no ship-to relocation. Practitioner/analyst read: export controls to China could impair **20–25% of KLA sales** at the gross level, with Jefferies estimating a **$300–350M calendar-2026 revenue headwind** specifically from the recent BIS update. China fell from **~41–43% of revenue (FY2024)** to **~33% (FY2025)** and into the **mid-20s%** in FY2026 quarters; management guides China to stabilize in the **mid-20s%** (KLA Q3 FY2026 results/commentary; ainvest; TIKR; sector coverage).

**Relative insulation (the important nuance).** Analysts view KLA as **relatively more insulated** than Lam/AMAT because **process-control (inspection/metrology) is the SME segment China has localized least** — Chinese self-sufficiency is advancing fastest in cleaning/etch/deposition (e.g., Naura in dry-strip), slowest in high-end inspection/metrology. So the *directly-controlled* exposure is real, but the substitutable-by-China portion of it is smaller for KLA than for peers.

**Timeline & probability.** Rules are **final and in effect**; enforcement active. Probability of *further* SME tightening (lower node thresholds, more metrology/inspection ECCNs, more Entity List end-users) in 12 months: **~40–50% (est.)** — this is the single most probability-weighted negative and the one where KLA is most directly named. Probability of *loosening* under a US–China truce: **low, ~15% (est.)**.

**Tripwires.** New Federal Register IFR revising 3B001/3B002/3B993 or adding inspection/metrology parameters · Entity List additions naming Chinese fabs/OSATs that are KLA customers · BIS "is-informed" letters / shipment-halt orders to SME vendors · any change to the de minimis / FDPR footnote treatment for SME.

## 2. China 50% domestic-equipment mandate — demand-side erosion independent of US controls

**Development & source.** China now requires chipmakers seeking state approval to build/expand fabs to prove via procurement tenders that **≥50% of equipment is Chinese-made**, with sub-threshold applications typically rejected (flexibility granted only where no domestic alternative exists). Reported to threaten **>$18B combined China revenue** for AMAT + Lam + KLA (Fintool, TechPowerUp, MarketScreener, Astute Group, The Economy).

**Exposed mechanism.** This is a *revenue-restriction* lever that operates even where US export controls permit the sale — it locks foreign tools out of the addressable China market by fiat. KLA's mitigant is the same as above: the "no domestic alternative" carve-out disproportionately protects high-end inspection/metrology, where Chinese substitutes are weakest. Still, it caps the China recovery optionality and reinforces the structural mid-20s% ceiling management already guides to.

**Timeline & probability.** Policy is being implemented now; enforcement tightens as domestic tool capability matures. Probability it materially expands to cover more process-control categories over 24 months: **~50–60% (est.)**, gated by China's own metrology localization progress.

**Tripwires.** MIIT/procurement directives raising the domestic-content threshold or removing inspection/metrology carve-outs · Chinese inspection/metrology vendors (e.g., domestic process-control entrants) winning leading-edge fab tenders · KLA China service/spares revenue trend (installed-base annuity is stickier than new-tool sales).

## 3. Section 232 tariffs — KLA is on the *equipment* line, and lacks the fab-owner offset

**Development & source.** Proclamation (signed 2026-01-14, effective 2026-01-15) covers **"semiconductors, semiconductor manufacturing equipment, and their derivative products."** Phase 1 imposed 25% on a narrow band of advanced accelerators; Commerce was directed to report by **2026-07-01** and **recommended Phase 2** broader tariffs at a **"significant" rate explicitly including semiconductor manufacturing equipment and derivative products**, paired with a **tariff-offset for companies investing in US semiconductor production/supply chain**. As of 2026-07-19 **no Phase 2 proclamation is published** — decision live (White House proclamation; Morgan Lewis; Baker Donelson; C.H. Robinson; NineScrolls). Practitioner/analyst estimate: **Lam and KLA each ~$350M annual impact** from the broader tariff environment.

**Exposed mechanism for KLA (two-sided, net negative vs a fab owner).**
- *Cost/import risk:* KLA sources optics, precision subsystems, and components globally and assembles tools partly outside the US; a Section 232 SME tariff (and derivative-product coverage) taxes **imported inputs and any offshore-built tools/subsystems entering the US** — a COGS/margin drag borne by KLA as importer, or passed to fab customers as higher tool prices (capex friction that can slow orders).
- *Offset asymmetry:* the recommended **tariff-offset rewards US semiconductor-production investment** — i.e., the fabs (Intel, TSMC AZ, Micron, Samsung), not necessarily their tool suppliers. KLA does US manufacturing/R&D (Michigan/California) which may earn *some* offset eligibility, but the structure primarily protects fab owners. KLA cannot "own a US fab" to neutralize the tariff the way MU does.

**Timeline & probability.** Phase 2 scope notice plausibly Jul–Sep 2026, effective Sep–Oct 2026. Probability some Phase 2 expansion occurs: **~60–70% (est.)**; probability SME/derivative coverage lands in a form that nets negative for KLA after any offset it can claim: **~35–45% (est.)**. This is the second live binary alongside Item 1.

**Tripwires.** Federal Register notice modifying the Jan-2026 proclamation / new proclamation · HTSUS annex touching **8486** (SME) and related subheadings · exact offset-eligibility criteria (does US SME manufacturing/R&D qualify, and at what credit rate) · any country-specific SME carve-out from trade negotiations.

## 4. CHIPS Act & US/global fab buildout — the structural tailwind KLA over-indexes to

**Development & source.** CHIPS-subsidized US fab construction (Intel, TSMC Arizona, Micron NY/ID ~$200B, Samsung Taylor) plus global leading-edge and advanced-packaging buildout drive WFE demand. KLA raised its **2026 advanced-packaging process-control revenue outlook to ~$1B** (large YoY increase); CEO commentary notes KLA does **not need peak WFE** to hit its 2026 plan given share gains, with KLA's share of WFE approaching **~8%** (KLA earnings commentary; AlphaStreet; Henry Fund; ainvest).

**Exposed mechanism.** KLA benefits **indirectly** — it is not a CHIPS grant recipient, but every subsidized leading-edge/advanced-packaging fab is a process-control tool buyer, and inspection/metrology intensity *rises* at advanced nodes and in AI-driven advanced packaging (KLA's fastest-growing pocket). This is a genuine multi-year demand offset to the China compression. Unlike MU, KLA's CHIPS linkage is demand-pull, not a balance-sheet grant/ITC — so no equity-stake overhang risk applies to KLA.

**Timeline & probability.** In force; multi-year. Probability the US/global leading-edge + advanced-packaging capex cycle remains a net tailwind through 2027: **~70–75% (est.)**, cyclicality being the main risk, not policy.

**Tripwires.** CHIPS disbursement/construction milestone slippage at Intel/TSMC-AZ/Micron/Samsung · advanced-packaging (HBM/CoWoS) capex trajectory · KLA advanced-packaging process-control bookings vs the ~$1B 2026 guide · any CHIPS "restructuring"/equity policy that chills recipient fab capex timing.

## 5. China retaliation / cross-border — SME vendors are an obvious pressure point

**Development & source.** China's retaliation toolkit (rare-earth/gallium/germanium/antimony export licensing, "unreliable entity" listings, MOFCOM probes) has been deployed against US measures; SME vendors are a natural target in any escalation. No public action *specifically names KLA* as of 2026-07-19 (MOFCOM's active AD probe covers US **analog ICs**, not SME). China's leverage is more via the 50% mandate (Item 2) and input-material controls than a direct KLA designation.

**Timeline & probability.** Probability China formally targets KLA (unreliable-entity, customs friction, procurement ban) in 12 months: **~20–25% (est.)**, rising if US Phase 2 SME tariffs or further BIS SME tightening land, or if the US–China truce (elements expiring ~Oct–Dec 2026) lapses.

**Tripwires.** MOFCOM/MIIT statements naming SME or KLA · rare-earth/critical-material licensing tightening (KLA optics/precision-material inputs) · truce non-renewal by ~Nov 1, 2026.

## 6. SEC / litigation — clean current slate; only legacy history

**Development & source.** KLA's material SEC history is the **2007 options-backdating settlement** (KLA-Tencor was **not charged with fraud and paid no civil penalty**). Searches surface **no current open SEC investigation or securities class action naming KLA** as of 2026-07-19; recent "KLAR" securities-suit hits are **Klarna Group**, a different issuer — not KLA. KLA discloses only ordinary-course commercial/IP/employment litigation (KLA IR SEC filings; KLA 2007 settlement release).

**Timeline & probability.** No live matter. Probability of a new material SEC/securities action in 12 months: **low, <10% (est.)**.

**Tripwires.** Any 8-K referencing SEC inquiry/subpoena · export-control enforcement disclosure (BIS penalty/voluntary self-disclosure) — the more likely regulatory-legal vector for an SME vendor than a securities case. Verify latest 10-K legal-proceedings note via EDGAR before committee (flag for 巴爺爺).
