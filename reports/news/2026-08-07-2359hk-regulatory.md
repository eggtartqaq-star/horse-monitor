## 0. Bottom line up front

The BIOSECURE Act is **enacted law**, not a proposal. It was signed **18 December 2025** as
part of the FY2026 NDAA. But the enacted version **does not name WuXi AppTec**. It defines
"biotechnology company of concern" (BCC) by reference to lists, and WuXi AppTec was placed on
the operative list (DoD/DoW Section 1260H) on **8 June 2026**.

On **7 August 2026**, Chief Judge James E. Boasberg (D.D.C.) **preliminarily enjoined** the
Department of War from enforcing or giving effect to that 1260H designation, finding WuXi had
shown a likelihood the designation was **arbitrary and capricious**.

So the correct characterisation as of today is: **the statute is in force; the designation
that would capture WuXi is judicially suspended; the operative prohibitions would not bite
until roughly late 2028 in any case; and the nearer binding constraint is a separate DoD
procurement ban with a 30 June 2027 date that the same injunction currently blocks.**

This is **not resolved**. It is **deferred and contested**. See §8 for the anti-anchoring test.

## 1. The BIOSECURE Act — precise legislative status

### 1.1 The full sequence, with the distinctions that matter

| Stage | What happened | Date |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduced (118th Cong.)** | BIOSECURE Act introduced; **named** BGI, MGI, Complete Genomics, WuXi AppTec, WuXi Biologics in the bill text | 2023–2024 |
| **Passed one chamber** | House passed BIOSECURE (H.R. 8333) by wide margin — **passed one chamber only, never became law** | Sept 2024 |
| **Lapsed** | 118th Congress ended without Senate floor passage; **the bill died** | Jan 2025 |
| **Re-introduced (119th Cong.)** | Re-emerged, incl. S.3469 "BIOSECURE Act of 2025"; pushed as an NDAA amendment | Sept 2025 |
| **Senate passage as NDAA amendment** | Senate passed its FY2026 NDAA **including** a revised BIOSECURE amendment. House NDAA did **not** include it — conference risk was live | **9 Oct 2025** |
| **Conference text** | BIOSECURE provisions survived into the final NDAA compromise text | Dec 2025 |
| **ENACTED** | President signed FY2026 NDAA; **BIOSECURE became law** | **18 Dec 2025** |
| **In force / operative?** | **No.** Prohibitions are not self-executing — they require an OMB list, OMB guidance, and a FAR rule (see §2) | pending |

**The distinction the committee must hold onto:** *enacted* ≠ *in force*. BIOSECURE is on the
statute books. Its **operative prohibitions on federal contractors have not yet taken effect
and cannot take effect until the FAR is amended** — first realistically expected **late 2028**
(Hogan Lovells' published estimate).

### 1.2 The single most important change between draft and enacted text

The 2024 draft **named WuXi AppTec and WuXi Biologics in the statute**. The **enacted** version
does not. Ropes & Gray (Jan 2026): *"Unlike the bill introduced to Congress in 2024, the Act
does not specifically name WuXi AppTec, WuXi Biologics, BGI, MGI, or Complete Genomics as
BCCs."* Instead a BCC is:

- **(A)** an entity on the **DoD Section 1260H list** of "Chinese military companies operating
  in the United States" **that is also involved in** manufacturing/distribution/provision/
  procurement of biotechnology equipment or services — **and**, per the reconciled text,
  requires an **OMB determination**, not automatic capture; **or**
- **(B)** an entity named through a separate **criteria-based OMB process**.

At enactment (Dec 2025) the 1260H list contained **BGI Group** but **not** WuXi AppTec, WuXi
Biologics, or Complete Genomics. Contemporary press correctly reported this as a **"reprieve"**
for the WuXi entities. That reprieve lasted **under six months**.

### 1.3 What actually captured WuXi — the 1260H listing

- **18 Dec 2025** — the same day BIOSECURE was signed — Reps. Moolenaar, Garbarino, Crawford
  and Sen. Rick Scott sent a letter to Secretary of War Hegseth urging that **WuXi AppTec,
  WuXi Biologics and WuXi XDC** (plus DeepSeek, Gotion, Unitree) be added to 1260H, calling
  them "the next logical tranche" of military-civil fusion firms.
- **8 June 2026** — the **Department of War published its annual 1260H list: 188 companies,
  65 newly added**, including **WuXi AppTec Co., Ltd.**, **Complete Genomics**, and
  **Novogene**. The stated basis: indirect ownership by SASAC and indirect affiliation with
  SASTIND and the PLA.
- **WuXi Biologics (2269.HK) and WuXi XDC (2268.HK) were NOT added.** Despite being named in
  the December congressional letter, they are **not** on the 2026 1260H list and are **not**
  currently BCCs by either route.
- **8 June 2026** — WuXi AppTec publicly called the designation **"mistaken and baseless,"**
  asserting it is an independent, publicly traded company with no government or military
  ownership, control or affiliation.

### 1.4 The litigation — the decisive fact of the last 60 days

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 11 Jun 2026 | WuXi AppTec files suit, *WuXi AppTec Co., Ltd. v. U.S. Dep't of Defense*, **No. 1:26-cv-02069 (D.D.C.)**, against DoD/DoW, Sec. Hegseth and deputies, challenging the 1260H designation |
| 29 Jun 2026 | WuXi moves for a **preliminary injunction**, citing customers "suspending relationships" |
| **7 Aug 2026** | **Chief Judge James E. Boasberg grants the preliminary injunction.** DoD is "preliminarily ENJOINED from enforcing, implementing, or otherwise giving effect to" the 1260H designation. 35-page opinion. WuXi "established a likelihood that the designation was arbitrary and capricious"; the court found defence officials "repeatedly misread the evidence." Boasberg: *"A scarlet letter sends a clear message: keep away."* |
| 7–10 Aug 2026 | Pentagon spokesperson **declines to say whether DoD will appeal** ("does not comment on ongoing litigation"). **No appeal noted as filed as of 2026-08-10.** |

**Critical qualifier:** a preliminary injunction is **interim relief pending final judgment**.
It is not a merits victory, it is not vacatur of the designation, and it is appealable to the
D.C. Circuit. It rests on a *likelihood* of success. The listing is suspended, **not erased**.

**Evidentiary note from the opinion that cuts the other way:** Boasberg's irreparable-harm
finding was grounded in the fact that customers and suppliers **had already** "cancelled
contracts, terminated longstanding relationships, and moved their business to competitors."
That is a judicial finding that **commercial damage from mere listing is real and occurred
within eight weeks** — before any statutory prohibition was operative. This is the single most
investment-relevant sentence in the record: **the market punishes the label, not the effective
date.**

## 2. Operative dates, grandfathering, and who is restricted from what

### 2.1 The implementation chain (all conditional, none complete)

1. **Enactment** — 18 Dec 2025. ✅ done
2. **OMB publishes initial BCC list** — due within one year of enactment, i.e. **by 18 Dec 2026**. ⏳ not yet published
3. **OMB issues implementing guidance** — up to **180 days** after list publication (→ ~mid-2027)
4. **FAR Council amends the FAR** — within **one year** after OMB guidance (→ ~mid-2028)
5. **Prohibitions effective** —
   - **60 days after FAR revision** for entities designated via the **1260H** route
   - **90 days after FAR revision** for entities designated via the **criteria-based OMB** route

**Net: first realistic bite ≈ late 2028.** Hogan Lovells states the effective date for
particular BCCs "is first expected to occur in **late 2028**."

### 2.2 What is actually prohibited — narrower than headlines suggest

The prohibitions attach **only where there is a federal nexus**. Executive agencies may not:

1. **Procure** biotechnology equipment or services from a BCC;
2. **Contract with entities that use** a BCC's equipment/services **in the performance of**
   that federal contract (including via subcontractors);
3. **Make loans or grants for the purpose of** procuring such equipment/services.

**What is NOT prohibited:** a private pharmaceutical or biotech company with no federal
contract, grant or loan is **not barred from using WuXi AppTec.** Medicare and Medicaid
agreements are not FAR contracts and are **outside scope**. This matters enormously: WuXi's
revenue base is overwhelmingly **commercial pharma R&D and manufacturing**, not federal
procurement. The direct statutory revenue-at-risk is far smaller than the headline implies.

**The real transmission mechanism is therefore reputational and precautionary, not statutory** —
large pharma with *any* federal touchpoint (BARDA, NIH grants, DoD contracts, IRA/BioPreferred
adjacency), plus boards and audit committees managing headline risk, pre-emptively de-risk
supply chains years ahead of any deadline. Boasberg's own findings confirm this happened.

### 2.3 Grandfathering

- Contracts entered into **before** the applicable effective date are **grandfathered for five
  years** from the date the FAR is revised with respect to that BCC.
- For a **newly designated** BCC, contracts predating the designation's effective date are
  grandfathered five years from that effective date.
- **Important carve-out:** grandfathering is **unavailable** for existing contracts with
  companies that were on the **1260H list as of 18 December 2025**. WuXi AppTec was **not** on
  the list on that date (added 8 June 2026), so on current facts **WuXi contracts would
  qualify for the five-year grandfather** — pushing full effect toward the early-to-mid 2030s
  for pre-existing work.

### 2.4 The nearer, harder deadline that most coverage misses — FY2024 NDAA § 805

Independent of BIOSECURE, **Section 805 of the FY2024 NDAA** imposes its own DoD-level bans
keyed directly to 1260H listing:

- **Entity Prohibition — effective 30 June 2026:** DoD may not enter into, renew or extend a
  contract with a 1260H entity or an entity under its control.
- **Goods and Services Prohibition — effective 30 June 2027:** DoD may not procure, **directly
  or indirectly**, goods or services that include goods or services sourced from a 1260H entity
  or an entity it controls — **including when sourced indirectly through third parties.**

The **30 June 2027** indirect prohibition is far more commercially dangerous than BIOSECURE's
late-2028 date, because it reaches **prime contractors' supply chains**: a pharma company
selling to DoD would have to certify it is not using WuXi anywhere in that chain. Absent the
injunction, this — not BIOSECURE — would have been the first hard forcing event, **roughly 11
months away.**

**The 7 Aug 2026 injunction blocks "enforcing, implementing, or otherwise giving effect to"
the designation, which on its face suspends the § 805 consequences too. If the injunction is
stayed, reversed, or dissolved, the § 805 clock resumes against a 30 June 2027 date.**

## 4. China-side countermeasures and domestic rules

**Retaliation posture — escalating but not yet aimed at WuXi's client-service ability:**

- **22 Jun 2026** — MOFCOM and MOF announced restrictive measures against **56 U.S. entities**,
  widely read as a response to the 8 June 1260H expansion.
- **Early Aug 2026** — MOFCOM announced further countermeasures: tightened dual-use export
  controls on UAVs and key components; six U.S. entities added to the countermeasure list over
  Xinjiang-related sanctions; a U.S. compliance-testing company listed; a national-security
  investigation into imported printing/copying equipment. MOFCOM warned of "further
  countermeasures."
- **7–8 Aug 2026** — Global Times amplified the injunction as proof "arbitrary blacklists" are
  "not beyond challenge" — i.e. Beijing is treating this as a **legal/propaganda win, not a
  trigger for escalation** on this specific file.

**Domestic Chinese rules — direction of travel is LIBERALISING for cross-border life sciences:**

- **Order No. 818**, effective **1 May 2026** — Regulations on Clinical Research and Clinical
  Translation of Biomedical New Technologies. Dual-track framework (traditional NMPA pathway
  plus a parallel pathway for new biomedical technologies). Reshapes cross-border CGT licensing
  and investment.
- **8 May 2026** — National Health Commission released a **public consultation draft** of
  updated Implementation Rules for the Human Genetic Resources (HGR) regulations. The draft
  **narrows** "foreign party" to entities where foreign organisations hold **≥50% of equity or
  voting rights**, **removing the prior "actual control" test**. This is **relief**, not
  restriction — it lets many China biotechs under VIE structures escape strict HGR limits.
  **Consultation draft only — not final.**

**Assessment:** I found **no Chinese export control, data-security rule, or retaliatory measure
that presently impairs WuXi AppTec's ability to serve foreign clients.** The HGR direction is
mildly favourable. The asymmetric risk is that Beijing could weaponise HGR/data rules or add
biotech to its own export-control lists if US pressure escalates — but that is **hypothetical,
not observed**. This is a tail risk to monitor, not a current impact.

## 5. What WuXi AppTec has actually done — announcements vs. delivered capacity

**Delivered / contracted (real, not intention):**

- **WuXi ATU divestiture — DONE.** Definitive agreement to sell the **US and UK operations** of
  WuXi Advanced Therapies (WuXi Advanced Therapies Inc. and Oxford Genetics Ltd.) to **Altaris,
  LLC** (New York healthcare PE). ~**RMB 9.8bn** value; ~**2.4% of annual revenue**; completion
  targeted H1 2025. This is a **complete withdrawal from US/UK cell-and-gene-therapy
  operations** — explicitly framed by trade press as a BIOSECURE de-risking move. The company
  now reports "Continuing Operations" separately, which is why continuing-ops growth rates run
  ~9pp above total revenue growth.
- **Middletown, Delaware** — **1.74 million sq ft**, to become WuXi's **largest US facility**.
  Oral solid dosage manufacturing **begins operations Q4 2026**; sterile/injectable **Q4 2027**.
  This is under construction with a dated start, not a press release.

**Announced with dated milestones (credible but not yet operating):**

- **Couvet, Switzerland** — spray drying added **2026**; parenteral and LNP capability to follow.
- **Singapore** — API manufacturing for small molecules, oligonucleotides, peptides and
  conjugates; **Phase I operations expected 2027**.
- **Capex** — FY2026 capex raised **≥17%** to **RMB 6.5–7.5bn**, explicitly to fund drug
  substance and drug product capacity across multiple regions.

**Customer reassurance:** an "Open Letter to our Customers" and an "Update on Our Legal Action"
were published on the corporate site; the 8 June statement rejected the designation outright;
the company litigated within **three days** of listing.

**Verdict on §5:** WuXi has done **more than announce**. One divestiture is contractually
executed; one large US site has a Q4 2026 operating date. But the **centre of gravity of
production remains in China**, and the ex-China build-out (Singapore 2027, Switzerland phased,
Delaware sterile 2027) **completes on a timeline that trails the policy risk** rather than
leading it. Geographic de-risking is real but **partial and lagging**.

## 8. ANTI-ANCHORING TEST (L-003) — what would make this benign, and did I find it?

I set the disconfirming criteria before assessing. Five conditions would make this policy risk
**RESOLVED and benign**:

| # | Condition for "resolved" | Found? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | BIOSECURE lapsed or was never enacted | **NO — it is law, signed 18 Dec 2025.** Disconfirmed hard |
| 2 | WuXi permanently and finally removed from 1260H (merits judgment or DoD delisting) | **NO — only a preliminary injunction, appealable, merits undecided** |
| 3 | Statute structurally incapable of reaching commercial pharma revenue | **PARTIAL YES — genuinely important.** Prohibitions attach only to federal procurement/grant/loan nexus. Private commercial pharma is not barred from using WuXi. This is a real, durable structural limit |
| 4 | Customers demonstrably not leaving; financials unimpaired | **YES — strongly.** H1 2026 (3 Aug 2026): revenue **RMB 28.90bn, +38.9% YoY**; continuing ops **+48.0%**; adj. non-IFRS net profit **RMB 11.57bn, +83.2%**; adj. gross margin **+9.4pp to 53.9%**; backlog **RMB 66.43bn, +25.2% YoY** (30 Jun 2026); **FY26 guidance RAISED to RMB 58.5–60.5bn from RMB 51.3–53.0bn**. Stock at highest since 30 Sep 2021 |
| 5 | Policy pressure abating | **NO — the opposite.** Dec 2025 congressional letter targeting three WuXi entities; June 2026 listing of three biotech names; BINSA introduced 2 Jun 2026; StatNews (25 Jun 2026) reports lawmakers explicitly want to "do more" because "Biosecure didn't stop China's rise in biotech" |

**Score: 2 of 5 conditions met, and neither of the two decisive ones (1 and 2).**

**Honest statement of the tension, because it is the whole case:** conditions 3 and 4 are
powerful and I weight them seriously. The statute's federal-nexus limit is real, the
grandfathering is real, the late-2028 date is real, the injunction is real, and the H1 2026
numbers are the strongest possible evidence that customers are **not** leaving in aggregate.
An analyst anchored on "China policy risk = uninvestable" would have been badly wrong on this
stock over the last twelve months, and I say so plainly.

**But conditions 1, 2 and 5 are unmet, and they are the ones that determine whether this is a
services business or a policy asset.** The dispositive counter-evidence is Boasberg's own
finding — that customers and suppliers **had already cancelled contracts and moved business to
competitors** in the eight weeks after listing. Aggregate revenue can grow strongly while the
marginal *new* Western programme quietly routes to Lonza or Samsung. Backlog growth of +25.2%
is reassuring but is a lagging indicator of decisions taken before June 2026.

**Conclusion: the risk is LIVE, currently suspended by a reversible court order, and I did not
find the evidence that would make it benign.**

## 10. Assessment for the Investment Committee

**This is a policy asset with a services business attached, not the reverse.**

The correct framing is **not** "is BIOSECURE law?" (it is) nor "is WuXi named in it?" (it is
not). It is: **a company generating 60–68% of revenue from US customers is one administrative
listing decision away from a demand shock that a court has already found causes immediate,
documented commercial harm — and the only thing currently preventing that listing is a
preliminary injunction that the government has not said it will accept.**
