## 0. Bottom line up front

Five findings, in order of investment relevance.

1. **The redomiciliation does not remove BIOSECURE exposure, but BIOSECURE was never the
   binding constraint on this name.** The statute reaches ownership, control and activity, not
   the certificate of incorporation, so Basel changes nothing definitionally (§2.2). What
   actually limits BeOne's exposure is the **business model** — it sells finished branded
   medicines to payers and providers, not R&D services into other companies' federal-contract
   supply chains — and BIOSECURE's prohibitions attach only where there is a **federal nexus**,
   which Medicare and Medicaid agreements are not (§2.5). BeOne is **not on the 1260H list, was
   never named in the 2024 draft bill, and was not named in the 18 Dec 2025 congressional
   letter** that targeted the three WuXi entities (§3).

2. **The index-flow fear in the brief was the reverse of what happened.** Thirteen months after
   redomiciling, BeOne was **added to the benchmark Hang Seng Index**, effective 8 June 2026,
   and sits at the **10% cap in three Hang Seng healthcare indices**. Hang Seng still classifies
   it as **"Other HK-listed Mainland Co."**, and the Southbound-eligible SCHK series carries it
   (§5). I found **no** Stock Connect exclusion event. That is a positive flow record, not a
   negative one — with one primary-source verification still outstanding (§13).

3. **The genuinely near-dated, under-discussed risk is the Section 232 pharmaceutical tariff,
   which bites for non-Annex-III companies on 29 September 2026 — six weeks from today.** Rates
   are set by **country of origin of the goods**, not by corporate domicile, so the Swiss
   certificate does **not** buy the 15% Swiss rate for product or API originating in China
   (§9.2). BeOne's stated mitigation is that BRUKINSA drug product is made and packaged in the
   US by contract partners while **APIs are imported** — and the Section 232 order covers APIs
   expressly. I could not establish whether BeOne holds a BIS onshoring agreement (20% rate) or
   an HHS MFN agreement (0% to Jan 2029). **This is the highest-value open question in the file.**

4. **A second dated binary sits at 1 February 2027: IRA Medicare negotiation selection for
   IPAY 2029.** BRUKINSA was first approved **14 Nov 2019**; the small-molecule eligibility
   window used for IPAY 2027 required approval by 1 Feb 2018, i.e. seven years before selection.
   On the same arithmetic BRUKINSA **becomes eligible at the IPAY 2029 selection** (§9.1). With
   US BRUKINSA sales of **USD 893m in Q2 2026 alone**, top-50 Part D ranking is plausible. **I
   flag this as my inference from published rules, not as a CMS statement** — but it is a dated,
   checkable, and to my knowledge unpriced event roughly five and a half months out.

5. **BEQALZI (sonrotoclax) is an accelerated approval and therefore carries withdrawal risk.**
   FDA granted accelerated approval on **13 May 2026** for R/R mantle cell lymphoma on a
   surrogate endpoint, conditional on confirmatory trials. I did not obtain the confirmatory
   trial or its readout date (§13). This is a binary event class the committee should not carry
   unmonitored on a name where the pipeline story is doing valuation work.

**Net regulatory posture:** BeOne is in a **materially better national-security position than
2359.HK** and for reasons that are mostly structural rather than cosmetic. But the redomiciliation
narrative has absorbed attention that belongs on **trade policy and US drug pricing**, where the
exposure is larger, nearer and denominated in dollars rather than headlines.

### 1.2 The legal mechanism — the precise point

The move was a **continuation**, not a merger, not a scheme of arrangement, and not a new
holding company inserted above the group: **Section 206 of the Cayman Islands Companies Act (as
amended)** (de-registration by way of continuation) combined with **Article 161 of the Swiss
Federal Act on Private International Law (PILA)** (continuation of a foreign company into
Switzerland without liquidation).

Sources: [SEC Exhibit 5.1 legal opinions filed with the POSASR / S-8 POS suite, May 2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1651308/000110465925053039/tm2515777d11_ex5-1.htm);
[Homburger](https://homburger.ch/en/news/homburger-advised-beone-medicines-ltd-on-its-redomiciliation-to-switzerland).
Swiss counsel: **Homburger** (David Oser, Bianca Nedwed, Kaiyan Yeo).

**Why the mechanism matters, stated carefully:**

- A continuation **preserves legal identity**. The same legal person continues with the same
  assets, contracts and liabilities; no assets transfer to a new entity. Every pre-existing
  contract, licence, marketing authorisation and regulatory obligation travels with the company
  unchanged. **Nothing was novated away, and nothing was left behind.** That is the structural
  fact underpinning §2: there is no "old China entity" that was severed.
- What **does** change is **the law of incorporation** — Cayman company law to the Swiss Code of
  Obligations. Consequences ordinarily associated with Swiss incorporation include annual
  say-on-pay votes, annual director re-election, capital-band rules, and Swiss withholding tax
  on dividends. **I have not read BeOne's articles of association**; treat that list as the
  standard Swiss framework, not confirmed company-specific fact (§13).
- **Tax residence:** the ordinary consequence of Swiss incorporation plus a Basel head office is
  Swiss tax residence. **I obtained no filing stating BeOne's effective tax rate, a tax-residence
  certificate, or a Basel-Stadt ruling.** Do not assume a tax saving; equally do not assume the
  reverse (§13).
- **Board composition:** **NOT OBTAINED.** I did not verify any change to board membership or
  nationality mix arising from the move (§13). Do not infer one.

## 2. Does Swiss domicile remove BIOSECURE / Section 1260H exposure?

**Short answer: no, not by itself. The definitional architecture is the reason — and the reason
BeOne is nonetheless low-exposure is different from the one the redomiciliation narrative offers.**

### 2.1 The statutory test, restated from our own 2026-08-07 work

Per `reports/news/2026-08-07-2359hk-regulatory.md` §1.2, the **enacted** BIOSECURE Act (signed
**18 Dec 2025** in the FY2026 NDAA) **names no companies**. A "biotechnology company of concern"
(BCC) arises through one of two prongs:

- **Prong (A)** — an entity on the **DoD/DoW Section 1260H list** of "Chinese military companies
  operating in the United States" **that is also involved in** the manufacture, distribution,
  provision or procurement of biotechnology equipment or services, subject to an **OMB
  determination**; **or**
- **Prong (B)** — an entity named through a separate **criteria-based OMB process**.

### 2.2 Prong (A) — does a Swiss domicile put a company outside the 1260H list?

**No, on the face of the authority.** Section 1260H (FY2021 NDAA) directs DoD to identify
**"Chinese military companies operating directly or indirectly in the United States."** The
operative concepts in the listings DoD has actually made are **ownership, control and
affiliation**: in the June 2026 tranche the stated basis for the WuXi AppTec listing was
*indirect ownership by SASAC and indirect affiliation with SASTIND and the PLA*
(2026-08-07 report §1.3; [WilmerHale, 11 Jun 2026](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260611-pentagon-adds-65-new-entities-to-the-1260h-list-of-chinese-military-companies)).

Nothing in that architecture keys off place of incorporation. **A Swiss certificate would not
preclude a listing** if DoD concluded the entity was Chinese-controlled or military-affiliated.
Note that the June 2026 list included **48 subsidiaries of previously listed parents** — DoD is
plainly comfortable reaching through corporate structures.

**But the redomiciliation is not therefore worthless, and the distinction should be precise.**
Because the test is ownership/control/affiliation rather than incorporation, the move does not
*defeat* the test — however it **plausibly weakens the factual case for a listing at the margin**,
since a Swiss-incorporated, Swiss-headquartered company with a majority non-China revenue base,
a US flagship plant and no SASAC/SASTIND ownership thread is a harder listing to defend on an
administrative record. Boasberg's 7 Aug 2026 opinion is direct evidence that such a record must
now survive arbitrary-and-capricious review (§2.4).

**Critically: I found no evidence of any state-ownership link of the kind DoD cited for WuXi
AppTec.** BeOne's disclosed historical shareholder base has been dominated by Western
institutions, and previously Amgen. **I did not verify the current shareholder register** (§13),
but the SASAC/SASTIND thread that drove the WuXi listing is not a feature I can find here.

### 2.3 Prong (B) — the OMB criteria-based route is the real residual exposure

Prong (B) is where the redomiciliation offers the **least** protection, because the process is
criteria-based rather than list-derivative and OMB has **published nothing yet**. Per our
2026-08-07 work §2.1, the **initial OMB BCC list is due by 18 Dec 2026** — four months away.
Until it exists, nobody can say which criteria OMB will apply, or whether a Swiss-domiciled,
China-operating oncology company falls inside them.

Note the structural point our WuXi file made: an **OMB prong-(B) designation is independent of
the 1260H route** and would not be covered by an injunction addressed to a 1260H listing. For
BeOne that is theoretical today — it holds no listing and no injunction — but the **18 Dec 2026
date is the single largest unresolved regulatory unknown on this name**, and it is dateable.

### 2.4 Read-across from the Boasberg injunction — assessed as instructed

Our 2026-08-07 report records: Chief Judge James E. Boasberg (D.D.C.), **7 Aug 2026**, Case
**1:26-cv-02069**, preliminarily enjoined DoD from enforcing WuXi AppTec's 1260H designation,
finding a likelihood it was **arbitrary and capricious** and that defence officials "repeatedly
misread the evidence"; DoD's stay request was refused.

**Read-across to BeOne — three findings, in confidence order:**

1. **Positive and material: administrative listings are now judicially reviewable in practice,
   not merely in theory.** A China-origin firm obtained interim relief against DoD in a US
   federal court. For any name whose bear case rests on "one day they will be listed," the
   *expected cost* of a listing falls, because it can be contested and the government must
   produce a defensible record. That is a **genuine reduction in tail severity** for BeOne.
2. **Also positive and specific: the evidentiary bar Boasberg applied favours a company with a
   weak state-ownership thread.** The WuXi record turned on SASAC/SASTIND ownership tracing the
   court found the government misread. A company with **no** such thread is a still weaker
   listing candidate after that opinion.
3. **Negative and equally important: the same opinion establishes that the label alone causes
   immediate, documented commercial harm.** Boasberg's irreparable-harm finding rested on
   customers and suppliers having **already** cancelled contracts and moved business within
   about eight weeks of listing. **Winning eventually is not the same as not being listed.**
   The qualifier for BeOne is that its counterparties are US payers, prescribers and hospital
   systems, not federal procurement officers, so the transmission channel would be slower and
   narrower than WuXi's (§2.5) — but oncology formulary committees and health systems are not
   immune to headline risk either.

**Standing qualifier, unchanged:** a preliminary injunction is interim relief, appealable,
resting on a likelihood of success. As of our last check (2026-08-10) **no notice of appeal was
confirmed filed**. I have not re-verified the docket for 11–14 Aug 2026 (§13).

### 2.5 The mechanism question nobody asks — would BIOSECURE even bite this business model?

This is where BeOne differs fundamentally from WuXi AppTec, and the committee should hold the
distinction firmly.

BIOSECURE's prohibitions attach **only where there is a federal nexus**: executive agencies may
not procure biotechnology equipment or services from a BCC; may not contract with entities that
*use* a BCC's equipment or services in performing a federal contract (including via
subcontractors); and may not make loans or grants for the purpose of such procurement
(2026-08-07 report §2.2).

- **WuXi AppTec sells services into other companies' supply chains.** That is precisely the
  transmission surface: every customer with a federal touchpoint must scrub WuXi out of the chain.
- **BeOne sells finished branded medicines to patients through payers and providers.** It is not
  a service input to third parties' federal contracts. **Medicare and Medicaid agreements are not
  FAR contracts and sit outside BIOSECURE scope** (2026-08-07 §2.2) — and that is the dominant
  US revenue channel for an oncology drug.

**So even in the adverse scenario where BeOne were designated a BCC, the direct statutory
impairment of US drug sales looks structurally limited.** The realistic damage channels would be
(i) **Veterans Affairs / DoD / federal-facility formulary access** — real but a modest share of
US oncology volume that **I have not sized** (§13); (ii) **federal research grants and
collaborations**; and (iii) **reputational and precautionary partner behaviour**, which Boasberg
documented as the fastest-acting channel.

**Conclusion of §2:** Swiss domicile does **not** legally immunise BeOne under either prong. What
actually limits its exposure is **the business model, the absence of an identifiable
state-ownership thread, and the statute's federal-nexus limit** — with the redomiciliation and
the US manufacturing build acting as supporting facts that make a designation harder to justify
on the record. **That is a materially better position than 2359.HK's, and it is better for
reasons that mostly are not the redomiciliation.** Anyone underwriting the move as the risk
mitigant has the right conclusion for the wrong reason, which is a fragile place to be if the
facts change.

| **Application window** | Onshoring plan applications **opened 13 May 2026**; initial applications due **12 June 2026** |

| **Effective — ALL OTHER COMPANIES** | **29 SEPTEMBER 2026** |
| **Generics/biosimilars** | Exempt for now; Commerce to advise on revisiting within one year of the 2 Apr 2026 EO (~2 Apr 2027) |

**The critical analytical point, and it is the one the redomiciliation narrative gets wrong:**

> **Rates are set by COUNTRY OF ORIGIN OF THE GOODS, not by corporate domicile.** Being a Swiss
> AG does **not** confer the 15% Swiss rate on product or API that originates in China. The
> favourable Swiss rate attaches only to goods actually **of Swiss origin**. BeOne has **no
> disclosed Swiss manufacturing**; its manufacturing is Hopewell (US), Guangzhou (China) and
> Suzhou (China) (§1.4).

**BeOne's stated mitigation, and its limit:**

Trade press reports BeOne has mitigated tariff impact by "globalizing and regionalizing" its
supply chain, pointing to **US production for BRUKINSA** and the Hopewell biologics facility for
potential commercial supply of TEVIMBRA and future medicines. **But the same reporting states
that BeOne currently partners with contractors for BRUKINSA drug production and packaging in the
US and "does need to import active pharmaceutical ingredients."**
([FiercePharma — "BeOne, now a Swiss company, widens Brukinsa's BTK lead"](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/beone-now-swiss-company-widens-brukinsa-btk-lead-over-astrazeneca-calquence)).

**The Section 232 order covers APIs expressly.** So the exposure is not zero simply because the
tablet is pressed in New Jersey — the **imported API is itself dutiable at the origin rate**.
That is a much smaller tariff base than finished-goods value, which is genuine and material
mitigation, but it is **mitigation, not immunity**.

And the **Hopewell small-molecule expansion is stated as fully operational by 2029** (§1.4) —
**three years after the 29 September 2026 effective date**. The de-risking completes on a
timeline that trails the policy, the same structural pattern this desk identified at WuXi AppTec.

**What I could NOT establish, and it is the most valuable single unknown in this report:**

1. Whether BeOne is an **Annex III** company (31 Jul 2026 date) or falls in the "all others"
   cohort (**29 Sep 2026**). Annex III is described as the 17 large pharmaceutical companies, so
   the **29 Sep 2026** date is the more likely one — **but I did not verify Annex III membership**.
2. Whether BeOne applied for or holds a **BIS onshoring agreement** (→ 20%). The USD 300m
   Hopewell announcement is exactly the kind of commitment such an application would rest on, and
   the timing — application window opened 13 May 2026, initial applications due 12 June 2026, the
   expansion announced with Q2 results on 5 Aug 2026 — is **suggestive but not evidence**.
3. Whether BeOne has signed an **HHS MFN pricing agreement** (→ 0% to Jan 2029).
4. The **country of origin of BRUKINSA API supplied to the US**, and how far the "second source
   of API outside China" programme has progressed.

**Any of items 2–4 would materially change the magnitude.** These are §12 Tier-1 tripwires and
they should be resolved before the committee sizes anything.
