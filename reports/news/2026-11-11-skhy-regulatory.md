## 0. Bottom line up front

Six findings, in order of investment relevance.

1. **The Wuxi DRAM fab is running on a one-year US licence that expires on 31 December 2026, seven weeks from today, and no 2027 licence has been reported as issued for any SK hynix China site.** The VEU authorisation was revoked by a BIS final rule of 29 Aug 2025, effective 31 Dec 2025; Commerce granted a quantity-limited, maintenance-only annual licence for 2026 on 30 Dec 2025 — one day before lapse. Reuters-sourced reporting of 3–4 Nov 2026 says the **2027 licence for Dalian (NAND) is under negotiation, not granted**, and that Commerce has withheld Dalian expansion licences while SK hynix reportedly links a new US NAND fab to that approval. **Nothing reported says Wuxi's maintenance licence is in jeopardy; everything reported says the clock is real** (§1).

2. **Wuxi is roughly one-third of SK hynix DRAM wafer output, frozen at the 1a node by construction.** It cannot go to 1b/1c (EUV, Korea-only), so it is a commodity-DRAM fab — which in a shortage with DRAM contract prices up 25–29% q/q is a cash machine, not a stranded asset. The stranding risk is back-ended to the next down-cycle; the acute risk is licence non-renewal degrading output within quarters. **No upgrade-or-strand cost estimate exists in the public domain that I could find** (§1.3).

3. **There is no Section 232 tariff on Korean memory today.** The only semiconductor 232 action in force is **Proclamation 11002 (14 Jan 2026)** — 25% on a narrow class of advanced AI logic chips with a US-buildout exemption; Korean press reports memory as outside it. **"Phase 2"** (broad semiconductors, SME, derivatives, with an offset programme) is **recommended by Commerce, not proclaimed**. Secretary Lutnick's 2–3 Sep 2026 "100% on memory not made in America, or build here" is a **statement of intent, not a rule**. Korea holds a Nov 2025 "no less favorable" clause that is **narrower than the press framing** and whose benchmark — Taiwan's 15 Jan 2026 deal with 2.5x/1.5x duty-free quotas tied to US capacity — SK hynix cannot match with a packaging plant alone (§2).

4. **The HBM-to-China controls (Dec 2024, ECCN 3A090.c) are in force and have not loosened.** They matter less to SK hynix revenue than the headline suggests: China was ~20% of 2025 revenue by my arithmetic (down from ~30%), and SK hynix's HBM goes overwhelmingly to Nvidia/US designs. The live risk is **enforcement blowback**: The Information (Nov 2026) reports Huawei telling customers its Ascend 960DT will run on **smuggled Samsung/SK hynix HBM3E** at ~USD 4,000 a stack. SK hynix denies supplying Huawei since 2020. If BIS responds by tightening packaging-site conditions or Korea tightens its own export enforcement, it is a compliance cost; if BIS finds a diversion chain touching an SK hynix distributor, it is a headline event (§3).

5. **For the Owner's actual tax position: Korean dividend withholding is 22% at source on the ADS unless treaty certification is in place through the depositary (Citibank/DTCC CA Web, GlobeTax); the US–Korea treaty portfolio rate is 15%.** Uncertified holders get 22% and must long-form reclaim. Korean capital-gains tax does not reach a sub-25% non-resident holder of listed shares, and a Nasdaq sale of the ADS is outside the Korean securities transaction tax in any case. **The one action item is confirming treaty certification with the Owner's broker before the next record date** (§4.3).

6. **The most under-discussed regulatory fact on this instrument is that the ADS premium over KOSPI (reported 16–51% since listing; ~35% on 28 Jul) is a product of a Korea Securities Depository cap on ADR issuance (2.5% of outstanding shares), exhausted by 23 Jul 2026, with one-way conversion only until 29 Jul.** The premium exists because a regulator limits arbitrage. **If KSD/FSC lift or widen the cap, the premium compresses and the ADS underperforms the ordinary share with no change in the business** (§4.4). I did not obtain the current premium — the committee should ask the technical desk.

**Listing housekeeping:** Foreign private issuer, KPMG Samjong auditor (PCAOB-inspected jurisdiction, no HFCAA exposure), 90-day lock-up expired 8 Oct 2026, a US DRAM antitrust class action disclosed in the F-1/A (§5). SEC comment letters not obtained (`sec.gov` blocked).

### 1.2 Current licence regime for shipping US-origin tools to Wuxi (as of 12 Nov 2026)

**What is established (secondary, consistent across sources):**

- Wuxi operates under an **annual, quantity-limited BIS licence for calendar 2026**, granted 30 Dec 2025. The licence is for **operation and maintenance** — replacement parts, like-for-like tools — not for capacity expansion or node upgrades.
- **No 2027 licence has been reported as issued for any SK hynix China site as of early November 2026.** Reuters-sourced reporting (republished by [The Gazette, 3 Nov 2026](https://gazette.com/2026/11/03/sk-hynixs-chip-plans-hit-snag-over-u-s-restrictions-on-china/) and [Korea Times, 4 Nov 2026](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20261104/sk-hynixs-new-york-chip-plans-hit-snag-over-us-restrictions-on-china-sources-say)) says the two sides are "negotiating a separate license for 2027 for equipment to keep up the Dalian facility" and that "it remains unclear whether SK Hynix can secure its 2027 permits by year's end." **The reporting is Dalian-specific; a targeted search for a Wuxi 2027 renewal returned nothing either way.** Given both sites lost VEU in the same rule and were licensed together in Dec 2025, the working assumption is that **Wuxi's 2027 licence is on the same clock — a 31 Dec 2026 deadline** — but that is my inference, not reported fact.

**Working figure: roughly one-third of DRAM wafer output, falling as Yongin/Cheongju ramp.** The decline in the quoted share over time is itself informative — it is consistent with SK hynix diluting Wuxi by adding Korean capacity rather than removing Wuxi capacity. TrendForce expects SK hynix's China share of DRAM output to settle near Samsung's **30–35%**, and puts China at **40–45% of SK hynix NAND output** in 2025 — the Dalian exposure ([TrendForce, 30 Dec 2025](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2025/12/30/news-samsung-sk-hynix-escape-worst-case-as-china-veu-ends-annual-review-risks-loom); [TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/?p=50867)).

**Node status — the operational crux:**

- **Wuxi has reportedly completed conversion to the 1a (fourth-gen 10nm-class) node** despite the controls ([DigiTimes, 16 Jan 2026](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260116VL202/sk-hynix-dram-plant-production-euv.html)). 1a is the last SK hynix DRAM node that does not require EUV lithography on the critical layers in volume.
- **No major process transition is expected at Wuxi in 2026** (no 1b); **1b and 1c are reserved for Icheon and Cheongju** (Hankyung via [TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2025/12/30/news-samsung-sk-hynix-escape-worst-case-as-china-veu-ends-annual-review-risks-loom)).
- Analyst view: Wuxi "will face difficulty in migrating beyond 1a nm because no EUV tools can ship to China" ([Nomad Semi](https://www.nomadsemi.com/p/memory-shortage-and-asml)). EUV export to China is restricted by Dutch and US rules independent of VEU; VEU revocation closes the non-EUV upgrade route too.
- **Capex at Wuxi is still rising:** KRW 581bn in 2025 at the Wuxi DRAM plant, +102% from KRW 287.3bn in 2024 ([Seoul Economic Daily, 25 Mar 2026](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/03/25/samsung-sk-hynix-pour-15-trillion-won-into-china-plants-to-20260325); [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/samsung-sk-hynix-pour-fresh-investment-into-aging-china-factories-despite-us-curbs/12832376)). That is maintenance-and-1a-conversion scale, not new-fab scale; it tells you the company is sweating the asset, not abandoning it.

**Upgrade-vs-strand cost: NOT OBTAINED.** I found **no analyst or company estimate** of the write-down or replacement cost if Wuxi is frozen at 1a. What can be said structurally, without inventing a number:

1. Wuxi is now a **legacy/mainstream DRAM fab by construction**: 1a-node DDR4/DDR5/LPDDR5 and similar, not HBM base dies (HBM3E/HBM4 base dies are 1b/1c, Korea-only) and not the leading-edge server DDR5 bins. It is, in effect, SK hynix's **commodity-DRAM profit centre in a shortage** — `reports/industry/2026-08-21-msft-industry.md` §4.1 records DRAM pricing tracking +275–300% 2025→2027, and Q3 2026 results show DRAM prices up 25–29% q/q with shortage guided through 2030 ([Guru3D](https://www.guru3d.com/story/sk-hynix-posts-record-quarter-and-expects-memory-shortage-through-2030/)). In this cycle, a frozen-at-1a fab is a cash machine, not a stranded asset.
2. The stranding risk is **back-ended**: it becomes material when 1a output can no longer be sold at acceptable margins, i.e. when the cycle turns or customers require 1b+ parts. SK hynix's answer is the **Yongin cluster** (reported +360k wafers/month DRAM, [TechTimes, Jun 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317859/20260606/sk-hynix-dram-capacity-roadmap-revealed-yongin-alone-adds-360k-wafers-monthly.htm)) — new Korean capacity that progressively makes Wuxi a smaller share of the whole.
3. The **acute** risk is not stranding but **non-renewal of the annual maintenance licence**, which would degrade Wuxi output within quarters as tools fail and cannot be serviced with US-origin parts. That is the 31 Dec 2026 tripwire (§7).

### 1.4 Assessment

**Status: in force (final rule, Aug 2025, effective 31 Dec 2025); mitigated by an annual licence that expires 31 Dec 2026; 2027 renewal not reported as granted.** Probability-weighted view (my estimate, not a sourced figure): maintenance-licence renewal for Wuxi is the base case — Commerce renewed in Dec 2025 one day before lapse, Kessler's stated red line is *expansion/upgrade*, not *operation*, and the Reuters sourcing describes the 2027 maintenance licence as under negotiation rather than refused. The adverse case is a late, reduced or conditional renewal used as leverage in the Dalian/New York negotiation. The exposure is **operational and dated**, exactly as the brief framed it.

### 2.2 Rate on Korean-origin memory today; investment-linked exemptions; the Indiana plant

- **Current 232 rate on Korean-origin DRAM/NAND/HBM imported as chips: none identified.** Korea Herald reports "Korean memory chips currently appear to be excluded" from Proclamation 11002 ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10657692)). The proclamation's covered-article definition is performance-threshold-based (logic/AI accelerators); **I have not read the annex and cannot rule out HBM-adjacent coverage from the text itself** (§8).
- **Korean-origin goods otherwise face the 15% reciprocal rate** under the Nov 2025 Korea–US deal (reduced from 25%) — but covered semiconductors are carved out of reciprocal tariffs by the 232 architecture, so for memory the operative answer is "no 232 duty, and the reciprocal carve-out question is moot until Phase 2 exists" ([Korea Times, 14 Nov 2025](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/business/companies/20251114/despite-easing-of-uncertainty-fact-sheets-still-have-points-of-contention); [KEIA](https://keia.org/the-peninsula/what-the-updated-u-s-korea-trade-terms-signal-for-investors-and-policymakers/)).
- **The benchmark for an investment-linked exemption is the US–Taiwan deal of 15 Jan 2026:** firms building US chip capacity may import **up to 2.5x planned capacity duty-free during construction** and **1.5x new US capacity duty-free after completion**, with a lower preferential 232 rate above those quotas; Taiwan's general tariff capped at 15%; ~USD 250bn investment plus credit guarantees. One outlet (Supply Chain Dive) reports 1.5x during construction — I use the majority reading. Taiwan's own negotiator says the underlying 232 chip rate "remains undecided" ([Baker McKenzie, 20 Jan 2026](https://globalimportblog.bakermckenzie.com/2026/01/20/us-and-taiwan-finalize-trade-agreement-with-focus-on-boosting-semiconductor-collaboration/); [TrendForce, 16 Jan 2026](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/01/16/news-taiwan-u-s-trade-deal-cuts-tariffs-from-20-to-15-secures-250b-investment-with-tsmc-at-center/); [C.H. Robinson](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-us/resources/insights-and-advisories/client-advisories/2026q1/01-27-2026-client-advisory-us-taiwan-trade-pact-lowers-tariffs-and-expands-semiconductor-incentives/); [Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/front/archives/2026/01/21/2003850944)).
- **Korea's claim on that benchmark is the "no less favorable" clause** in the 14 Nov 2025 joint fact sheet. The quoted text: semiconductor terms "no less favorable than terms that may be offered in a future agreement covering a volume of semiconductor trade at least as large as Korea's, **as determined by the United States**." **Taiwan is not named**; the comparator is volume-based and US-determined. Lutnick publicly disputed that chip tariffs were part of the deal at all ([Korea Times, 18 Jan 2026](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/business/companies/20260118/korea-pins-hopes-on-us-pledge-of-no-less-favorable-chip-tariffs); [Korea Times op-ed, 21 Jan 2026](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/20260121/illusion-of-no-less-favorable-status); [Korea Times, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/opinion/20260915/what-us-tariff-push-means-for-koreas-semiconductor-industry)). **Assessment: this is a comparative promise with no quota, no rate and a US-controlled trigger. It is worth something politically and little legally.**
- **The Indiana plant does not buy a Taiwan-style quota on its own.** SK hynix broke ground **27 Aug 2026** on a **>USD 4bn** (variously USD 3.87bn) **advanced packaging / HBM** facility at Purdue Research Park, West Lafayette, with up to **USD 458m** CHIPS Act support, ~1,000 jobs, mass production **H2 2028** (one source 2029) ([Purdue, Q3 2026](https://www.purdue.edu/newsroom/2026/Q3/sk-hynix-breaks-ground-on-4-billion-advanced-packaging-production-facility-in-purdue-research-park); [HotHardware](https://hothardware.com/news/sk-hynix-begins-4b-us-plant-feed-insatiable-ai-chip-demand); [TrendForce, 29 Dec 2025](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2025/12/29/news-sk-hynix-reportedly-plans-first-u-s-2-5d-packaging-line-eyes-turnkey-hbm-to-challenge-tsmc/)). Korean analysis notes Samsung and SK hynix "have so far stopped short of establishing dedicated advanced memory **wafer** production" in the US, and estimates matching TSMC's proportional commitment would require roughly **6.5x** what they have committed ([Korea Times, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/opinion/20260915/what-us-tariff-push-means-for-koreas-semiconductor-industry); [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/washington-threatens-samsung-and-sk-hynix-with-100-memory-chip-tariffs/)). A packaging plant is the kind of commitment Lutnick's "build here" formulation can be read either way on — **the reported Solidigm NY NAND fab (§1.2) is the wafer-fab commitment that would change that reading, and it is currently hostage to the Dalian licence.** The two fronts are the same negotiation.
- **Exemption mechanics, if Phase 2 arrives:** reporting indicates zero-duty treatment requires **two separate agreements with two federal agencies** (the pharma-232 template — a BIS onshoring agreement plus a second agency deal), not merely a pledge ([TechTimes, 3 Sep 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/326408/20260903/chip-tariff-phase-2-has-conditions-lutnick-reveals-pharma-deal-structure-behind-it.htm)). That is the structure my 6160.HK file §9.2 documented for pharmaceuticals. **Unverified for semiconductors until a proclamation exists.**

### 2.3 Country-of-origin: Wuxi-origin vs Icheon-origin DRAM; the ADS listing

- **The principle from the 6160.HK file applies directly: tariffs attach to the country of origin of the goods, not the domicile of the company or the venue of its listing.** The Nasdaq listing changes nothing about tariff exposure. Icheon/Cheongju output is Korean-origin; **Wuxi output is China-origin** (wafer fabrication confers origin under the substantial-transformation test as generally applied to semiconductors — general principle, not a ruling I have read for SK hynix specifically).
- **China-origin DRAM imported into the US as a chip already carries a materially heavier stack than Korean-origin DRAM** — chiefly the **Section 301 tariff on Chinese semiconductors (HTS 8541/8542), raised to 50% effective 1 Jan 2025** under the 2024 four-year review. **This is from my own knowledge of the 301 action, not from a source fetched in this session; the committee should treat the 50% figure as to-be-verified** (§8). Any Phase 2 232 rate would stack on or supersede that per its own terms.
- **Why this bites less than it reads:** memory rarely enters the US as a bare chip. It arrives inside **servers, PCs and phones assembled in Mexico, Taiwan, Vietnam or China**, where the **finished good's** origin and tariff treatment govern, and inside **HBM stacks packaged onto US-designed accelerators in Taiwan**. SK hynix's Wuxi output is predominantly commodity DRAM sold into Asian assembly chains. The exposure channel is therefore **derivative-product tariffs on servers/modules**, not the chip line — which is exactly what the unverified Phase 2 reporting says the administration is targeting (§2.1).
- **Where it does bite:** any SK hynix DRAM shipped from Wuxi directly to US module makers or to US-based server assembly faces the China-origin stack today. **I did not obtain the share of Wuxi output that enters the US directly** (§8). Structurally SK hynix can route Korean-origin die to US-destined demand and Wuxi die to Asia — a logistics cost, not a revenue loss, while Korean capacity suffices.

### 2.4 Assessment

**Status: Phase 1 in force but does not reach memory; Phase 2 threatened at up to 100% on memory, not proclaimed; Korea holds a weak comparative clause; SK hynix holds a packaging-plant commitment that may or may not qualify under rules that do not yet exist.** My probability view (estimate, not sourced): a Phase 2 proclamation within 12 months is more likely than not given the Taiwan template, the pharma precedent and Lutnick's repeated statements; a Korea-specific accommodation at or near Taiwan terms is plausible but contingent on a wafer-fab-scale US commitment — i.e. on the same Dalian/New York negotiation as Front 1. The investment-relevant point is that **the tariff and the export-licence fronts have converged into a single bargaining table**, and the price of a Phase 2 exemption may be paid in Front 1 currency (accepting Dalian/Wuxi constraints) or in capex (a US NAND or DRAM fab) rather than in duty.

### 3.2 Addressable-market effect; Nvidia China chips and SK hynix HBM

- **China as a share of SK hynix revenue is falling and is now ~20%:** 2025 China revenue **KRW 19.14tn** vs US **KRW 66.89tn (68.9%)**; total 2025 revenue ~KRW 97.15tn → China ≈ **19.7% by my arithmetic**; Q3 2025 reported at 24%, down from ~30% historically ([Bizhankook](https://en.bizhankook.com/articles/31779.html); [Korea Times, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/tech-science/20260128/sk-hynix-posts-record-2025-profit-on-ai-memory-boom); [TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/?p=44686)). **The tightening risk is enforcement, and it is current.** Per The Information (early Nov 2026), Huawei told customers its **Ascend 960DT (Q1 2027)** will rely heavily on **Samsung and SK hynix HBM**, asked buyers to **source the memory themselves**, and smuggling networks are supplying **HBM3E at ~USD 4,000/stack** by having packaging houses in Taiwan, Korea and Southeast Asia bond Korean HBM to deliberately weak logic dies (legal under the co-packaging exception), then de-bonding it in China ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/huawei-hbm-smuggling-samsung-sk-hynix-ascend-960dt); [TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/news/?p=66148); [Herald Corp](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10924542); [SammyFans, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.sammyfans.com/2026/11/03/huawei-sourcing-smuggled-samsung-sk-hynix-hbm/amp/)). **SK hynix states it complies with export rules and has not supplied Huawei since 2020.** The reporting does **not** allege direct supply. 1. **BIS tightening of the co-packaging exception / License Exception HBM** at or before the 31 Dec 2026 TGL expiry — the loophole described is precisely the packaging-site condition. Compliance cost to SK hynix: know-your-customer on packaging houses and distributors; possible loss of marginal legitimate HBM2E-class sales.
2. **Korean government enforcement** (MOTIE strategic-items controls) on Korean packaging/distribution intermediaries.
3. **Tail risk:** a BIS charging letter or Entity List action naming a distributor in SK hynix's chain. No sign of it; it would be a headline event, not a revenue event.

### 3.3 Assessment

**Status: in force since Dec 2024; no loosening of HBM controls; accelerator-side loosening (H200) has not produced Chinese demand; enforcement tightening is the live direction.** Direct addressable-market effect on SK hynix is **small and already in the base** — China is ~20% of revenue and almost none of it is HBM. The brief's question "is there any sign of it loosening or tightening?" answers: **tightening, via enforcement, triggered by the Huawei 960DT smuggling reporting.** The dated item is the **31 Dec 2026 TGL expiry**.

### 5.2 Foreign private issuer status and reporting obligations

- The listing was executed on **Form F-1** with an **F-6** for the depositary programme — the foreign-private-issuer route ([Strasmore](https://www.strasmore.com/blog/sk-hynix-nasdaq-debut)). Post-listing current reports are on **Form 6-K** (an interim-review 6-K for the period to 30 Jun 2026 is on EDGAR — [SEC 6-K (not fetched)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002120882/000119312526354777/d147827d6k.htm)).
- **What FPI status means, in general terms (not a legal opinion):** annual report on **Form 20-F** (due within four months of fiscal year-end, so **by 30 Apr 2027** for FY2026); no 10-Q/8-K; no Regulation FD, Section 16 insider reporting or US proxy rules; home-country governance permitted in place of most Nasdaq governance rules with disclosure of differences; financial statements may be under **IFRS as issued by the IASB** without US GAAP reconciliation (SK hynix reports under K-IFRS; whether the 20-F will be IFRS-IASB or K-IFRS reconciled is **not obtained**).
- **What it does not mean:** Sarbanes-Oxley §302/§404 certification and PCAOB-registered auditor requirements apply to FPIs. Antifraud liability (Rule 10b-5) applies in full.
- **FPI status is tested annually (last business day of Q2).** SK hynix comfortably satisfies the test (majority of shares held outside the US; management and assets in Korea). No risk of losing FPI status is identified.
