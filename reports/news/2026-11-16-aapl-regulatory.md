## 1. Front 1 — US v. Google search remedies (default-search payment to Apple)

**(A) What the ruling says (as reported; I did not read the final judgment).** Judge Mehta (D.D.C.) found Google liable for monopolising general search (Aug 2024) and entered remedies (Sep 2025 opinion; final judgment thereafter). 2026 coverage of the appeal reports these terms:
- The judgment **does not categorically prohibit Google from paying Apple** to be the default.
- It **prohibits exclusive** search-distribution contracts.
- Google **may still compensate Apple for placement**.
- Certain default-placement agreements with Apple and browser developers **must be limited to one-year terms**.
- Data-sharing remedies were ordered.

([MacRumors, 22 May 2026](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/05/22/google-search-monopoly-appeal/); [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/us-v-google-search-antitrust-trial-august-2026-updates/)). The "one-year" term comes from secondary coverage.

**Status: payments to Apple continue under a final judgment that permits non-exclusive paid defaults. Both sides have appealed that judgment.** Alphabet's Q2 and Q3 2026 10-Qs exist ([Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm)), but I did not read their disclosure of the appeal.

**Dollars at risk.** The ~USD 20bn/yr figure is the 2022 payment disclosed at the liability trial (historical, per tasking; not re-sourced this session). Apple does not break out the payment; it sits in Services licensing revenue. **It is a payment for placement with no product cost attached, so a loss would hit operating income roughly dollar-for-dollar. That is my reasoning, not a sourced margin.** The judgment as entered **already shortened contract tenure (one-year terms) but left the payment itself intact.**

**(C) Outcome scenarios (my assessment, not reported fact).**
- **Affirm remedies (status quo):** the payment continues and is re-papered annually. The annual term gives Google a yearly option to reprice.
- **Government wins its cross-appeal on payments:** the case is remanded for a payment ban or restriction. This is the **tail for Apple**. Appellate review of remedy discretion is generally deferential, and Mehta's stated reasons for not banning payments rest on a factual record. I treat this as a minority outcome and assign no number.
- **Google wins on liability:** remedies fall and the payment continues unconstrained. This is favourable to Apple.
- **Next dated event: D.C. Circuit oral argument — date NOT OBTAINED.** Briefing appears to be at or past the amicus stage. A decision in 2027 is the realistic window (estimate).

## 2. Front 2 — US DOJ antitrust case against Apple (D.N.J., No. 2:24-cv-04055, filed 21 Mar 2024)

**(A) What the complaint alleges and what the court held.** The complaint alleges Sherman Act §2 monopolisation of smartphone / performance-smartphone markets through restrictions on super apps, cloud gaming, cross-platform messaging, smartwatches and digital wallets. **Judge Julien Neals denied Apple's motion to dismiss in full on 30 Jun 2025.** He held that monopoly power was plausibly alleged and that the ecosystem restrictions are actionable rather than protected refusals to deal ([Justia, Doc. 283](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-jersey/njdce/2:2024cv04055/544402/283/); [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/68362334/united-states-v-apple-inc/)). **Denying a motion to dismiss is a pleading-stage ruling, not a finding of liability.**

**(C) Plausible remedies (commentary and my assessment, not a prediction of any ruling).**
- The theory is conduct-based, so the realistic remedies are **conduct remedies**: interoperability and API access for smartwatches, messaging, wallets/NFC, cloud gaming and super-app distribution.
- Apple has already made similar concessions in the EU under the DMA. That lowers the incremental cost of a US settlement on similar terms.
- Nothing in the reported record puts structural relief in serious play.
- **Dollars at risk are indirect** (lower hardware switching costs) **and back-ended**. On any schedule I found, a liability ruling before 2028 is unlikely (estimate).
- **The upside event is a consent decree.** A DMA-like settlement would likely be read as removing an overhang.

**Next dated event:** Judge Neals's rulings on (a) the agency-discovery order and (b) the document-cutoff impasse, both **undated**. Fact discovery closes ~May 2027 (reported).

### 3.1 Epic v. Apple — US anti-steering contempt (N.D. Cal. → 9th Cir. → SCOTUS No. 25-1311)

- The 2021 UCL injunction required Apple to allow developers to steer users to outside purchasing.

## 4. Front 4 — Tariffs and supply chain

### 4.2 What rate applies to an iPhone imported to the US today?

**I cannot confirm a figure from a primary source.** The best available reading, assembled from a single tariff tracker plus my own knowledge (all to be verified), is that **no additional US duty applies today to smartphones under HTS 8517.13, from either China or India**:
- The 232 Phase 1 scope excludes them.
- The reciprocal layer was carved out and/or invalidated.
- The global surcharge lapsed on 23 Jul 2026.
- 301 List 4B is suspended.
- The MFN rate is free.

The China-specific IEEPA "fentanyl" layer was the one non-zero item on China-origin iPhones in 2025. **Its status after the IEEPA litigation is NOT OBTAINED.** This is the single most important number to verify against HTSUS Chapter 99 and current CBP CSMS messages before the committee meets.

### 4.3 India shift

India-origin production is the mitigant against China-specific layers. Its value depends on the China–India tariff differential, which on the reading above is currently **near zero for smartphones**. **The India shift is insurance against future China-specific action, not a source of present savings.** I did not obtain 2026 data on the India share of US-bound iPhones.

**(C) Assessment.** Today's state is benign. The exposure is **Phase 2**: a derivative-product 232 would reach the finished phone, and Apple's protection would be its US investment commitment. Under the pharma template described in the SKHY file §2.2, exemption reportedly requires **formal agreements with agencies, not a pledge**. **Probability of a Phase 2 proclamation within 12 months: more likely than not (my estimate, carried from the SKHY file).** Whether Apple is carved out is the open question. **Next event: Phase 2 proclamation (undated).**

## 7. Recommended monitoring tripwires

1. **D.C. Circuit sets oral argument in US v. Google (No. 26-5023).** Put it on the calendar and brief the committee ahead of time. **Any appellate opinion that disturbs the "payments permitted" holding goes to the committee at once.** It is the largest dollar item.
2. **A Federal Register / White House proclamation under Section 232 that names "derivative products" or HTS 8517.** Read the annex the same day for smartphone coverage and for exemption mechanics, especially whether a "US investment" agreement is required and whether Apple has signed one.
3. **CBP CSMS message or HTSUS Chapter 99 change touching 8517.13.** This verifies §4.2. **It should be checked once before committee, not just watched.**
4. **SCOTUS 25-1311 decision.** If Apple wins the clear-prohibition point, the US link-out commission comes back into play (upside). If Apple loses, the status quo continues. Also watch the district court's rate ruling on Apple's reported 5–15% proposal.
5. **Google–Apple contract renewal reporting.** Under one-year terms, a reported cut in the revenue share is the slow-burn version of tripwire 1.
6. **Any announced DOJ–Apple consent decree**, or a Judge Neals scheduling order that sets a trial date.
7. **JFTC formal enforcement step under the MSCA**, or a Commission DMA decision on Apple's revised EU terms that imposes periodic penalties. Either would extend the "fee defeats the statute" theory across jurisdictions.
8. **A Chinese procurement directive, SAMR investigation notice, or CAC/MIIT action** naming Apple, or a pause in Apple Intelligence's China rollout.
