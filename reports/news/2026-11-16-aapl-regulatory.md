## 1. Front — US v. Google search remedies (default-search payment to Apple)

**Layer A — what the ruling says (as reported; final judgment not read).** Judge Mehta (D.D.C.) found Google liable for monopolising general search (Aug 2024) and entered remedies (Sep 2025 opinion; final judgment thereafter). As reported in 2026 coverage of the appeal: the judgment **does not categorically prohibit Google from paying Apple** to be the default; it **prohibits exclusive** search-distribution contracts; Google **may still compensate Apple for placement**; certain default-placement agreements with Apple and browser developers **must be limited to one-year terms**; data-sharing remedies were ordered
([MacRumors, 22 May 2026](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/05/22/google-search-monopoly-appeal/);
[itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/us-v-google-search-antitrust-trial-august-2026-updates/)).
I have not read the judgment text; the "one-year" term is from secondary coverage.

**Status: payments to Apple continue under a final judgment that permits non-exclusive paid defaults; that judgment is on appeal by both sides.** Alphabet's 10-Qs for Q2 and Q3 2026 exist ([Q3 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm)) but sec.gov is egress-blocked; I did not read their disclosure of the appeal.

**Dollars at risk.** The ~USD 20bn/yr figure is the 2022 payment disclosed at the liability trial (historical, per tasking; I did not re-source it this session). Apple does not break out the payment; it sits in Services licensing revenue. **Its margin is near 100% by construction (it is a payment for placement, not a product) — so a full loss would hit operating income roughly dollar-for-dollar. This is reasoning, not a sourced figure.** The judgment as entered **already reduced tenure risk (one-year terms) but not the payment itself**.

**Layer C — commentary / outcome scenarios (my assessment, not reported fact).**
- *Affirm remedies* (status quo): payment continues, re-papered annually. The annual term gives Google an option to renegotiate price each year — a slow-burn pricing risk rather than a cliff.
- *Government wins cross-appeal on payments*: the D.C. Circuit could remand for a payment ban or restriction. That is the **tail** for Apple. Mehta's stated reason for not banning payments (harm to distribution partners, and that a ban could hand share to rivals without improving competition) is the factual record the appellate court reviews; appellate courts generally defer on remedy discretion. I put this as a minority outcome; I do not assign a number.
- *Google wins on liability*: remedies fall, payment continues unconstrained. Favourable to Apple.
- **Next dated event: D.C. Circuit oral argument — date NOT OBTAINED.** Briefing appears complete or near-complete as of Nov 2026 (amicus stage); a decision in 2027 is the realistic window (estimate).

## 2. Front — US DOJ antitrust case against Apple (D.N.J., No. 2:24-cv-04055, filed 21 Mar 2024)

**Layer A — what the complaint alleges / what the court held.** Sherman Act §2 monopolisation of smartphone / performance-smartphone markets via restrictions on super apps, cloud gaming, cross-platform messaging, smartwatches and digital wallets. **Judge Julien Neals denied Apple's motion to dismiss in full on 30 Jun 2025** — holding monopoly power plausibly alleged and the ecosystem restrictions actionable rather than protected refusals to deal ([Justia, Doc. 283](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-jersey/njdce/2:2024cv04055/544402/283/); [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/68362334/united-states-v-apple-inc/)). A denial of a motion to dismiss is a pleading-stage ruling, not a finding of liability.

**Status: discovery. No trial date. Settlement talks reported (Jul 2026), outcome unknown.**

**Layer C — plausible remedies (commentary and my assessment, not predictions of a ruling).** The complaint's theory is conduct-based; the realistic remedy set is **conduct remedies** (opening APIs/interoperability for smartwatches, messaging, wallets/NFC, cloud-gaming and super-app distribution). Much of this Apple has already conceded in the EU under the DMA, which lowers the incremental cost of a US settlement on similar terms. Structural relief (divestiture) is not seriously in play on the reported record. **Dollars at risk are indirect** (erosion of ecosystem lock-in, i.e. hardware switching costs) and **back-ended — no liability ruling before 2028 on any schedule I found (estimate)**. The **upside event** is a consent decree; a settlement resembling the DMA interoperability concessions would likely be read as removing an overhang.

**Next dated event:** Judge Neals's ruling on (a) the agency-discovery objection and (b) the document-cutoff impasse — **dates not set/NOT OBTAINED**; fact-discovery close ~May 2027 (reported).
