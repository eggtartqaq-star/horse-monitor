## Section 1 — Top news items, ranked by materiality

### 1. Brookfield expands financing framework to $25B (2026-06-30) — MATERIALITY: VERY HIGH
**What happened `[FACT]`:** Brookfield and Bloom announced on June 30, 2026 a **fivefold expansion of their partnership from $5B to $25B**, to build and finance on-site fuel-cell power for AI infrastructure globally. The $5B framework itself dated from October 2025. The commitment sits inside Brookfield's **AI Infrastructure Fund** (launched Nov 2025, $100B target across AI factories, power and compute). Shares rose ~12% in extended trading on the news. (Bloom Energy IR / Globe and Mail / Investing.com / American Public Power Association, Jun 30 – Jul 2, 2026)

**Why it matters `[READ]`:** This is a *financing framework*, not a purchase order — the distinction is the whole ballgame and most coverage blurs it. What it genuinely de-risks is the **capital intensity of the customer**, not Bloom's own demand. Bloom's constraint on AI deals is that a 100–500MW behind-the-meter fuel-cell installation is a multi-hundred-million-dollar project the data-center developer must fund; a $25B balance sheet standing behind that removes the financing bottleneck. `[OUTLET]` Coverage framed it as fuel cells being "written into the core design of large AI data centers rather than treated as a small pilot."

**The offsetting fact `[FACT]`:** Q1 2026 **related-party sales to Brookfield JVs were $373.3M, up from $2.8M a year earlier**. `[READ]` A very large and fast-growing share of Bloom's revenue is being sold into vehicles co-owned by its own financing partner. That is not inherently improper — it is standard project-finance structure — but it means "revenue" and "third-party end-demand" are no longer the same number, and it is exactly the kind of structure the 2019–2020 Managed Service Agreement restatement arose from. Watch the related-party disclosure in the Q2 10-Q.

**Affected:** BE core thesis. **Route to:** Dept. 2 (巴爺爺 — related-party revenue quality; Peter — governance).

### 2. Hunterbrook short report + Bloom 8-K rebuttal (2026-07-08 / 07-09) — MATERIALITY: VERY HIGH
**What happened `[FACT]`:** On **July 8, 2026** Hunterbrook Media published "Bloom's Big Lie," alleging Bloom understates its dependence on **Chinese scandium oxide** and that 5GW production ambitions are unachievable. Core model: scaling from ~1GW of 2026 deployments to 5GW/yr would need **~220 tons of scandium oxide against ~240 tons of projected global supply**; Hunterbrook claimed trade data, satellite imagery and supplier conversations show **four separate routes** by which Chinese scandium still enters Bloom's chain. Hunterbrook disclosed it may hold positions profiting from a decline. Stock fell ~12% intraday.

On **July 9, 2026** Bloom filed an **8-K** and "categorically rejected" the claims as "false and misleading," stating it has sufficient scandium oxide for current demand and backlog, that supply is **not dependent on China**, and that it is not China-dependent to scale. Stock rebounded ~6%. (Hunterbrook; SEC 8-K be-20260709; TradingView; Benzinga; Yahoo Finance, Jul 8–9, 2026)

**Contradiction stands unresolved `[READ]`:** Neither side has been verified by a third party as of the reference date. Note the two claims are not strictly contradictory as worded — Hunterbrook alleges Chinese material is *present in the chain*; Bloom denies *dependence*. Those can both be true. Do not treat the 8-K as closure.

**Two distinct risk vectors `[READ]`:**
- (a) **Physical/scale risk** — if the ~240t global supply figure is roughly right, the binding constraint on Bloom's 5GW ambition is a rare-earth input, not factory capacity. This attacks the terminal-value story, not this year's numbers.
- (b) **Regulatory cross-link** — if Chinese sourcing is material, it collides with the **48E FEOC "material assistance"** restrictions phasing in from 2026 (see regulatory brief §1, §6), potentially threatening the *customer's* 30% ITC. `[READ]` This is the more dangerous vector and it is not, in my view, priced.

**Legal `[FACT]`:** Rosen Law and Kessler Topaz announced securities class-action **investigations** (not filed complaints as of ref date). Bloom carries a 2020 restatement history (Hindenburg 2019; $3.0M settlement, final approval May 2024).

**Route to:** The dictator (FEOC/48E linkage), Dept. 2 (10-Q supply-chain language on Jul 28).

## Section 2 — Industry context: the AI on-site power complex

### 2.1 The bull driver is real and quantified
**`[FACT]`:** US interconnection queues held **~2,600 GW** of proposed generation and storage in early 2026. Average time from interconnection application to commercial operation in **PJM rose from under 2 years (2008) to over 8 years (2025)**. Queues in **Northern Virginia, Phoenix and Dallas run 4–7 years**. PJM's 2025 long-term load forecast projects **+32GW of peak load 2024–2030, ~30GW of it data centers**. The US imports **>80% of large power transformers**, with 2–4 year lead times. (Utility Dive, EnkiAI, Quartz, PJM via trade press)

**`[READ]`:** This is the core of the thesis and it is not hype — it is a physical queue with a measurable length. The buyer of Bloom's product is not buying electricity; they are buying **schedule**. A hyperscaler whose GPU delivery slot is 12 months out and whose grid connection is 5 years out will pay an enormous premium per kW to close that gap. Bloom's ~90-day deployment is the entire product.

### 2.2 The competitive set — Bloom's real position
**`[FACT]` Gas turbines / GE Vernova:** backlog **116GW at end-Q2 2026** (up from 100GW in Q1); GEV expects **≥125GW under contract by end-2026** and total backlog hit **$176B in Q2 2026**. Capacity is **~10GW/yr of heavy-duty turbines through 2030** against that backlog — roughly **a decade of committed output** — with a path to 20GW/yr run-rate in Q3 2026, 24GW in 2028, 30GW in 2030. **Gas turbine prices on pace for +195% by end-2026 vs 2019.** (Utility Dive, CompressorTECH², GEV 8-K)

**`[READ]`:** GE Vernova's sold-out order book **is Bloom's moat**. Not technology — scheduling. A developer who wants a turbine in 2027 cannot have one. That is the scarcity window, and it is defined by someone else's factory, not Bloom's.

**`[FACT]` Economics, honestly stated:** Fuel cells run **$3,000–6,000/kW** capex vs **$400–800/kW** for gas turbines — roughly **5–8x more expensive per kW**. Fuel cells offer **15–20% higher efficiency** than most open-cycle turbines/recips and produce **no combustion emissions (no NOx, CO, particulates)**, easing local air permitting. Turbines deploy in 12–18 months; modular fuel cells in under a year (Bloom claims ~90 days). `[OUTLET]` The consensus trade view is **hybrid**: turbines for bulk power, fuel cells for high-efficiency baseload and emissions-sensitive sites. (Goldman Sachs, Utility Dive, Orrick, greengasturbines.com)

**`[FACT]` Sector momentum:** fuel-cell companies closed **$7.65B in binding AI-data-center agreements between Oct 2025 and Jan 2026** — of which Bloom captured the overwhelming majority (Brookfield $5B at the time, AEP $2.65B).

**`[FACT]` Peers:** FuelCell Energy (FCEL) — Jefferies upgrade Jun 26, 2026 drew capital out of BE. Plug Power — PEM/hydrogen-centric, a different and weaker exposure to this specific trade. **SMRs** — the long-horizon substitute; suitable only for buyers with long planning horizons and nuclear permitting capacity, i.e. **not a threat before the 2030s**.

**`[READ]` Ranking of competitive pressure on Bloom:** (1) gas turbines/recips once GEV/Siemens/Mitsubishi capacity unclogs ~2028–2030 — the real threat; (2) utility grid buildout itself; (3) FCEL — credible SOFC/carbonate alternative, currently a sentiment competitor more than a share competitor; (4) SMRs — post-2030; (5) Plug — largely orthogonal.

### 2.3 The natural-gas reality — stated plainly
`[FACT]` Bloom's SOFCs convert **natural gas** to electricity electrochemically, without combustion. `[READ]` The honest position: **Bloom is a natural-gas company with excellent local air quality and a hydrogen option.** The no-NOx/no-particulate claim is true and commercially valuable — it is what gets an air permit in Loudoun County or the South Coast AQMD where a diesel genset or recip engine would not. The "clean energy" framing is **not** true at the CO₂ level: it is a 15–20% efficiency improvement on gas, not decarbonisation. Three consequences:

1. **It is a tailwind under the current regime.** Per the regulatory brief, the EPA endangerment-finding rescission (final, Feb 18, 2026) removes the federal carbon-price tail risk on the installed base. Bloom is structurally *less* exposed to climate policy than its marketing implies.
2. **It is a liability under a future one.** A 20-year installed base burning gas behind the meter, outside utility emissions oversight, is a policy target waiting for a political turn. The regulatory brief's §5 tripwire (state PUCs / air districts moving on behind-the-meter gas) is the right one to watch.
3. **The ESG bid is not available.** `[READ]` Do not model an index/ESG-flow tailwind. Whatever multiple BE earns has to come from AI-infrastructure buyers, not sustainability mandates.

### 2.4 Life-cycle stage and how long the window stays open
`[READ]` The industry is in **early-to-mid growth with a hard, datable scarcity window**, not a durable structural moat:
- **Now → ~2028: window wide open.** GEV backlog ~a decade deep at current capacity; interconnection queues 4–8 years; no substitute can deliver power in 90 days. Bloom prices at will. This is where the 34% gross-margin guide comes from.
- **~2028 → 2030: window narrows.** GEV reaches 24GW/yr (2028) and targets 30GW/yr (2030); Siemens/Mitsubishi add capacity; transformer supply eases; some queue reform lands. The customer's *alternative* returns, and with it price pressure on a product that costs 5–8x per kW.
- **Post-2030: window closes or Bloom must have re-based.** Either the cost curve has brought fuel cells materially closer to turbines, or SMRs/grid buildout absorb the load and Bloom reverts to a niche high-efficiency/emissions-sensitive supplier.

`[READ]` So the terminal-value question is: **how much backlog can Bloom lock into 15–20 year contracts before ~2028?** That reframes the news flow — the AEP 20-year offtake and the Brookfield/Oaktree project structures matter far more than any single GW headline, because they convert a temporary scarcity rent into a contracted annuity. **Judge every future announcement on contract tenor, not gigawatts.**

### 2.5 Correlation warning — the shared master risk
`[READ]` **BE is another derivative of the same AI-capex pool that drives MU, MRVL and KLAC.** Different point in the value chain (power vs memory/networking/equipment), identical funding source: hyperscaler and neocloud capex budgets. The **shared master risk is AI-capex deceleration**, and BE is the *highest-beta* expression of it, because:
- it carries the highest multiple of the four (~30x trailing P/S);
- its revenue is concentrated in a handful of named counterparties (Oracle, Brookfield JVs, AEP, Nebius) rather than diversified end-markets;
- its order book is heavily "up to" GW options, which can be quietly unexercised without a cancellation headline;
- Nebius-type neocloud customers are themselves capital-markets-dependent.

**Portfolio implication for Zac / Mo Peter:** BE should be sized against a **combined AI-capex factor exposure** alongside MU/MRVL/KLAC, not as an independent "energy" or "industrials" diversifier. Its sector label is misleading; its factor loading is not. A -40% bear case is already publicly modelled on an AI-capex slowdown plus tax-credit paring.

## Section 6 — Industry call

### **OVERWEIGHT — AI on-site / distributed power. Confidence: MODERATE (6/10).**

**For:** The bottleneck is physical, measurable and multi-year (2,600GW queue; 8-year PJM interconnection; 116GW GEV backlog against ~10GW/yr capacity). Buyers are the best-capitalised companies on earth and are paying for schedule, not electricity. Federal policy is a tailwind (48E ITC restored; federal GHG risk reduced) and real money is underwriting it (Morgan Stanley tax equity on the Nebius deal). The demand is contracted, not surveyed.

**Against:** The window is datable and closes as turbine capacity normalises ~2028–2030. Valuations across the complex already discount several years of flawless execution. And the whole complex is a **single-factor bet on AI capex** — it is not a diversifier from a semiconductor sleeve, it is the same trade wearing an energy label.

**Confidence is capped at 6/10** because of the unresolved Hunterbrook allegation, the estimate dispersion into Jul 28, and the fact that the industry's key input assumption — that hyperscaler capex holds — is an assumption I cannot verify from news sources.

**Practical framing for the committee `[READ]`:** overweight the *theme*, but express it with size discipline against the combined AI-capex factor, and do not take initiating size in BE specifically ahead of the Jul 28 print given a ~32% implied move and an open disclosure question.
