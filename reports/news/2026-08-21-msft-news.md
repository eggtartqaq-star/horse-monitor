**[FACT] — a definitional trap, flagged.** These capex figures are on different
bases. USD 115.9bn is the press-release capital-expenditures line. The USD 41bn
Q4 figure is explicitly **"capital expenditures and finance leases."** Four

**[FACT]** Microsoft told the market on the FY26 Q4 call that calendar-2026 capex

**[MY READ] This is the most under-appreciated disclosure of the quarter.** A
USD 15bn cut to headline capex guidance that is explicitly **not** a cut to
spending, only a reclassification of where it appears. If an investor reads
"capex guidance cut $190bn → $175bn" as demand softening, they have read it
exactly backwards — management said the underlying expectation is unchanged.
Equally, if a bull reads the lower capex number as improving free cash flow,
that is also wrong: **operating leases still consume cash**, they just consume it
through operating expense rather than investing outflow. **Both the naive bull and
the naive bear reading of this number are wrong, and I expect both to circulate.**
A syndicated headline seen during this research — "Microsoft's $150 Billion Capex
'Magic': How an Accounting Shift Made It Wall Street's AI Darling" — confirms the
reclassification is already a contested narrative. I did not read that piece and
do not cite its argument; I cite only its existence.

**Cross-check for Dept 6 (Mo Peter / Mario):** FY26 capex USD 115.9bn plus
shareholder returns USD 48.7bn = **USD 164.6bn of cash uses against USD 133.7bn
of GAAP net income.** Uses exceed reported earnings. That does not by itself mean
negative free cash flow — depreciation is a large non-cash addback — but it is
the arithmetic that sits underneath the Morgan Stanley financing-gap argument in
§6.2, and Amy Hood's decision to volunteer that Microsoft will remain cash-flow
positive in FY2027 (§2a, point 5).

### 3.1 — The OpenAI relationship, restructured twice (MOST MATERIAL STRUCTURAL ITEM)

Two distinct events, both confirmed, both material, and they run in **opposite
directions** for Microsoft.

**Event 1 — the for-profit restructuring. [FACT] 2025-10-28.** OpenAI completed
its conversion, creating the **OpenAI Foundation** (non-profit, retains control)
and **OpenAI Group PBC** (the operating business). Reported by CNBC (2025-10-28),
Bloomberg (2025-10-28), The Hill, and DataCenterDynamics:

- Microsoft's stake moved from **32.5% to ~27%**, valued at **~USD 135bn**.
- **Microsoft's IP rights to models and products extended through 2032**, and now
  include models post-AGI.
- **The AGI declaration is now decided by an independent expert panel**, not by
  OpenAI unilaterally.
- **Microsoft lost its cloud right of first refusal.**
- OpenAI contracted to purchase an **incremental USD 250bn of Azure services.**

**Event 2 — the revenue-share and exclusivity reset. [FACT] 2026-04-27**, announced
by both parties (Microsoft official blog, 2026-04-27; OpenAI, "The next phase of
the Microsoft partnership"; covered by CNBC, Forbes, VentureBeat the same day):

- **OpenAI's revenue share to Microsoft is now capped: 20% of revenue through
  2030, hard-capped at USD 38bn.**
- **Microsoft will no longer pay a revenue share to OpenAI.**
- **OpenAI may now serve all its products on any cloud**, including AWS and
  Google Cloud.
- Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud provider and OpenAI products ship
  first on Azure "unless Microsoft decides otherwise."

**[OUTLET]** AI Magazine and Cryptobriefing reported the renegotiation saves
OpenAI an estimated **USD 97bn through 2030** versus the uncapped terms. Seeking
Alpha headlined it "Microsoft **falls** as OpenAI partnership evolves." VentureBeat
framed it as the two companies having "**gutted** their exclusive deal."

**[MY READ] — this is the item most likely to be misread by a screen.** The
December-2025-to-April-2026 arc is: Microsoft **gained** a 27% equity stake, IP
through 2032, and a USD 250bn Azure backlog; and **gave up** cloud exclusivity,
the ROFR, unilateral AGI definition, and the uncapped revenue share. A USD 38bn
hard cap on a stream that was previously uncapped **on a counterparty growing as
fast as OpenAI is a meaningful economic concession**, and the offset — Microsoft
no longer paying OpenAI a revenue share — flows through the *non-operating* line,
which is very likely part of why FY26 GAAP net income grew 31% against 21%
operating income growth (§2). **The bull reads the USD 250bn backlog. The bear
reads the capped share and lost exclusivity. Both are reading true facts from the
same agreement.** Route the legal/structural read to The dictator and the
counterparty-credit read to John (CRO).

## 4. The datacentre-lease story — confirmed vs inferred

### 4a. What is CONFIRMED
