## Flow verdict: NEUTRAL (two-sided churn, slight distribution tilt) — low-to-moderate confidence (~55%)

KLAC fell ~18–25% from its late-June all-time high (~$301–307, June 30) to ~$212–213 by July 17, but unlike a clean distribution tape the decline was punctuated by a powerful, upgrade-driven rebound (July 9) and stabilization near $213–215 into July 22. Heavy sell days on expanding volume (distribution) are offset by genuine, volume-backed dip-buying and a fresh large institutional position — this is churn at a lower level, not one-directional supply. Confidence is deliberately capped: granular per-print block/dark-pool data and per-strike OI/gamma were not accessible, and the read is dominated by the July 28 earnings event now warping options pricing.

## Volume character
- Two-sided, event-driven tape rather than steady accumulation or distribution:
  - **Jul 7:** −10%+ session — Samsung Q2 earnings triggered a broad chip-equipment selloff; profit-taking off the June AI-valuation peak. Down leg on heavy volume = distribution signature.
  - **Jul 9:** sharp rebound (+3.77% close per TradingKey; intraday prints as high as ~+10.65%) on a cluster of upgrades (TD Cowen, Stifel, Cantor $325, Morgan Stanley $274, Barclays). Volume-backed bounce off the lows = real dip-buying / accumulation signature.
  - **Jul 14:** +3.65%. **Jul 15:** −4.93% to $224.50. **Jul 16–17:** softer again around TSMC's Q2 print; ~$212–213 by Jul 17.
  - Net: down ~18% from $259.56 (Jun 18) to ~$212.75 (Jul 17); ~25% below the Jun 30 high.
- Average volume ~14.6M shares/day; the big moves (Jul 7, Jul 9, Jul 15) were clearly above-average, i.e. conviction on both the down and up legs — the definition of churn.
- **Insider footprint leans distributive:** CFO Bren Higgins sold ~27,700 shares in early July; a separate CEO sale of 4,512 shares was reported. Small relative to float but directionally one-way selling from insiders into the peak.

## Options flow read
- **Earnings July 28 (after close) dominates the options tape.** IV reported at ~95%, ~100th percentile of its 1-year range — options are pricing a large event move, so any single-day put/call print is contaminated by event hedging and speculative positioning, not directional conviction.
- Mixed, anecdotal signals — no clean pattern:
  - Call-buying flags: ~9,821 calls (+52% vs ~6,464 avg) one session; ~7,243 calls (+58% vs ~4,574 avg) earlier in July — bullish tilt on those days.
  - Counterweight: a snapshot put/call ratio of 2.96 (1,911 puts / 646 calls) on another read — heavy put activity, consistent with pre-earnings hedging into a rich IV.
- Pattern over anecdote: with IV pinned at the top of its range and a binary event 9 days out, I do not read either the call bursts or the put snapshot as a durable directional signal. Per-strike OI, gamma and max-pain were not retrievable (OptionCharts/Barchart 403).

## Institutional footprint (all as of 2026-03-31, filed ~May 2026 — LAGGED, pre-dates the June peak and July selloff)
- Breadth roughly balanced, slight net reducers by count: ~849 institutions added vs ~887 reduced (MarketBeat). ~2,208 filers hold ~123.7M shares.
- Large two-way moves:
  - Reducers: Capital International Investors −50.0% (−2,722,695 sh, ~$4.0B); T. Rowe Price −37.9% (−1,024,401 sh, ~$1.5B).
  - Adders: FMR (Fidelity) +73.0% (+1,857,271 sh, ~$2.7B); Wellington +228.9% (+1,002,441 sh, ~$1.5B); PineStone Asset Management opened a new ~$151.7M / +1,099,300-share position (Quiver).
- Read: mixed-to-slightly-distributive by headcount but with several conviction adds. Critically, all of this is a 2026-03-31 snapshot at very different (lower) prices — it says nothing about who was buying or selling during the June melt-up or the July drop. Q2 2026 positioning won't be visible until ~mid-August.

## Data limitations (findings, not gaps filled with guesses)
- Per firm guidance, the granular flow vendors 403 through the proxy: Barchart, Stocknear, Fintel, OptionCharts, HedgeFollow, stockanalysis.com. No clean per-strike OI/gamma, no max-pain, no dark-pool/block prints were retrievable. Dark-pool participation for KLAC is therefore a visibility gap, stated as such — not inferred.
- Price/volume figures are search-surfaced site snapshots; some sources conflict on the exact ATH (~$301.71 vs ~$307.37) and on Jul 9 magnitude (+3.77% close vs +10.65% intraday). Levels are approximate; direction is consistent.
- The verdict is a lower-confidence NEUTRAL precisely because the tape is genuinely two-sided and the dominant near-term driver (Jul 28 earnings) is unresolved.
