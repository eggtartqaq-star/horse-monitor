## Cycle/regime assessment

**Verdict: LATE-CYCLE expansion, unchanged from 2026-07-18. Confidence ~70%** (late-cycle 70% / mid-cycle extension 25% / imminent contraction 5%). Full evidence base in the MU report; nothing in the Jul 17–19 window changes it. Two marginal updates: (1) the oil re-spike strengthens the "stagflation-lite policy trap" element of the late-cycle read; (2) the AI-semis complex — the cycle's speculative epicenter — has begun correcting violently (MRVL −42.8%, MU −33.6% from highs, Korean chip rout, SK Hynix-led selloffs), which is *consistent with* late-cycle behavior rather than evidence against it.

**How the regime transmits to MRVL vs MU — the core of this note:**

1. **Revenue driver: design wins and multi-year programs vs spot pricing.** MU's earnings are levered to memory spot/contract *prices* — the 84.9% GM is a price phenomenon that mean-reverts violently. MRVL's revenue is *unit ramps against sockets already won* (Trainium, Maia), with bookings and two raised guides (FY27 ~$11.5B, FY28 ~$16.5B). In a capex slowdown, MRVL's downside shape is **pushed-out ramps and slower growth, not price collapse** — a smoother, later cyclicality. Offsetting: extreme concentration (data center 76% of revenue; two tier-1 XPU customers). A single socket loss or one hyperscaler pausing a program generation is a 10–20%+ revenue event, and sockets are re-competed every generation. MRVL trades peak-margin risk (MU's problem) for customer-concentration risk (its own).

2. **Duration sensitivity — the direct rates channel.** MRVL at ≈42x forward (vs 5-yr avg ~32x) with the equity story resting on FY28 ($16.5B) and beyond is a textbook long-duration asset. At 10Y ~4.55–4.62% with a hike-biased Fed, every 25bp of yield pressure compresses the multiple; the market just demonstrated the beta with −7.75% and −8% single sessions on *macro* headlines, zero company news. This channel is more punishing for MRVL than MU (MU's near-term cash flows are enormous and contract-locked; MRVL's are back-loaded). However — roughly a third of that duration risk has now been *paid*: the multiple de-rated from ~66x to ~42x forward in six weeks.

3. **Hyperscaler capex sustainability — the dominant channel.** 2026 capex of ~$673–725B (+~76%) is the single number MRVL lives on. The macro linkage is direct: hyperscalers fund incremental capex increasingly with debt priced off the long end; higher-for-longer raises their hurdle rates exactly as the monetization debate ("who can prove ROI") heats up. UBS's path — +25% in 2027, +6% in 2028 — is now the visible consensus fear, and MRVL's FY28 +45% revenue target must be achieved *against* that deceleration. It is arithmetically possible (custom ASICs gaining share of a decelerating total: hyperscalers shift spend toward owned silicon for TCO precisely when budgets tighten — the one genuinely counter-cyclical feature of MRVL's model), but it leaves no room for socket slippage. Note also: capex *growth* decelerating to +25% still means capex *rising* — this is a second-derivative fear, and the stock has already repriced ~43% on it.

4. **Positioning/crowding as a macro amplifier.** 82% of BofA-surveyed managers call semis the most crowded trade and none are short. In a late-cycle regime, crowding converts modest macro news into −8% sessions. This is symmetric: it also fueled the +7% bounce on Jul 9. Expect regime-driven volatility, not regime-driven trend, until the Aug 20 print or a hyperscaler guides capex.
