## Summary
- **Regime: late-cycle inflationary expansion; the Fed is tightening into strength (medium confidence, ~60%).** This is not a growth scare. Q3 GDP grew 3.4%, October payrolls +165k, ISM manufacturing 55.5. Gold and long bonds are falling *together*, which is the signature of a real-rate and dollar shock, not of risk-off.
- **Two overlapping drivers.**
  - **A rate/inflation scare.** The 10y went from 4.86% (6 Nov) to ~5.10–5.15% (12–13 Nov). DXY hit a 19-month high and a December hike was ~80%+ priced.
  - **An older AI/semis unwind on top of it.** SMH is -31% from its 22 Jun high, the hyperscaler capex-raise cadence stopped in October, and the memory cycle is late.
  - Equal weight is only -2.4% from its high, so this is a **correction in the leaders, not a broad bear market**.
- **Coca-Cola-type staples: the right trend, a late entry.**
  - KO at 29.2× earns a ~3.4% earnings yield against a ~5.1% 10y, and its 2.1% dividend sits below a ~3.9% cash rate. RSI is 74.
  - The staples bid is money leaving AI, not a bond trade. If that flow pauses, rates bite.
- **Style tilt (see table):**
  - **Overweight:** T-bills/short cash and equal-weight; modest overweight in financials.
  - **Neutral:** defensives and healthcare (stage in on pullbacks; prefer lower-multiple, higher-yield defensives over 29× staples), and quality mega-cap tech.
  - **Underweight:** semis/AI hardware and long Treasuries.
- **Next dates:**

  - Payrolls 4 Dec, FOMC 9 Dec, CPI 10 Dec.
  - MU ~16 Dec (unconfirmed).
  - The view flips on a sub-4.85% 10y with a Fed pause, or on growth breaking (see Falsifiers).

## Policy read

**What the Fed did [FACT].** It hiked 25bp unanimously in September, its first hike in three years, to 3.75–4.00%. It held on 28 Oct, but two voters (Cook, Hammack) dissented *for another hike*. Chair Warsh said at Jackson Hole that hikes may be needed and has since refused to pre-commit.

**What the data since 28 Oct say [FACT → READ].** Four of the five post-meeting releases lean hawkish:
- Payrolls +165k with upward revisions and unemployment down to 4.1% (6 Nov).
- CPI and core CPI hotter than consensus (10 Nov). Derived annualised pace: 0.4% m/m ≈ 4.9%/yr headline, 0.3% ≈ 3.7%/yr core. That is arithmetic on rounded m/m figures, so a range, not a print.
- ISM prices paid at 75.2 (manufacturing) and 73.0 (services), with ISM itself warning that more pricing pressure is coming.
- Q3 real GDP at 3.4% and nominal at 5.6%.

The one dovish datum is PPI, 0.1pt under consensus on both m/m and y/y. Even so, y/y is 6.5%, the highest since Nov 2022.

**What markets price [FACT, secondary].**
- A December hike was ~80% as of 9 Nov, the last reading I found. After the 10 Nov CPI it is probably higher [READ, not obtained].
- The 2y at ~4.86% (13 Nov, unverified) sits ~86bp above the 4.00% top of the funds range [derived]. The market is therefore pricing more than one further hike [READ].
- The 2s10s curve is ~+28bp (5.14% minus 4.86%, same source, 13 Nov) [derived]. This is a **bear steepening**: long yields are rising because of inflation and term premium, not because of growth fears.

**Forecast [EST, my view]:**
- **9 Dec FOMC: +25bp to 4.00–4.25%, probability ~75%.** Hold with hawkish guidance ~22%. Cut under 3%.
- At least one more hike in H1 2027: ~50%.
- 10y stays in a 4.85–5.40% range through year-end: ~65% confidence.

**Fiscal/tariff after the midterms [READ].** A divided government (Democratic Congress) slightly reduces fiscal-expansion risk; one outlet reported the 10y falling ~6bp on the result. But tariffs remain under executive control (Morgan Stanley, pre-vote), so there is no evidence of tariff relief. Apple's guide, for one, assumes the tariff status quo. The bigger inflation shock is oil/Iran, not fiscal policy.

**The dollar [FACT/READ].** DXY's highest close of 2026 (103.17, 12 Nov) tightens financial conditions. It is also a 2027 headwind for multinational staples: KO's FY26 EPS carried a ~3% FX *tailwind*, and that reverses if the dollar holds here [READ].

[READ] **Gold and long bonds falling together is the signature of a real-rate and dollar shock**, i.e. hawkish repricing. It is not risk-off; in a risk-off episode both would rally. This tells me the defensive bid in staples is **not** a bond trade. Bond proxies (XLP, KO, PG) are making highs while bonds make lows, a break in the usual correlation. The staples bid is a **flight from AI-capex exposure into earnings visibility and pricing power**. It is flow-driven, and that makes it vulnerable: if the flow pauses, the 5%+ 10y reasserts itself on 26–29× multiples.

[READ] **Equal weight beating cap weight (+5.4pt in 1m) means this is a correction in the leaders, not a broad bear market.** RSP set its 52-week high on 5 Nov and is 2.4% below it. SPY is 5.3% below its high and QQQ 13.3%.

### Regime call

**Late-cycle inflationary expansion. The Fed is tightening into strength, and equity leadership is rotating away from AI-capex beneficiaries toward breadth and defensives.** Confidence: **medium (~60%)**.
- **For late-cycle:** unemployment 4.1% near lows; input prices 73–75; Fed hiking; bear-steepening curve; defensives leading; memory/semis cycle at a visible peak; gold and bonds both failing as hedges.
- **Against (the mid-cycle re-acceleration case, ~25%):** GDP and ISM manufacturing are *accelerating*, breadth is healthy, financials are firm. A one-and-done December hike with peaking inflation would make this a mid-cycle wobble.
- **Contraction / pre-recession (~15%):** U-6 rising, a weak 12-month payroll trend, and policy lags from a hiking cycle at 5%+ long yields.

## Risks to this view / Falsifiers

**Bear cases against my own read:**
1. **"You're underweight the bottom."** NVDA at 19× and RSI 22 is the cheapest and most oversold it has been in this table, and hyperscaler capex dollars were still +108% y/y in Q3. A strong NVDA guide tonight plus Alphabet's "significantly higher" 2027 could produce a sharp short-covering rally, in which underweight semis and overweight cash lag badly.
2. **"Inflation is already peaking."** PPI came in under consensus, both prices-paid indices fell, and CPI m/m slowed 0.6→0.4. If November CPI (10 Dec) is soft and the Fed hikes once and signals it is done, long bonds rally, and **both** staples (as bond proxies again) and growth tech rerate up. Cash and financials then underperform.
3. **"It's a growth scare in waiting, and you're too cautious on staples."** U-6 is rising, the 12-month payroll average is 48k, and this is a hiking cycle into 5%+ long yields. If growth cracks, premium staples keep working regardless of a 29× multiple, and neutral is too low.

**Falsifiers (any one changes the tilt):**
- **Rate scare over:** 10y closes back below **4.85%** (its 5–6 Nov level) *and* DXY below **~101.5** (its early-Nov low), or the Fed skips on 9 Dec with dovish guidance. Then cut cash, raise quality tech and long duration.
- **AI unwind over:** a strong NVDA guide, SMH reclaiming its MA50, and no MU guide cut on ~16 Dec. Then lift semis to neutral.
- **Correction goes broad:** RSP closes below its MA200 or underperforms SPY for a month. Then this is no longer a leadership rotation; add to cash and defensives.
- **Growth breaks:** negative payrolls, unemployment ≥ 4.4%, or ISM manufacturing < 50. Then staples, healthcare and long Treasuries go to overweight, and financials to underweight.
- **Staples rotation exhausts:** KO/PG close below their MA50 (≈ $91.0 / ≈ $154.2, derived) while semis do *not* rally. That would be rates biting bond proxies; take staples to underweight.
