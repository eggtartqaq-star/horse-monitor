## Cycle/regime assessment

**Verdict: LATE-CYCLE expansion, unchanged. Confidence ~70%** (late-cycle 70% / mid-cycle extension 25% / imminent contraction 5%). The evidence base is in the MU/MRVL notes. The KLAC-specific nuance: **within a late-cycle regime, capital equipment is the classic cyclical, but process control is the most defensive slice of WFE, and this particular WFE cycle is still forecast to expand.** That combination pulls KLAC toward the "quality cyclical" corner rather than the "speculative AI beta" corner where MRVL/MU sit.

**How the regime transmits to KLAC — the core of this note (four channels):**

1. **WFE capex cyclicality vs fabless/memory.** KLAC sells into fab *capital budgets* — the most deferrable line in a downturn, and equipment orders lead wafer output, so KLAC can inflect before end-demand. That is the late-cycle risk. **Two structural offsets:** (a) **process-control intensity rises secularly** — EUV, gate-all-around/2nm, and advanced packaging add process steps and defectivity risk per wafer, so inspection/metrology dollars grow faster than total WFE (KLA gained share in *both* overall WFE and process control in Q3, and advanced packaging grew +70% y/y); (b) the cycle itself is **still expanding** — SEMI sees WFE +9% to $135.2B in 2026 and record $156B total equipment in 2027. Unlike MU (spot-price commodity) or MRVL (two-customer ramp), KLAC's driver is a broad, diversified, still-rising capex pool. Net: **later, shallower cyclicality with a secular growth overlay.**

2. **Duration / multiple sensitivity — the direct rates channel.** KLAC at ~42x forward (≈2x its ~20–25x history) is a long-duration asset, and the market is pricing a "durable upcycle" (targets $270–$325, above spot). At a 4.55–4.62% 10Y with a hike-biased Fed, that premium is the vulnerable variable — every 25bp of yield pressure compresses it. This is KLAC's single largest regime exposure. It is **less severe than MRVL's** because KLAC's cash flows are current, diversified, and dividend-backed (a 21%-raised payout is a quality signal and a partial valuation floor), but the *starting* premium is rich and, crucially, **only ~30% has been unwound** vs MRVL's ~43% — the market has not repriced KLAC's quality premium the way it repriced MRVL's speculative one.

3. **WFE sustainability: AI-capex boom vs China normalization — the two-sided demand question.** The bull leg (leading-edge logic, HBM, advanced packaging) is precisely where KLAC over-indexes and is booming. The bear leg is **China at 24% of revenue** — a pool inflated by 2023–25 mature-node build-out and exposed to both digestion and export-control tightening (a LeBron/geopolitics variable). The same hyperscaler-capex deceleration that repriced MRVL (UBS: +76% 2026 → +25% 2027 → +6% 2028) will eventually reach *fab* capex with a lag; SEMI's expansion forecast through 2027 is optimistic and assumes the AI leg keeps funding leading-edge. KLAC's diversification means no single customer or hyperscaler pause is a 10–20% revenue event (unlike MRVL), but a synchronized WFE roll — AI capex decelerating *and* China normalizing — would hit it hard and is the genuine late-cycle tail.

4. **Positioning/crowding.** Semis remain the most crowded trade (82% of BofA-surveyed managers; MRVL note). KLAC's own sell-side is still *raising* targets above spot — meaning the crowd has not capitulated on KLAC the way it has on MRVL. In a late-cycle regime that converts modest macro news into −8% sessions (which is exactly what took KLAC from $230 on Jul 14 to $212.75 on Jul 17), an un-purged consensus long is a source of downside beta into the Jul 28 print and the Jul 29 FOMC.
