## 1. Segment taxonomy — "biotech" is five different asset classes

This is the single most important section for a non-specialist reader. The word "biotech" is
used to describe businesses whose risk profiles have almost nothing in common.

### 1.1 CRO / CDMO — "picks and shovels"

**What it is.** Contract Research Organisations run trials and discovery chemistry for other
people's drugs. Contract Development & Manufacturing Organisations make the molecule. They sell
*services*, under contract, to drug developers.

**Why the risk is different.** They do not own the drug. If a customer's Phase III fails, the CRO
has already been paid for the work. Revenue is contracted and visible — Medpace disclosed a
**backlog of $2.93bn at 31 Mar 2026, with ~$1.9–1.94bn expected to convert to revenue over the
next 12 months** ([Yahoo Finance / Zacks, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/medpace-growth-trends-show-cro-135200975.html)).
That is a fundamentally different information set from a pre-revenue biotech, where you know
nothing until the data readout.

**What they are exposed to instead.** (a) *Customer funding cycles* — when biotech venture funding
and IPOs dry up, small-biotech R&D spend falls and bookings weaken; (b) *cancellation risk* in
backlog (oncology and cardiovascular have been the larger sources of recent cancellations —
[Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/medpace-stock-outlook-turns-backlog-140000420.html));
(c) *policy risk*, which is now the dominant factor for the Chinese CROs — see Section 5.

**This is where 2359.HK sits.**

### 1.2 Large-cap pharma

Diversified, profitable, dividend-paying, many products. Risk is not "will the drug work" but
*patent cliffs*, *pricing policy*, and *whether the pipeline replaces expiring revenue*. These
trade on earnings multiples like any other mature industrial. Eli Lilly's market cap was
**$1.5 trillion as of early February 2026**
([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/272716/global-top-biotech-and-pharmaceutical-companies-based-on-market-value/)) —
this is a mega-cap equity, not a speculation.

### 1.3 Commercial-stage biotech

Has at least one approved, selling drug. Real revenue, often still reinvesting heavily. Risk is
*concentration* (frequently one or two products carry the whole company), *competition*, and
*reimbursement*. Alnylam crossed **$1bn in quarterly product revenue for the first time in Q1
2026** ([Alnylam IR](https://investors.alnylam.com/press-release?id=29796)) — that is a company
that has crossed from speculation into a business, but with heavy dependence on the TTR franchise.

### 1.4 Clinical-stage / pre-revenue — this is where beginners get destroyed

No approved product. No revenue. The company is a pile of cash burning down against a series of
binary data readouts. Value moves in step-functions of ±50% on a single press release.

**HKEX Chapter 18A exists specifically to let these companies list without a revenue or profit
track record** ([Charltons, Chapter 18A rules](https://www.charltonslaw.com/legal/ipo/New-Rules-Listing-Biotech-on-HKEx.pdf)).
That is the whole point of the chapter. A "18A stock" is, by construction, a company the exchange
has excused from the normal financial-eligibility tests. It is not a defect in the rule — it is a
label telling you exactly what you are buying.

**The base rates are brutal (Section 4).**

### 1.5 Tools / diagnostics / life-science equipment

Sells instruments, reagents, sequencers and tests to everyone in the industry. Behaves more like
a capital-goods/consumables business: recurring consumable revenue, exposed to customer capex
cycles, academic/NIH funding, and China demand. Thermo Fisher grew Q2 2026 revenue **10% to
$11.99bn, organic +5%, adjusted EPS +13% to $6.03**
([Yahoo Finance, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/thermo-fisher-raises-2026-outlook-153700819.html)).
Mid-single-digit organic growth — a mature industrial, not a growth speculation.

### 1.6 Risk ladder — plain language

| Segment | What kills you | Time to find out | Can it go to zero? |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRO / CDMO | Customer funding cycle; **policy/sanctions**; backlog cancellation | Quarters | Very unlikely (real cash flows) |
| Large-cap pharma | Patent cliff; drug pricing policy; tariffs | Years | Effectively no |
| Commercial-stage biotech | Product concentration; competitor entry; reimbursement | Quarters–years | Unlikely but possible |
| **Clinical-stage / 18A** | **One trial readout; cash runway** | **A single morning** | **Yes, routinely** |
| Tools / diagnostics | Customer capex cycle; funding; China | Quarters | Very unlikely |

A pre-revenue 18A name and a CRO are not the same asset class. Sizing them the same way in a
portfolio is a category error.

## 4. How to navigate a sector this size

### 4.1 The base rate for single pre-revenue biotech names

This is the number the Owner should read twice.

- BIO analysed **9,985 phase transitions across 7,455 development programmes at 1,103 companies**
  and found a **cumulative approval rate from Phase I of 9.6%**
  ([Clinical Development Success Rates for Investigational Drugs](https://diyhpl.us/~nmz787/pdf/Clinical_development_success_rates_for_investigational_drugs.pdf)).
- Wong, Siah & Lo (Biostatistics, Oxford) put overall approval at **13.8% of all drug development
  programmes**, with more recent Phase I likelihood-of-approval as low as **6.7%**
  ([Oxford Academic, Biostatistics](https://academic.oup.com/biostatistics/article/20/2/273/4817524)).
- Phase-by-phase, the survival funnel is roughly Phase I ~70% pass, Phase II ~33%, Phase III
  ~25–30% (same sources) — **Phase II is the killing field.**
- Odds improve materially with the right selection: rare-disease non-oncology programmes achieved
  **~25%** overall success and biomarker-guided programmes **~26%**
  ([Nature Reviews Drug Discovery, Parsing clinical success rates](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrd.2016.136)).

**Translation.** If you buy one pre-revenue biotech, roughly **nine times in ten the lead asset
never becomes an approved drug.** Even in the favourable subsets you are at one-in-four. And the
financial reality confirms it: at mid-2026 **nearly half of publicly traded US biotechs were
trading below the cash on their balance sheets**
([Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/navigating-current-challenges-in-biotech-m-and-a)),
and **219 companies have been lost from the sector over four years**
([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/business/four-years-and-219-lost-companies-later-biotech-still-has-a-cash-problem)).
"Liquidation-as-a-service" is now a named practice area — Concentra alone made at least seven
approaches in 2025 ([Julien Willard](https://julienwillard.com/insights/biotech-reverse-merger-shell-shortage)).

Note carefully: those base rates are **per drug programme**, and the portfolio maths is what saves
the sector. An index owning 157 shots on goal captures the 10% that work. An investor owning
three names very plausibly captures zero. **This is the entire argument for ETFs in this sector,
and it is a mathematical argument, not a stylistic preference.**

### 4.3 The rule I would apply

1. **Sector exposure via ETF is the default.** Get the 10% winners by owning the whole funnel.
2. **Single names only in segments where you can underwrite a business** — CRO/CDMO, large-cap
   pharma, commercial-stage biotech, tools. In those, revenue, backlog and margins are analysable.
3. **Clinical-stage / 18A single names: treat as an options-style sleeve, size accordingly.**
   Position sizing should assume total loss is the modal outcome, not the tail.

### 5.2 The one factor that dominates the stock

**A CRO's risk is customer risk and policy risk, not trial risk.** For WuXi AppTec, the policy leg
is now the whole story.

Timeline, sourced:

1. **18 December 2025** — the **BIOSECURE Act was signed into law** as part of the FY2026 NDAA
   ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/01/biosecure-act-enacted),
   [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/biosecure-act-becomes-law-limiting-grants-with-biotechnology-companies-of-concern)).
2. **8 June 2026** — the US Department of War published its annual **Section 1260H list of 188
   "Chinese military companies," adding WuXi AppTec Co. Ltd.**
   ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/wuxi-apptec-added-to-dows-1260h-list),
   [FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/06/wuxi-apptecs-1260h-listing-brings-the-biosecure-act-back-to-center-stage/)).
   The stock fell as much as 8% intraday, closing **down 2.6%** that session
   ([Yicai Global](https://www.yicaiglobal.com/news/wuxi-apptec-drops-after-us-adds-it-to-list-of-firms-with-alleged-ties-to-chinese-military)).
3. **The mechanism is not yet live.** Because of the 1260H listing, WuXi AppTec satisfies the
   statutory criteria for "biotechnology company of concern" (BCOC) status **once OMB establishes
   and implements a finding process**. Prohibitions **do not take effect for 1260H BCOCs until 60
   days after the FAR Council revises the Federal Acquisition Regulation**, and **OMB must publish
   its BCOC list by December 2026** ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/wuxi-apptec-added-to-dows-1260h-list)).
4. **WuXi AppTec is litigating the designation** ([MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/wuxi-apptec-an-update-on-our-legal-action-challenging-1260h-designation-ce7f5cd8de80f124)).
5. **WuXi Biologics and WuXi XDC are not currently designated** as companies of concern under the
   Act, though they remain under review (same source set). Sell-side reads the fundamental impact
   as limited — UBS said the 1260H list has limited fundamental impact
   ([AAStocks](http://www.aastocks.com/en/mobile/news.aspx?newsid=NOW.1528594&newstype=71&newssource=AAFN)),
   Macquarie recommended buying on the basis that inclusion has no impact
   ([Futu](https://news.futunn.com/en/post/68949313/macquarie-recommends-buying-wuxi-apptec-02359-hk-predicting-that-its)).

**The asymmetry that defines the position: the US is ~72% of WuXi AppTec's revenue**
([Futu](https://news.futunn.com/en/post/70469353/strong-performance-in-the-us-market-wuxi-apptec-s-net)).
The company's growth engine and its principal political risk are the *same jurisdiction*. There is
no diversification inside the business against this risk.

The market has, so far, chosen fundamentals over the overhang: H shares **jumped more than 10% to
a near-five-year high** after the H1 beat ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/wuxi-apptec-extends-rally-to-nearly-fiveyear-high-after-h1-earnings-beat-4832784)).

**My read.** This is a binary policy option sitting on top of an excellent operating business.
That is a genuinely difficult combination to size, because the operating business justifies a
premium and the policy leg justifies a discount, and they do not net out — they compound the
variance. **The December 2026 OMB BCOC list is the dated catalyst.** Until then the stock is
trading fundamentals; after it, it may not be.

Note also the second-order point: BIOSECURE restricting Western pharma's use of Chinese CROs would
be a **positive** for IQV, CRL, MEDP and Western CDMO capacity. The same event is not neutral
across the segment.

## 6. Emerging themes & inflection points

**1. China as the world's molecule source — the strongest secular signal in the sector.**
Cross-border licensing of Chinese biotech assets rose from **42 deals in 2022 to 93 in 2025
(+120%)**, with total upfront value up roughly **400%, from $1.1bn to $5.6bn**
([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/analyst-china-no-longer-bargain-basement-biotech-acquisitions)).
Average upfront per deal is up **230%, from $52m (2022) to $172m (2026 YTD)** (same source).
**38 out-licensing deals were announced by mid-February 2026 alone**, average deal value up 76% to
$1.3bn, upfronts doubled to $77.7m
([Academicjobs summary of Reuters analysis](https://www.academicjobs.com/cn/research-publication-news/record-surge-china-biotech-out-licensing-deals-early-2026-4916),
[Reuters via StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Reuters/Analysis-China+biotech+licensing+boom+to+hit+record+in+2026+as+pipeline+swells/25994472.html)).
China accounts for **nearly 90% of global ADC licensing activity**
([Vision Lifesciences](https://visionlifesciences.com/insights/china-biotech-licensing-opportunities-2026)).
This is a *structural* repricing of where innovation originates, and it is the core bull case for
the HK 18A cohort — the exit is now a licensing deal with Western pharma, not only a domestic launch.

**2. Tariffs and MFN pricing — a regime change for pharma economics, not a cyclical wobble.**
On **2 April 2026** the White House issued a Section 232 proclamation imposing **0%–100% tariffs on
patented pharmaceuticals and APIs**
([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/04/adjusting-imports-of-pharmaceuticals-and-pharmaceutical-ingredients-into-the-united-states/),
[Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/04/trade-winds-shift-for-pharma)).
Companies with **both an MFN pricing agreement with HHS and an onshoring agreement pay 0%** — but
that expires **20 January 2029**, after which it escalates to 20% or 100% depending on onshoring
status. Effective dates are **31 July 2026** for some firms and **29 September 2026** for others.
**As of May 2026, 17 large pharma companies had signed voluntary MFN pricing agreements**
([Clinical Leader](https://www.clinicalleader.com/doc/u-s-pharma-tariffs-and-mfn-become-law-after-april-update-0001)).
Two consequences: large pharma has traded US price for tariff relief (a permanent margin
concession), and **US onshoring of manufacturing is now a policy-mandated capex cycle** — read
through to Western CDMO capacity.

**3. The barbell inside biotech is extreme.** Deal-making is booming — **$106bn across 201
transactions in 2026 YTD** ([Yahoo Finance/Zacks](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/biotech-etfs-put-strong-show-in-1h-2026-160000067.html)) —
while **nearly half of listed US biotechs trade below cash**. Capital is flowing to validated
assets and abandoning the rest. In H1 2026 there were **13 conventional IPOs raising ~$4bn versus
7 reverse mergers carrying ~$1.9bn of concurrent financing**
([Julien Willard](https://julienwillard.com/insights/biotech-reverse-merger-shell-shortage)) —
the IPO window has reopened, but selectively.

**4. Hong Kong has won the biotech listing venue competition, at least for now.** 12 biotech IPOs
raising $1.3bn in 2026, **first globally by fundraising volume**, second-largest IPO market for
biotech after the US ([BioSpectrum Asia](https://www.biospectrumasia.com/analysis/26/26569/hong-kong-betting-big-on-biotech-ipos.html)).
HKEX is running a **March 2026 Listing Framework Competitiveness Review consultation**
([HKEX CP202603](https://www.hkex.com.hk/-/media/HKEX-Market/News/Market-Consultations/2016-Present/March-2026-Competitiveness-Review/Consultation-Paper/cp202603.pdf))
— worth monitoring for further 18A/18C liberalisation.

## 7. Life-cycle placement

| Segment | Stage | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Large-cap pharma** | **Mature** | Mega-cap, dividend-paying, diversified; growth now policy-constrained (MFN pricing concessions by 17 firms); competitive dynamics are patent cliffs and pipeline replacement |
| **Life-science tools / diagnostics** | **Mature, mid-cycle recovery** | TMO organic growth +5%; DHR cutting top end of core growth guidance on respiratory weakness. Mid-single-digit organic = mature industrial, currently in an upswing off a destocking trough |
| **CRO / CDMO (Western)** | **Growth, cyclically recovering** | IQVIA raised FY2026 revenue outlook to $17.28–17.48bn; MEDP backlog +2.9% YoY with cancellations in oncology/CV. Recovering but the demand signal is noisy — "more volatile in short-term demand signals than at any point since the pandemic-era boom" ([IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/cro-market-share-2026)) |
| **CRO / CDMO (China)** | **Growth, with a policy discontinuity** | WuXi AppTec H1 revenue +38.9%, guidance raised twice; simultaneously a named 1260H entity. Growth industry with a step-function regulatory risk attached |
| **Commercial-stage biotech** | **Growth** | Alnylam crossing $1bn/quarter product revenue for the first time; ARGX at 3.55% of IBB from nothing a few years ago |
| **China innovative biotech (18A cohort)** | **Emerging → early growth** | Licensing upfronts +400% since 2022; 90% of global ADC licensing; 12 HK biotech IPOs in 2026 ranking first globally. The *segment* is emerging; individual constituents remain pre-revenue |
| **US clinical-stage biotech** | **Emerging (perpetually), currently in capital-cycle shakeout** | Half of names below cash; 219 companies lost in four years; liquidation-as-a-service. This segment is in a *washout*, which historically is where forward returns are made — but only at the index level |

**The distinction that matters for the thesis:** the China-innovation theme is **secular** (a
permanent relocation of where molecules are discovered, evidenced by five years of accelerating
licensing data). The Western CRO recovery and the tools recovery are **cyclical** (destocking and
biotech-funding cycles). The BIOSECURE overhang on 2359 is neither — it is a **discrete political
event with a date attached**. Do not blend these three types of risk into one "biotech" view.

## 10. What would falsify these views

**On the China-innovation secular thesis (would falsify OVERWEIGHT on 18A index):**
- Cross-border licensing deal count or aggregate upfronts *declining* year-on-year in 2026 full-year
  data versus 2025's 93 deals / $5.6bn.
- Legislation or FAR rulemaking that extends BIOSECURE-style restrictions from *services* to
  *in-licensed assets*, i.e. penalising US pharma for licensing Chinese molecules. That would cut
  the exit route for the entire 18A cohort at a stroke. **This is the single biggest tail risk to
  the HK biotech thesis and it is not currently priced.**
- HK biotech IPO fundraising falling back materially from 2026's first-globally position.

**On the Western CRO OVERWEIGHT:**
- IQVIA cutting rather than raising FY guidance; MEDP backlog turning negative YoY or cancellation
  rates broadening beyond oncology/CV.
- BIOSECURE being narrowed, delayed past 2027, or WuXi winning its 1260H litigation — which removes
  the reshoring call option.
- Biotech funding rolling over again, which hits small-biotech R&D spend with a 2–3 quarter lag.

**On the 2359 NEUTRAL:**
- *Upside falsification:* OMB's December 2026 BCOC list omits WuXi AppTec, or the litigation
  succeeds. The overhang lifts and the operating record justifies a re-rate — I would be wrong to
  have been neutral.
- *Downside falsification:* FAR Council rulemaking completes and prohibitions bite, or major US
  customers pre-emptively dual-source. Watch for **any deceleration in the US revenue line
  (currently ~72% of total)** — customer attrition will show in revenue mix *before* it shows in
  the law. That is the leading indicator to monitor quarterly.

**On the sector-wide NEUTRAL on large pharma:**
- Section 232 tariffs being repealed, materially narrowed, or the 2029 escalation removed.
- Evidence that MFN agreements are less economically costly than assumed once actual price
  concessions are disclosed in FY2026 filings.
