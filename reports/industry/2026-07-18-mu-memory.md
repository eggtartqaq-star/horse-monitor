## 1. Industry snapshot

The memory industry is in the steepest upcycle on record — a shortage-driven price
super-cycle triggered by AI server and inference demand colliding with capacity that
had been reallocated toward HBM.

**Pricing and revenue (cited):**

- 1Q26 DRAM industry revenue reached **$97B, +81% QoQ**, with conventional DRAM
  contract prices up **~93–98% QoQ** ([TrendForce, 2026-06-01](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260601-13070.html)).
- 2Q26: conventional DRAM contracts **+58–63% QoQ**; NAND **+70–75% QoQ** — NAND
  outpacing DRAM for the first time this cycle on enterprise-SSD demand
  ([TrendForce, 2026-03-31](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260331-12995.html);
  [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/dram/dram-and-nand-contract-prices-to-climb-again-in-q2)).
- 3Q26 forecast: DRAM **+13–18% QoQ**, NAND **+10–15% QoQ** — still rising, but a
  marked deceleration as consumer OEMs hit an affordability limit
  ([Tom's Hardware, cycle-cooling report](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/ram/memory-price-surge-begins-to-cool-as-consumers-hit-affordability-limit-ai-demand-still-keeps-dram-and-nand-prices-climbing-through-q3-2026)).
- Cumulative DRAM pricing is on track for roughly **+275–300% from 2025 through
  2027**, ~3x the magnitude of the 2017–18 super-cycle, on ~3x the revenue base
  ([SemiAnalysis, "Memory Mania"](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/memory-mania-how-a-once-in-four-decades)).

**Demand mix:** North American CSPs are signing multi-quarter long-term agreements
(LTAs) to lock allocation through 2027; enterprise SSD is now the **largest NAND
application segment**, overtaking client SSD and smartphones
([TrendForce 2Q26](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260331-12995.html);
[NAND Research, May 2026](https://nand-research.com/memory-nand-flash-crisis-may-2026-update/)).

**Demand destruction (the cost of the spike):** IDC forecasts 2026 **PC units
−11.3%** and **smartphone units −12.9%** as OEMs cannot absorb memory cost inflation
([IDC](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/higher-asps-lower-unit-volumes-how-the-memory-crisis-is-reshaping-the-pc-and-smartphone-outlook/)).
Consumer end-markets are being rationed to feed the data center.

**The HBM wrinkle (important, underappreciated):** HBM contract prices *declined*
in 2026, and in 1Q26 HBM per-wafer revenue and profitability were **overtaken by
DDR5 64GB RDIMM** — conventional server DRAM became more profitable than HBM.
TrendForce expects suppliers to use this leverage to push **HBM contract prices up
multiple-fold in 2027** negotiations ([TrendForce, 2026-06-02](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20260602-13074.html)).
HBM wafer input at the big three is projected at **18% / 22% / 30%** of total DRAM
wafer input at end-2025 / 2026 / 2027 (bit share only 8% / 9% / 13%) — the crowding-out
of conventional DRAM intensifies into 2027 (same source).

## 2. Life-cycle stage & evidence

- **Memory overall: mature, deeply cyclical** — three-player DRAM oligopoly, demand
  growth historically GDP-plus, profits swinging with price. Evidence: the current
  P&L explosion is almost entirely *price*, not units (93–98% QoQ contract jumps;
  unit demand in PCs/phones is *shrinking*).
- **HBM / AI memory sub-segment: growth stage** — bit share of DRAM supply rising
  8%→13% (2025→2027), wafer share 18%→30%, new product generations (HBM4, HBM4E)
  every ~12–18 months, and customer prepayment/contract structures (LTAs, Micron's
  Strategic Capacity Agreements) that did not exist in prior cycles.
- **Cycle position: late-middle innings of the upswing.** The *rate of change* of
  pricing peaked in 1Q26 (+93–98%) and is decelerating (+58–63% → +13–18%). One
  cycle framework holds that industry profits do not peak before **Q4 2027**, with
  peak preconditions (trough inventory, peak YoY pricing, stock inflection) not yet
  met as of early 2026 ([Luminix DRAM cycle analysis](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/industry-analysis/dram-cycle-position-analysis-peak-timing-indicators)) —
  but memory equities historically peak **before** earnings do, and MU is already
  ~30% off its June high (see §5).

## 4. Emerging themes & inflection points

1. **HBM repricing in 2027** — the single most important negotiation of the year.
   HBM prices fell in 2026; TrendForce expects multi-fold increases in 2027 as
   suppliers arbitrage HBM vs DDR5 profitability. Winner: whoever has qualified
   HBM4/HBM4E supply when prices reset.
2. **Contract-structure regime change** — LTAs and Micron's 16 SCAs (> $100B
   committed value) convert spot-cyclical revenue into backlog. If honored through
   a downturn, this dampens the next trough; if cancellable, it is cosmetic. We do
   not yet know which.
3. **Consumer demand destruction** — PC −11.3%, phones −12.9% in 2026 (IDC). The
   elasticity buffer is gone; further price increases now come almost purely from
   data center willingness-to-pay, i.e., from AI capex.
4. **The 2027–2028 capacity wave** — Samsung P4/P5, SK Hynix M15X + 8–9x 1c ramp +
   Yongin, Micron ID1/ID2. If AI capex decelerates into that wave, the down-cycle
   will be violent (one scenario: DDR5 −50%+ within a few quarters
   ([bacloud outlook](https://www.bacloud.com/en/blog/230/when-will-ram-prices-drop-global-memory-market-outlook-20242026.html))).
5. **US onshoring / policy** — Micron Idaho + Virginia expansion; Korean capex
   subsidies (Samsung KRW 2T, SK Hynix KRW 1T applications). Regulatory tailwind for
   MU's domestic capacity premium.

## 6. Cyclical vs secular — what the long thesis rests on

**Be precise about this.** Roughly, MU's earnings explosion decomposes into:

- **Cyclical (the majority of current EPS):** a conventional DRAM/NAND price spike
  (+93–98% QoQ at peak) caused by a *shortage*, amplified by demand destruction in
  consumer units. This component mean-reverts — always has. ~86% gross margins are
  not a new steady state for commodity DRAM.
- **Secular (the durable part):** AI memory intensity — HBM bit share of DRAM
  supply rising 8%→13% by 2027 while consuming 30% of wafers; enterprise SSD as the
  largest NAND segment; contract structures (LTA/SCA, $100B+ at MU) that
  institutionalize customer prepayment; US onshoring.

**A long thesis in MU at today's price must rest on the secular story plus the
proposition that SCAs/LTAs make this cycle's trough structurally higher than past
troughs.** It cannot rest on the cyclical component — that is what the market
already paid for in H1 2026, and the rate-of-change of pricing has visibly peaked.

**What would falsify the thesis:**

1. Conventional DRAM contract prices go flat-to-negative QoQ before mid-2027
   (i.e., 3Q26's +13–18% fades faster than expected).
2. 2027 HBM price negotiations fail to deliver the expected step-up (would confirm
   HBM is commoditizing, not premiumizing).
3. Evidence of SCA/LTA cancellation, renegotiation, or customer inventory builds
   (watch CSP inventory days and Micron deferred revenue/prepayments).
4. AI accelerator capex deceleration (NVDA/CSP guidance) coinciding with the
   2027–28 fab wave (P5, Yongin, M15X full ramp, ID1).
5. Samsung HBM4 qualification wins at NVIDIA at MU's expense (watch share: MU
   holding ~20%+ HBM share is load-bearing).

## 7. Sector view

**Memory semis: OVERWEIGHT — confidence: moderate (60%), 12-month horizon, with a
hard review trigger.**

Rationale: pricing is still rising (3Q26 +13–18% DRAM), industry profit peak is
plausibly not until late 2027, HBM has a 2027 repricing catalyst, oligopoly pricing
power is at a historical maximum, and MU specifically has moved to #2 in HBM with a
sold-out book, $100B+ contracted backlog, node leadership at 1-gamma, and has
already de-rated ~30% from its June high. The remaining upside is the secular/HBM
leg, not the spot-price leg.

Constraints on the call: this is a **late-middle-cycle overweight, not a
buy-and-hold**. Memory equities top before earnings do. Downgrade to Neutral
immediately if falsifier (1) or (4) triggers; the position should be sized by Zac
and risk-checked by John accordingly.

**MU inside the sector:** best risk/reward of the three (vs Samsung's conglomerate
drag and SK Hynix's HBM crown already fully priced), by virtue of HBM share gains
from a low base, US policy tailwind, and the sharpest recent de-rating.
