1 · "Biotech" is five different asset classes

The single most useful thing in this document. A pre-revenue Chapter 18A name and a contract research organisation are not the same investment, are not exposed to the same risks, and do not belong in the same sentence — but both get called "biotech".

SegmentWhat kills youTime to find outCan it go to zero?
CRO / CDMOCustomer funding cycle; POLICY and sanctions; backlog cancellationQuartersVery unlikely
Large-cap pharmaPatent cliff; drug pricing policy; tariffsYearsEffectively no
Commercial-stage biotechProduct concentration; competitor entry; reimbursementQuarters–yearsUnlikely but possible
Clinical-stage / 18AONE trial readout; cash runwayA single morningYES, routinely
Tools / diagnosticsCustomer capex cycle; funding; ChinaQuartersVery unlikely

Only one row can go to zero on a single morning, and it is the row most beginners buy — because pre-revenue names are cheap per share and have the best stories.

2 · The number to read twice

BIO analysed 9,985 phase transitions across 7,455 development programmes at 1,103 companies and found a cumulative approval rate from Phase I of 9.6%. Wong, Siah & Lo put it at 13.8% overall, with recent Phase I likelihood of approval as low as 6.7%. Phase II is the killing field — roughly a third survive it.

Buy one pre-revenue biotech and roughly nine times in ten the lead asset never becomes an approved drug. Selection helps but does not rescue it: rare-disease non-oncology programmes reach ~25%, biomarker-guided ~26%. That is still one in four.

The market confirms the arithmetic. At mid-2026 nearly half of publicly traded US biotechs traded below the cash on their own balance sheets, and 219 companies have been lost from the sector in four years.

This is why the index route is a mathematical argument rather than a stylistic preference. 157 shots on goal captures the ~10% that work. Three hand-picked names plausibly captures zero.

6 · Where 2359.HK sits — and the one thing that decides it

WuXi AppTec is a CRDMO, not a drug developer. Contracted revenue, no binary trial risk. Operationally it is performing exceptionally: H1 2026 revenue RMB28.90bn, +38.9% YoY, gross margin +9.4pp, backlog +25.2%, full-year guidance raised to RMB58.5–60.5bn. It has also actually divested its US/UK cell-therapy unit to Altaris and has a Delaware site starting up in Q4 2026 — action, not announcement. In the first half of 2026 it out-earned Lonza for the first time (US$4.28bn vs US$4.17bn): it is now the largest firm in its category, not the challenger.

Year to date the H line is +97% against the A line's +73.5% (7 Aug basis). The 52-week low was HK$96.70 — the stock has roughly doubled.

The Hong Kong line is the expensive one. WuXi is one of a small set of Chinese "hard tech" names where the usual A-share premium has inverted: the H shares traded at a ~5% premium to the A shares on 10 August (~10% on 4 August, >4% back in April). Buying 2359.HK rather than 603259.SS means paying up. The honest reasons to do it anyway are account access, Stock Connect flow and index membership — not value.

Two number traps in the public coverage. Vendor market-cap fields for this dual-listed name span HK$440bn to HK$609bn — a 35% spread — because some count the H float only; the ~HK$600bn figure is the one consistent with the current price and the share count. And several English summaries rendered 288.97億元 as "RMB 289bn": the correct first-half revenue is RMB28.897bn, ten times smaller.

But read what the growth actually is. Chemistry is ~86.5% of revenue and grew +53.3%; Biology grew +11.2%, roughly flat in real terms. Inside Chemistry, TIDES — oligonucleotides and peptides — is RMB7.26bn, +44.3%, and WuXi is reported to hold about a quarter of the global clinical-stage GLP-1 market across 24 programmes. Three concentrations sit on top of each other: one segment, one drug class, one country of customer. That is a diversified-CRDMO label on a GLP-1 manufacturing business, and Chinese CDMOs are adding peptide capacity fast.

The policy timeline, stated precisely

Most coverage blurs "introduced", "passed", "enacted" and "in force". Those distinctions decide the whole case.

DateWhat actually happened
2023–24BIOSECURE introduced, naming WuXi AppTec and WuXi Biologics in the bill text
Sep 2024Passed the House only — then died with the 118th Congress
9 Oct 2025Re-introduced; Senate passed it as an FY2026 NDAA amendment
18 Dec 2025ENACTED. But the enacted text does not name WuXi — it defines a "biotechnology company of concern" by reference to lists, and WuXi was on none of them. A genuine reprieve.
8 Jun 2026Reprieve ends — Dept of War adds WuXi AppTec to the Section 1260H list of Chinese military companies
7 Aug 2026Chief Judge Boasberg (D.D.C.) preliminarily ENJOINS the designation, finding it likely arbitrary and capricious and that officials "repeatedly misread the evidence." Case 1:26-cv-02069. The court then refused the Pentagon a stay pending appeal and set WuXi's bond at US$1 — an appeal is therefore signalled, though no notice of appeal was verified.
18 Dec 2026OMB's initial list is due. Four months out, and the nearest real event.
~late 2028Earliest date prohibitions actually bite: OMB guidance +180 days → FAR rule +1 year → +60 days

Three facts that cut in WuXi's favour, and are widely missed. The scope is federal procurement, grant and loan nexus only — private pharma with no federal contract is not barred from using WuXi. Grandfathering runs five years, and the carve-out that would deny it applies only to firms listed as of 18 Dec 2025 — WuXi was added later, so its existing contracts qualify. And nothing is in force today.

Anyone calling this "uninvestable China risk" has not read the statute.

And the fact that decides it anyway. Boasberg's own irreparable-harm finding records that customers had already cancelled contracts and moved business to competitors within eight weeks of the listing — before any prohibition was operative.

The market punishes the label, not the effective date. That is why "nothing bites until 2028" is false comfort. Aggregate growth of +38.9% will mask new-award diversion for several reporting periods before it shows in the headline.

And the half-year the market just bought cannot contain it. The designation was live for roughly three weeks of a six-month reporting period (8–30 June). The cancellations a federal judge found as fact land in second-half 2026. Watch the RMB66.43bn backlog rather than revenue — cancelled work leaves the backlog first. Named customers and contract values were not disclosed.

Roughly 60–68% of revenue is US — the range is honest; the sources do not reconcile to a point estimate. Growth engine and political risk are the same jurisdiction, so the company cannot hedge it internally. Verdict: LIVE, not resolved. The injunction is interim and appealable, and policy pressure is still increasing.

Read-across — where the same risk sits without the same protection

WuXi Biologics (2269.HK) and WuXi XDC (2268.HK) are NOT on the 1260H list — but both were named in a December 2025 congressional letter. Same targeting, none of the legal protection WuXi AppTec has just won. The next 1260H cycle is June 2027 and is not priced.

Capacity beneficiaries if Chinese CROs are restricted: Samsung Biologics, Lonza, Charles River (CRL), Fujifilm Diosynth — all capacity-constrained with two-to-five-year lead times.

On timing, honestly: on the injunction we are behind the market, not ahead of it. The stock re-rated 25% in six sessions to an all-time high, and the consensus average 12-month target of HK$183.46 now sits below spot — any screen reading "trades below target" is broken on this name today. Whatever edge remains sits in the OMB list on 18 December 2026, the appellate ruling, and the June 2027 1260H cycle for 2269 and 2268 — none of which appears priced.
