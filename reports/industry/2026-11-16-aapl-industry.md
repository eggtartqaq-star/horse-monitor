*Note: sources describe FQ4 2026 as "Ternus's first call" as CEO. I did not independently verify the CEO transition date; it is reported, not checked (§11). One source gives prior-year GM as 47.2%; the 47.9% headline is consistent across relays. The FQ4 total GM was flattered by tariff refunds — the underlying trend is lower than the headline.*

**Answer to the brief's question: both, in sequence.** (1) **Apple has passed part of it on** — management named price increases on all three hardware lines; Counterpoint expected ~$200 on the Pro Max against ~$300 of BOM. (2) **Margin has absorbed the rest**: product GM −2.9pp QoQ headline (~1pp underlying, memory-driven), and the December-quarter guide of **45–46%** is below the 47.9% just printed — **a guided step-down of roughly 2–3 points, explicitly attributed to memory**. (3) **The cushion is temporary** — lower-cost carry-in inventory is being consumed and Apple reprices memory quarterly, so FQ1–FQ2 2027 are where the full contract-price step-up lands. Apple is a **price-maker in its own market and a price-taker in memory**; it is exercising the first to partly offset the second.

**The two-position link (Owner).** The mechanism is direct: SK hynix's 80% operating margin and Apple's ~2–3pt guided GM step-down are the **same dollars on opposite sides of a contract renegotiated quarterly**. Hedging logic follows — the technical desk measured SKHY↔AAPL correlation at **−0.21** over SKHY's listing life (`reports/technical/2026-11-16-skhy-live-kline-and-aapl-switch.md`). **The industry data say that negative correlation has a fundamental cause: the memory price.** If memory prices roll over (my 11 Nov checklist: NAND flat in October; mobile DRAM at plateau), the transfer reverses — SKHY loses, Apple's GM recovers. **The two positions are a partial natural hedge on the memory cycle; they are not two independent bets.** (Industry inference, mine.)

## 3. AI devices — upgrade cycle in the data, or narrative?

| Evidence | Finding | Date | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global replacement cycle | **Extending toward ~4 years**, expected to continue through 2026–27, driven by higher memory/component costs raising device prices (Counterpoint) | Aug 2026 | A | [Counterpoint report page](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/reports/memory-crisis-pushes-global-smartphone%20replacement-cycles-toward-4-years), [telecomlead](https://telecomlead.com/?p=127956) |
| Installed base 2025 | **+2%**, driven by replacement cycles nearing four years and more second-life use (Counterpoint) | early 2026 | A/B | [telecomlead](https://telecomlead.com/smart-phone/global-smartphone-installed-base-grows-2-in-2025-as-apple-and-samsung-extend-lead-124551) |
| Upgrade intent | "**49% wait 3+ years**" headline | 2026 | C — survey origin not traced | [webpronews](https://www.webpronews.com/smartphone-users-delay-upgrades-49-wait-3-years-to-2028) |
| Apple 2026 iPhone outlook | **"ASP-led as memory constraints cap shipments"** (Counterpoint report title) | 2026 | A (title only) | relayed, [telecomlead](https://telecomlead.com/?p=128354) |
| Apple units | Q3 2026 iPhone units **flat (Omdia) / −4% (IDC)** | Oct 2026 | A | §1 |
| Apple-specific AI cost | Dec-qtr GM guide includes **"Siri AI ramp expenses"** | Oct 2026 | A via B | §2.2 |
| AI features → upgrade demand | **No dataset found linking AI features to upgrade rates** | — | — | NOT OBTAINED |

**Verdict: narrative, not data — and the data point the other way.** An AI-driven upgrade cycle would show as **shortening** replacement cycles and **rising** units. 2026 shows replacement cycles **lengthening toward four years** and **iPhone units flat to down** in Q3, with Apple's growth described by Counterpoint as **ASP-led**. Apple's FQ4 revenue +12% is consistent with price/mix, not a volume supercycle. Meanwhile AI shows up on Apple's P&L as a **cost** (Siri ramp in the GM guide) before it shows up as revenue. **I cannot reject the hypothesis that AI is supporting the *mix* shift to Pro models** — the data obtained cannot separate AI pull from memory-driven low-end disappearance — but **there is no evidence of a unit upgrade cycle.** Hype discipline: the burden of proof is on the cycle, and it has not been met.

**Inflection to watch:** the installed base is ~1 in 4 active smartphones and ageing toward 4-year cycles. That is a **deferred** replacement pool, not a lost one. If memory prices roll over in 2027 and device prices stabilise, the deferred pool is the upside — but that is a memory-cycle call, not an AI call.
