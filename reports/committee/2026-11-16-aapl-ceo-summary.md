## Investment case in five sentences

1. Apple is a **high-quality business at a demanding price**: financials A− (78), wide moat (76),
   management B (72), but fair value $185–245 (central $210) against a $356 price.
2. The price implies a long-run return of **about 3–6% a year**, roughly a percentage point over
   Treasuries (themselves unverified at 4.9–5.15%). That is acceptable only if Apple's earnings are
   nearly as safe as a bond.
3. FY2026 looks like a **cyclical peak**: iPhone +21%, Greater China +28%, a one-off tariff refund.
   Meanwhile the memory costs that help SK hynix are squeezing Apple's margin (guide 45–46%).
4. **The moat is narrowing at the toll, not at the device.** iPhone loyalty holds at ~90%. But the
   App Store commission and Google's search payment (~44% of gross profit in Q4) are under legal
   attack, with a Supreme Court argument on 11 Jan 2027 (single source).
5. At 25% volatility a stop genuinely limits loss, so the CRO **approves holding with a hard stop
   and a size cap**, while valuation recommends a trim and bars adding.

## Biggest risk

**A de-rating, not a collapse.** The cash flows support about $210–245. If the market stops
treating Apple as a near-bond, nothing in the business has to break for the price to fall 30%+.
The catalysts for that are dated: the 11 Jan Supreme Court argument, late-January results
(margin vs the 45–46% guide), and the Google appeal.

## Does this merit a committee?

**Yes — convene `/committee AAPL`.** The Owner holds an unreviewed position at the all-time high,
two departments reach different actions, and the audit found every desk at or below "fully
priced" with nobody tasked to argue the bull case. The committee should hear a steel-man of the
low-beta case before it votes. **Rule 4:** once the Owner decides, 腦大裝草 records it; there is no
