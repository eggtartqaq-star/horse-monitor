## 2. FLAG A1 (CRITICAL) — FY2025 was a net **LOSS**, not a profit, and the package never caught it

**巴爺爺 states, marked [PR]:** *"Net income/(loss) attributable to common: … FY2025 **+$6.0m** … FY2025's $6.0m of net income on $2,021m of revenue is a 0.3% net margin … Bloom crossed breakeven in 2025."*

**The filed figure is a net loss of $(87,140) thousand for FY2025.** Two independent lines of evidence:

1. Direct search-index extraction of the FY2025 10-K: *"Bloom Energy reported a net loss of $87,140 thousand ($87.1 million) for FY2025."*
2. **菲比斯's own accumulated-deficit derivation, inside this same package**: $(3,986.98)m − $(3,897.62)m = **$(89.36)m implied FY2025 loss**, which he printed and captioned *"in the 'record' year."* That derivation agrees with the filed loss to within $2.3m.

**Where the $6.0m came from.** It is a **trailing-twelve-month** figure, not FY2025. 菲比斯's own table gives it away: *"TTM GAAP net income (implied by 10,135x trailing P/E on $61.1B cap) ≈ $6M."* Reconstructing: FY25 $(87.1)m − Q1'25 loss + Q1'26 $70.7m ≈ +$6m implies a Q1'25 loss of ~$(22)m — internally coherent. **巴爺爺 took a TTM number reverse-engineered from a stockanalysis.com P/E ratio, relabelled it FY2025, and stamped it [PR].** That is the textbook hallucination pattern: a derived third-party figure promoted to primary-source status.

**This is the most serious finding in the audit, and it is serious because of the escape.** The package contained the correct answer, in a second report, on the same day. **𢦀鳩仔 read both reports and reconciled neither.** 菲比斯 printed a $(89.4)m loss and a $6M profit **six rows apart in one table** and did not flag the contradiction. John repeated the framing. Four analysts, one document set, zero cross-checks on the single most-quoted P&L line.

**Consequences that must be corrected before committee:**

- **"GAAP profitability has arrived" is false for FY2025.** It is true for Q1 2026 only, exactly one quarter.

## 3. Ruling on the FY2025 operating-cash-flow dispute

**Ruling: the dispute is resolved in the company's favour *on the reported GAAP line*, and simultaneously the underlying quality concern gets worse, not better.**

**(a) $418.1m is confirmed.** *"Bloom Energy generated $418.1 million of cash flow from operating activities for the full year 2025"* — company press release, 5 Feb 2026, corroborated by search-index extraction. This is the audited GAAP line. 巴爺爺 was right to mark it [PR].

**(b) Sheephill's $114m is not a competing measurement of the same line, and its stated composition is internally incoherent.** Sheephill: *"operating cash flow of $114m in FY2025, almost entirely composed of non-cash add-backs: $139m in stock-based compensation and $111m in debt-related charges."* Test that arithmetic against the corrected net loss:

```
Net loss (filed)                 $ (87.1)m
+ SBC (Sheephill's own figure)   $ 139.0m
+ debt-related charges (theirs)  $ 111.0m
                                 ---------
Subtotal before D&A and WC       $ 162.9m   ← already exceeds their own $114m OCF
```

For OCF to be $114m with those add-backs, working capital plus D&A must net to **roughly −$49m or worse** — i.e. a working-capital **drag**. That directly contradicts their characterisation "almost entirely composed of non-cash add-backs," and it contradicts the desk's own hypothesis that the gap is a customer-deposit **inflow**. **Sheephill's construct is not reconcilable as stated.** Two candidate explanations, neither verifiable at 403: (i) it is **FY2024** OCF — note 巴爺爺 lists FY2024 as *"positive (first positive year) — amount unverified"*, and $114m would fit; (ii) it is an adjusted, ex-working-capital measure presented without saying so.

**(c) The debt-related-charge detail is the one thing Sheephill gets *usefully* right, and it corroborates my A1 finding.** FY2025 operating income of **+$72.8m** against a net loss of **$(87.1)m** requires ~$160m of below-the-line charges. A ~$111m loss on extinguishment — consistent with retirement of the 2028 Green Converts, which 巴爺爺 independently *inferred* from the debt stack — explains most of it. **Three independent threads (accumulated deficit, the filed loss, the operating-income-to-net-loss bridge) converge on the same answer.**

**(d) What remains genuinely unresolved.** The composition of the $505m earnings-to-cash gap; FY2025 and Q1'26 capex; AR and inventory. 巴爺爺's qualitative judgement — *"working-capital-funded operating cash flow is not durable cash flow"* — stands, and now applies to a **larger** gap.

**Impact on the valuation:** 𢦀鳩仔's assumption V (worth **<$1/share**) is correct and I endorse his instruction to the committee not to over-weight this line. **The OCF dispute was never the important question. A1 is.**

## 4. Ruling on the related-party reconciliation (the package's most consequential claim)

**𢦀鳩仔's claim (§2.2):** Q3'25 $288.0m + Q4'25 $574.2m = $862.2m, matching Brookfield's FY25 $862.1m to $0.1m; therefore the $288m in Q3'25 is **Brookfield Fund-JV revenue, not SK ecoplant**, and residual related-party revenue (SK and others) is only **$29.9m = 1.5% of FY25 revenue**.

### **RULING: UPHELD on substance. FLAGGED on epistemics.**

**Substance — the inference is logically forced, not coincidental.** Given (i) Brookfield FY25 = $862.1m and (ii) the JVs did not exist before August 2025, Brookfield's entire FY25 revenue must fall in Q3+Q4. Total Q3+Q4 related-party revenue is $862.2m. Therefore SK ecoplant's H2'25 related-party revenue is **$0.1m**. Any material SK contribution in Q3 would force Brookfield's Q4 above the $574.2m Q4 total — arithmetically impossible. This is a **constraint, not a coincidence**, and 𢦀鳩仔's "exact match to rounding" framing understates how tight the logic actually is.

**Independent corroboration I obtained, which the package did not have.** The Q3 2025 source itself explains the mechanism: *"As part of the contracts with Brookfield, Bloom Energy made equity investments into joint venture vehicles, and because of those equity investments, those JVs became a related party to Bloom Energy Corporation. This is why these revenues must be disclosed as related party transactions."* **The counterparty reassignment is confirmed from the source, not merely inferred.**

**Third confirmation, from the 10-K's customer-concentration footnote.** The FY2025 10-K discloses three customers/distributors at approximately **43%, 13% and 12%**, *"the first of which is related party."* $862.1m ÷ $2,021m = **42.66% ≈ 43%.** The 43% concentration customer is the **Brookfield JV group**, not SK. A third-party summary of that footnote mis-attributes the 43% to SK ecoplant — **the same mis-attribution 巴爺爺's source made.** The error is in the ecosystem, and 𢦀鳩仔 is the only analyst in the firm who caught it.

**Credit where due: this is the best single piece of analysis in the package**, and it is the kind of finding that justifies the existence of a valuation desk that reads other analysts' footnotes.

**Now the epistemics, which fail.**

- **FLAG B1 (HIGH) — unverified input promoted to load-bearing fact.** 巴爺爺 wrote, in bold: *"I could not open the primary filing and this figure is UNVERIFIED."* 𢦀鳩仔 used it as one of two inputs to a reconciliation, obtained a clean result, and wrote *"The conclusion:"* — with no re-verification attempt and no residual uncertainty language. **A clean reconciliation between two numbers, one of which is unverified, verifies nothing on its own; it only shows the two are mutually consistent.** He got the right answer by luck of source quality, not by method. Had Panabee been wrong on the amount, three reports would now carry a fabricated structural finding stated as settled.
- **FLAG B2 (MEDIUM) — selective source trust.** He kept Panabee's **number** while discarding Panabee's **attribution**. If a source is wrong about who the counterparty is, its reliability on the amount requires an argument. None is given.
- **FLAG B3 (MEDIUM) — a factual error inside the correct conclusion.** He writes the Brookfield series as *"$2.8m (Q1'25) → $288m (Q3'25) → $574.2m (Q4'25) → $373.3m (Q1'26)."* **The Brookfield JVs did not exist in Q1 2025** — by his own sentence two lines earlier. The $2.8m Q1'25 figure is *not* Brookfield; it is almost certainly the SK/other residual. The series is presented to dramatise Brookfield's ramp and it silently mis-labels its own first term. The ramp is real ($0 → $288m → $574.2m); the presentation is not.
- **FLAG B4 (MEDIUM) — an unexamined implication that cuts *against* the bear.** If SK ecoplant is only ~$29.9m of FY2025 revenue, then SK's **500MW / ~$1.5bn commitment through 2027** is running dramatically behind schedule. That is a *demand* datapoint of real significance — either a large unrecognised backlog or a stalled partnership — and **no report in the package mentions it.** 𢦀鳩仔 pursued the bearish implication of his own finding (financing-vehicle concentration) and left the ambiguous one untouched.

**Net effect on the committee's read:** the finding stands, and it materially rewrites 巴爺爺's red flag #1. **巴爺爺's stated downgrade trigger — "confirmation that related-party revenue exceeds 50% of a quarter" — is now confirmed three times over (55.5% Q3'25, 73.8% Q4'25, ~50% Q1'26) for a counterparty that is a co-owned financing vehicle.** By his own scoring band that takes the financial score to **≤45**. He scored 67. **FLAG A5 (HIGH): 巴爺爺's score is inconsistent with his own published trigger, on facts already in the file.**

**Why it nonetheless fails the independence test — four findings:**

**F1 — The four reports are serially dependent on one analyst's judgement, and the committee will read that as four confirmations.** 菲比斯's estimates are load-bearing in all four documents:
- his **30/45/25 scenario weights** are adopted verbatim by 𢦀鳩仔 (*"I did not adjust them"*) — so the probability weighting that produces $28.70 is **not the valuation desk's own view**;
- his **erosion call** sets 𢦀鳩仔's terminal margins;
- his **10–12% WACC estimate** sets the discount rate;
- his **60% MoS / 1.5% NAV cap** sets both the pass/fail test in §8 and John's position ceiling;
- his **implied-expectations arithmetic** *is* John's valuation gate.

菲比斯 himself labels the WACC, the implied-expectations revenue, and the scenario probabilities as **his estimates**. **One analyst's estimates, propagated through three downstream reports, arrive at committee wearing four signatures.** That is not corroboration; it is amplification. It is the same structural failure the desk correctly identified *in Bloom* — a single source counted multiple times — and the desk did not notice it in itself.

**F2 — The one explanation most likely to be true was acknowledged and then routed to a department that does not exist.** §7.4: *"The market may simply not be valuing BE on cash flows at all … **This is very likely true**, and it is not a valuation argument — it is a positioning argument. It belongs to 老詹 and Tim Cook, not to me."* 老詹 and Tim Cook **have filed nothing**. 𢦀鳩仔 is correct that it is outside his mandate. But the consequence is that the package's own stated most-likely explanation for a 7.5× gap is **conceded and never tested by anyone**, and the 8/100 does not carry that caveat into the scorecard. **A score of 8 asserts near-certainty. A file with an untested, self-identified "very likely true" alternative hypothesis cannot support near-certainty.** This is the single strongest bias finding in the audit — and note it is a *package* failure, not a personal one.

**F3 — Asymmetric skepticism, applied consistently in one direction.** The desk discounts BE's "up to X GW" options, BE's related-party revenue, BE's non-GAAP guidance, BE's $25bn Brookfield framework, BE's ~34% GM guide, and BE's $100m/GW capex claim. Each individually is defensible. But the *same* skepticism is not applied to the comparables that make BE look expensive: **GEV's 116 GW is accepted at face value** despite being 54% slot reservations, and GEV's 4.8× EV/Sales is used as the anchor that makes BE's 18.4× look absurd — while 𢦀鳩仔 simultaneously notes GEV trades at ~89× EV/EBITDA, which should have prompted the question of whether **GEV is the cheap one or BE is the expensive one**. He raises the point only to *discard the comp method when it yields a higher value* (~$52–85). The reasoning for discarding it ("importing GEV's optimism as though it were evidence") is sound — but it is deployed only against the generous answer, and he never asks the symmetric question: **does his DCF import 菲比斯's pessimism as though it were evidence?** Given F1, it does.

**F4 — Score saturation and mandate creep.** An 8/100 leaves essentially no room below. If BE at $215 is an 8, the scale cannot distinguish BE at $400. More importantly, 𢦀鳩仔 instructs the committee on how to weight his own score: *"The committee should not average 67, 68 and 8 into a '48, hold.' The valuation score is a **gate**, not a weight."* He is **substantively right** — and it is not his call. A component analyst prescribing the aggregation rule that maximises his own component's influence is advocacy, however correct. **Route the observation to the CEO as a scorecard-design question, not as a line in the valuation report.**

### Steel-manning the bull myself — did the package give it a fair hearing?

**No, on three specific arguments the file never engages:**

1. **The benign reading of the Brookfield JV is never tested.** The package converges on *"a financing structure being reported as demand."* The equally available reading: Brookfield JVs are **how a data-centre developer buys 1 GW without a balance sheet** — the customer's financing constraint solved, which is the *bull's* central claim. Bloom's stakes are **9.9% and 15%** (verified) — small sponsor-alignment stakes, not control, and consistent with ordinary project finance. The **$19.6m equity loss on a $24.6m stake** is cited three times as the smoking gun; but early-stage project vehicles carrying development cost and depreciation ahead of commercial operation **lose money by construction**, and an 80%-of-stake loss on a partial period is what a pre-COD SPV looks like. **Nobody in the package states the benign explanation and rejects it with evidence. It is simply not raised.** That is confirmation bias in its purest form: the alternative hypothesis was never written down.
2. **The bear case may under-value the contracted service annuity.** Both 菲比斯 and 𢦀鳩仔 call the ~$14bn service backlog at 20% GM *"the floor."* The bear case then values the entire operating business at **~0.25× terminal sales (~$0.8bn)**. A contracted, long-dated, ~$2.8bn-gross-profit annuity on a proprietary installed base is difficult to reconcile with $0.8bn of operating-business value. The bear's terminal value and the bear's own stated floor are in tension, and the tension is not addressed.
3. **The scarcity window may be longer than 4–5 years.** The package's central structural claim is that turbine slots free up 2029–30. My own verification cuts the other way: **GEV raised its outlook to ≥125 GW under contract by end-2026** and added 20 GW of gas contracts in a single quarter while shipping only 3 GW. Backlog is growing ~7× faster than it is being consumed. That is evidence the bottleneck is **lengthening, not clearing** — and it appeared in the same 22 July release the package cites for the 116 GW figure. **The desk cited the source and took only the half that supported the erosion call.** This is the cleanest documented instance of confirmation bias in the file.

**None of these would move the fair value to $215.** I want to be explicit: I am not arguing the bull is right. Correcting the SBC double-count (C1), the erosion triple-count (C2) and the annuity floor plausibly moves the central FV from $28.70 into the **$35–45** region — still a ~80% gap. **𢦀鳩仔's directional conclusion survives every criticism in this audit.** The problem is not the answer. **The problem is that a score of 8/100 claims a precision the file cannot support, produced by a method whose key inputs came from one colleague, in a package where the most likely competing explanation was conceded and never tested.**

### Groupthink check — does dissent exist anywhere?

**Barely, and only inside 𢦀鳩仔's report.** His §6.4 relative-value ceiling (~$85), his §7 four-ways-I-could-be-wrong, and his refusal to recommend a short are the only structured dissent in 300+ pages. 修大哥 at 68/100 is the most constructive voice and is quoted **only** where he supports caution (*"I would not treat a 68 as actionable"*); his constructive case is not carried into any downstream report. The dictator finds a **net regulatory tailwind** — the single clearly positive departmental verdict in the file — and it appears in John's review as one bullet under "what earns it points above zero" and **nowhere in the valuation at all**. **The two most constructive inputs in the package have near-zero weight in the two reports that drive the committee's decision.** That is the signature of a house view.

**Bias verdict: NARRATIVE LOCK-IN — CONFIRMED AT PACKAGE LEVEL, NOT AT ANALYST LEVEL.** 𢦀鳩仔 personally did more anti-anchoring work than any analyst in the firm's short history, and his §7 is the model I want others to copy. But he did it inside a file whose inputs all trace to one moat report, and the desk's fourth consecutive negative price verdict was reached without a single independent estimate of the discount rate, the scenario weights, or the terminal margin. **The bear framing has not been falsified; it has merely not been tested.**
