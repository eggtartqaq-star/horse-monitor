## 0. Verdict

1. **Engine 2 has a modest per-trade edge that is robust to parameter changes**, measured on the
   specification the Owner validated (20-day-breakout entry).
2. **The scanner the Owner trades from does not implement that specification.** In
   `daily_strong_signals.py` and `dual_engine_v6_4.run_scan()`, Engine 2 has **no breakout gate**.
   The manual (§三, condition 3) and every backtest require one.
3. **As actually traded** (2 slots, 10% notional cap, whole shares, next-open fills, 10bp/side), the
   account makes about **2%/yr on the backtest path** (+US$116 over four years). On **the path the
   scanner actually produces** it makes about **0.8%/yr** (+US$42), and is **negative in 2025–26**.
4. Neither result is statistically distinguishable from zero.
5. **The 10% cap probably costs money in 2025–26, but that is not proven.** It is not the worst
   setting in the earlier half on the backtest path.

## 3. Robustness — every locked parameter ±20%, one at a time (`per_trade`, backtest path)

- **E2 holds up.** Net EV stays positive in every perturbation, in both halves (+0.61% to +2.13%).
  It is not a sharp peak.
- **E1 has no robust edge to protect.** It hovers around zero OOS (−0.06% to +0.90%).
- **One observation, not adopted:** a 3.6×ATR trail improves E2 in both halves. Choosing it after
  seeing data that includes the OOS period would break the Owner's rule 5. It is recorded as a
  hypothesis for an untouched sample.

**Benchmarks, CAGR all / IS / OOS:**
- SPY: 17.0% / 20.9% / 13.0%
- Equal-weight 38 names: 34.1% / 55.9% / 22.5%

At most about 20% of the account is ever deployed, so the account return is small whatever the
edge. Inside the portfolio, **E2 loses money on both paths and E1 makes it**: the reverse of the
per-trade picture.

The cause is **not established**. The cap is one candidate. But in the fractional-share run (`M1`),
where price does not restrict which names can be bought, E2 still nets only +0.02% OOS. Slot
competition, and which signals win a slot, may matter as much.

## 5. The 10% notional cap — probably costly recently, not proven

**Cap curve, 2 slots** (CAGR all / IS / OOS · max DD):

| Cap | Backtest path | Scanner path |
|---|---|---|
| **10% (V6.4.2)** | 2.21 / 1.82 / 2.62 · −4.5% | **0.81 / 2.29 / −0.82** · −4.9% |
| 15% | 3.90 / 2.07 / 4.88 · −5.9% | 3.16 / 2.43 / 4.08 · −3.5% |

- **The curve is not monotonic, and the IS half does not support a change on the backtest path**
  (10% beats 20% and no cap there).
- **Tom's path test** (48 perturbations of start date and slot tie-breaking, backtest path): 15%
  beats 10% in **58%** of IS runs and **100%** of OOS runs. The direction is real for 2025–26 and weak before.
- **On the scanner path, 10% is the lowest at 2 slots in both halves.** That rests on 41–55 trades
  per half and was not path-tested.
- **The price-band table in the first version is withdrawn.** It used split-adjusted prices.
  On real prices, E2's ≤$128 band is +0.25% IS / +0.08% OOS per trade (Tom).
- **The cap's purpose:** it limits gap-through-stop loss (a 30% gap costs ~3% of the account at a
  10% position, ~4.5% at 15%). It is a risk limit, not a signal parameter. **The CEO does not
  change it.** John's ruling is in `reports/risk/2026-11-24-swing-notional-cap-cro.md`.
- **The firm recommended the 10% cap in July without pricing it.** The Owner's own notes already
  call a share-price ceiling anti-momentum. Whether the cap rebuilt that ceiling is plausible, not proven.

## 6. Other findings

1. **Live/backtest divergence (Tom F-3, HIGH, confirmed in source).** Engine 2 in
   `daily_strong_signals.py` (lines 124–137) and `dual_engine_v6_4.run_scan()` checks score ≥0.65
   and stop ≤12% but **never requires today's high ≥ the 20-day high**.
   - The manual (§三, condition 3) requires the breakout, and so do all backtests (`run_trend_backtest`,
     `oos_validation`, `momentum_pit`).
   - So the "OOS-graduated" engine is not the one being traded.
   - Fix: add `and row["High"] >= row["high_20d"]` to the Engine 2 condition in both scanners. This
     restores the documented, validated rule; it is not an optimisation. The scanner path's E2 is
     better IS (+2.00%) and worse OOS (+0.17%), so this data does not say which version is better.
2. **Costs decide everything at this size.** A US$1 minimum per side (`M2`) turns the account
   negative (−3.2% CAGR on the fixed lab). The manual says Webull is zero-commission. **Check one real
   contract note** for platform, SEC/TAF and FX fees.
3. **Rejected upgrades** (fail "better in both halves" against the fixed baseline 1.82% / 2.62%):
   - **A1, Engine 2 only:** 0.25% / −0.28%.
   - **A2, momentum top-half filter:** 1.37% / 2.30%.
4. **The momentum-pool +0.770% is a few big winners** (the Owner's `momentum_pit_trades.csv`,
   1,167 trades):
   - NVDA, TSLA, GE, MSFT and AMD are **58% of all profit**. Without the top 10 tickers, EV is +0.17%.
   - The largest 5% of trades contribute 164% of the total.
   - It held a median of 11 concurrent positions (max 39); the account holds 2.
5. **E1 behaves like a "10-day hold".** Only 4% of E1 trades reach the 2R target; 64% exit at the
   10-day limit (average +2.9%). The target mostly works as an entry filter.
6. **20 paper trades cannot decide an engine.** A 20-trade mean has a 90% CI of about ±2.6 (E1) to
   ±3.3 (E2) points. The 20-trade plan tests **discipline**, not edge.
7. **Version drift.**
   - Scanner screenshots from 6 and 28 Sep show positions above the V6.4.2 cap (CVX US$209;
     PFE 8 shares = US$229), so pre-V6.4.2 code was running.
   - The Pine indicator is Engine 1 only, labelled V6.4.1, and was used on a name outside the universe (CRWV).
8. **Indicator fragility (Tom).**
   - A one-bar ADX alignment slip changes ~7% of signal days and flips OOS CAGR (+2.6% → −0.6%).
   - `parity_check.py` must pass at ≥99% on the Owner's PC before any V6.6 number is trusted.
     Tom considers its current check too lax; tightening it is open.
9. **Fixed defects:**
   - The V6.5 E1 target-order port defect.
   - Split-adjusted sizing (WMT, NVDA, AVGO, NFLX, and MSTR added by Tom).
   - The slot bug (Tom).
   - End-of-data handling (Tom).
