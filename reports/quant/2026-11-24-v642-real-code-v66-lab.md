## 0. Verdict

**The per-trade edge of Engine 2 looks real and robust. As the account is actually traded, almost
none of it reaches the account.** Over four years, V6.4.2 as executed (2 slots, 10% notional cap,
whole shares, next-open fills, 10bp/side) makes **+US$50 on US$1,282 (~1%/yr)**, with a confidence
interval that includes zero. The single largest cause is the 10% notional cap, which the firm's
own CRO recommended in July (CL-3 / D-03).

## 3. Robustness — every locked parameter ±20%, one at a time (`per_trade`)

E2 net EV stays positive in **every** perturbation, in both halves (range +0.61% to +2.13%). It is
not a sharp peak. E1 hovers around zero OOS (−0.06% to +0.90%), so there is no robust E1 edge to protect.

Observation, **not adopted**: a 20% wider trail (3.6×ATR) improves E2 in both halves (IS +2.13%,

Owner's rule 5 forbids. It is recorded as a hypothesis for a future, untouched sample.

## 5. The main finding — the 10% notional cap starves the trend engine

The cap (US$128.21 per position) and whole shares mean E2 can only buy stocks priced under ~$128.
With next-open fills and before costs or any cap, E2's best price band is exactly the one the cap removes:

| E2 entry price | IS EV/trade | OOS EV/trade |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ $128 (affordable) | +1.49% (87) | **+0.06% (31)** |
| $128–250 | +2.27% (55) | **+2.84% (34)** |
| > $250 | +0.84% (71) | +0.66% (72) |

*(Price bands use split-adjusted prices; small samples. Direction, not magnitude.)*

**The cap trade-off** (`--mode caps`, split-corrected position sizing):

| Notional cap, 2 slots | CAGR all / IS / OOS | Net EV/trade | Max DD |
|---|---|---|---|
| **10% (V6.4.2)** | **0.96 / 0.94 / 0.51%** | +0.40% | −3.9% |
| 15% | 4.28 / 3.40 / 5.27% | +1.67% | −4.7% |
| 20% | 4.24 / 3.06 / 5.45% | +1.38% | −5.7% |
| none (risk sizing only) | 3.55 / 2.25 / 4.75% | +1.29% | −6.5% |

10% is the worst setting tested, in both halves. 15–20% recovers most of the edge for ~1–2pt more
drawdown. Drawdown barely moves because the 1% risk-to-stop rule still binds. What the cap buys is
**gap protection**: a 30% gap through the stop costs ~3% of the account at a 10% position, ~4.5% at 15%.
This is the July CRO's reason, and it is a real one. It is a risk limit, not a signal parameter,
so **the CEO does not change it**. It goes to John, then to the Owner.

**This reverses an earlier conclusion of this firm.** The cap was recommended here in July without
a portfolio simulation to price it. The Owner's own notes already record that a share-price ceiling
is anti-momentum ("加股價上限(反動量,有毒)"). The 10% notional cap rebuilt that ceiling by another route.

## 6. Other findings

1. **Costs decide everything at this size.** A US$1 minimum per side (`M2`) turns the account
   negative (−1.9% CAGR). The manual says Webull is zero-commission. **Check one real contract note**
   for platform, SEC/TAF and FX fees before trusting any number here.
2. **Rejected upgrades** (fail the "better in both halves" rule):
   - **A1, Engine 2 only:** CAGR −0.7%, worse in both halves.
   - **A2, momentum top-half filter on E2:** IS CAGR −0.01%.
3. **The momentum-pool +0.770% is a few big winners.** In the Owner's own `momentum_pit_trades.csv`
   (1,167 trades):
   - NVDA, TSLA, GE, MSFT and AMD are **58% of all profit**. Without the top 10 tickers, EV is +0.17%.
   - The largest 5% of trades contribute 164% of the total.
   - It held a **median of 11** concurrent positions (max 39). The account holds 2.
4. **E1 is a "10-day hold", not a mean-reversion target system.** Only 4% of E1 trades reach the 2R
   target. 64% exit at the 10-day limit, averaging +2.9%. The target mostly acts as an entry filter.
5. **20 paper trades cannot decide Engine 1.** At this volatility, a 20-trade mean has a 90% CI of about
   ±2.6 (E1) to ±3.3 (E2) percentage points around it. The 20-trade plan tests **discipline**, which is worth doing, not edge.
6. **Version drift.** Scanner screenshots from 6 and 28 Sep show CVX at US$209 and PFE 8 shares =
   US$229. Both exceed the V6.4.2 cap, so pre-V6.4.2 code was in use. The Pine indicator on the chart is
   **Engine 1 only**, labelled V6.4.1, and was applied to a name outside the universe (CRWV).
7. **V6.5 port defect.** The rebuild from PDF chose the E1 target before the R≥2 filter; the real
   code filters first. Fixed in `rules_v642.py`, with a test.
8. **Split adjustment.** The first V6.6 run sized positions on split-adjusted prices, which made
   pre-split NVDA, AVGO, WMT and NFLX look affordable. Caught on a manual trade check and fixed.
   All tables here are post-fix. The first run's 1.8% CAGR was wrong.
