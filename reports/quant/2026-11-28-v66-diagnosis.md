# V6.6 diagnosis: is the failure in the rules or in the market? (2026-11-28)

**Asked by:** the Owner, 28 Nov: "Do you think the fail of the system is because of fundamental rules in it? If yes, please help me to check and have modification. If no, explain why the system is not suitable in current market."

**Run by:** CEO, read-only, on data and runs already on file. **This is diagnosis on data the firm has already seen many times; it is not evidence for any change** (pre-registration `reports/quant/2026-11-28-v66-development-prereg.md`). Fingerprint `2b7b62182068` unchanged.

**Data:** Webull read-only bars, 38 names, 2022-11-30 → 2026-11-23.

**Sources:**
- the "per trade" run takes **every** signal, at the next open, with 10 bp per side. It is `lab.PER_TRADE`, re-run in memory and matching `results/summary.csv` rows 18–26;
- the account run is `results/hk_exec/trades_lab.csv`: the robustness live configuration, 130 trades;
- affordable means one share ≤ US$128.21 (10% of HK$10,000 at 7.8) at the entry price;
- the "top-10 hindsight risers" are the names that rose most over the window (robustness report §D).

## Answer: mostly the rules, plus the way the system was built and tested. The market explains the rest.

### 1. Engine 2's edge comes from hindsight in the stock list

| Engine 2 (trend breakout), every signal | Trades | Net EV per trade | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| All 38 names | 350 | **+1.12%** | 2.3 |
| Only the 10 names that rose most in 2022–26 | 89 | **+3.49%** | 2.6 |
| The other 28 names | 261 | +0.31% | 0.7 |
| Affordable at US$128 and not a top-10 riser | 68 | **−0.68%** | −0.9 |

- The 38 names were chosen in 2026: PLTR, NVDA, AVGO, MU, AMD and so on, stocks already known to have trended.
- A trend-following engine tested on them looks good. Remove them and the edge is gone (t 0.7).
- **This is a flaw in how the system was built and tested, not a market condition.** The V6.4 audit flagged it in July (C-1, survivorship / selection look-ahead). Today's numbers show it is the *whole* of Engine 2's edge.

### 2. The account rules then keep only the trades with no edge

| | Signals taken | Engine 2 net EV | Engine 1 net EV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every signal (no limits) | E1 737, E2 350 | +1.12% | +0.05% |
| The real account: 2 slots, 10% of HK$10,000, whole shares | E1 81, E2 49 | **−1.76%** | **+0.96%** |

- With a US$128 cap and whole shares, **21 of the 38 names were never traded.** NVDA, AVGO, META and LLY were never affordable, and the median trade was 1 share worth about US$104.
- In affordable names, Engine 2 is −0.00% per trade (90 trades) over all signals.
- So the size rule (10% of a HK$10,000 base, whole shares) combined with the small account excludes exactly the names where Engine 2 "worked", and leaves the ones where it never did.
- The 2-slot limit then takes only about 1 signal in 8.

### 3. Engine 1 (buy-the-dip) is the only part with a possible edge, and it is weak

| Engine 1, every signal | Trades | Net EV | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| All | 737 | +0.05% | 0.2 |
| Affordable | 250 | +0.75% | 1.5 |
| Not affordable | 487 | −0.30% | −1.0 |

- In the account, Engine 1 made **+US$81** over 81 trades: +US$108.6 from 6 targets, +US$112.5 from 56 day-10 exits, −US$139.7 from 19 stops.
- Its year-by-year result is uneven: 2023 −US$29, 2024 +US$5, **2025 +US$122**, 2026 −US$16.
- **None of this is statistically significant.**

### 4. The market's part

- **2023–25 was a strong, narrow bull market led by a few large, expensive stocks.** SPY rose 26.18% (2023), 24.89% (2024) and 17.72% (2025) (robustness report §A).
  - A small account that cannot buy those leaders, holds cash most of the time, and trades short swings will trail both buy-and-hold and, after its cash drag, T-bills.
  - That is partly the market, but mostly rule 2: the leaders were unaffordable.
- **Today:** SPY has been below its 200-day average since 19 Nov, so the system's own bull filter keeps it out. Nothing is lost by that.

## What this means for modifications

**What the diagnosis suggests:**
- drop or rebuild Engine 2;
- fix the affordability problem;
- test on a stock list chosen without hindsight.

The pre-registered hypotheses already cover two of these: **H3** (Engine 1 only) and **H5** (2022-fixed list).

**What must not happen:** changing the rules on the 2022–26 data just diagnosed, then testing them on the same data. That is exactly how the original edge was manufactured.

**Proposed addendum** (needs Math King's and the CRO's sign-off before any run; it adds tests, so the Holm correction gets stricter, not looser):
- **H6, fractional shares:** removes the affordability rule (`lab` Cfg `fractional=True`).
  - Only if Webull HK allows fractional US shares (an Owner fact).
  - Judged **on the 2022 list only**, because on the 38 names it would simply buy the hindsight winners.
- **H7, a combined V6.7 candidate:** Engine 1 only, on a universe fixed by rule (the S&P 100 per the latest OEF filing, refreshed once a year), with fractional shares if available. Judged on the 2022 list only, with every mark in §1 and §5 of the pre-registration.

**What has to happen before any of this can run:**
- the 2022 list from SEC EDGAR (the Owner's environment edit);
- the fractional-share fact (the Owner);
- Messi's slippage estimate (V1-b).
