# MU — Management Quality Assessment
**Date:** 2026-07-18 · **Analyst:** Peter, Management Quality (Dept. 2) · **Subject:** Sanjay Mehrotra (Chairman, President & CEO), Mark Murphy (EVP & CFO), Sumit Sadana (EVP & CBO)

## Management grade: A-

## 1. Promise-vs-delivery record — STRONG, with one notable blemish

**Delivered:**
- **HBM share target.** For several quarters through FY2024-25, management promised HBM market share would reach parity with Micron's overall DRAM share (~22.5%) in calendar 2025. Per Q4 FY25 coverage (Sept 2025), "Micron's HBM share is on track to grow and be in line with its overall DRAM share in calendar Q3" — target met on the stated timeline. Micron went from effectively zero HBM presence in 2023 to industry-share parity; by FQ3-26 (June 2026) Mehrotra reported HBM4 shipments "already... of over $1 billion" and HBM sold out through calendar 2026 under multi-year agreements.
- **Guidance accuracy (FY2026): serial beats of its own guidance, not misses.** FQ2-26 (Mar 18, 2026): revenue $23.86B and non-GAAP EPS $12.20 vs street $20.07B / $9.31. FQ3-26 (June 2026): revenue $41.46B vs own guidance of $33.5B ± $0.75B, and EPS ~$24.67 vs guided $19.15 ± $0.40 — a ~24% beat of their own midpoint. In a market with contract prices resetting upward this is conservative guidance rather than poor forecasting, but the error bars are now very wide; treat forward guidance as a floor, not a point estimate.
- **Structural demand claims backed by contracts:** 16 long-term customer agreements of 3–5 years signed (FQ3-26 call), plus the Anthropic strategic supply agreement (June 22, 2026).

**Blemish:**
- **December 18, 2024 guidance shock.** FQ2-25 guide of $7.9B ± $0.2B revenue / $1.43 EPS vs street $8.98B / $1.91; stock fell ~13% after-hours. Mehrotra's framing: "While consumer-oriented markets are weaker in the near term, we anticipate a return to growth in the second half of our fiscal year" — which did subsequently occur, but management's demand commentary through 2024 ran ahead of the consumer/NAND reality. This episode is the basis of the securities class action (below).

## 4. Governance flags

1. **Securities class action** (filed Jan 9, 2025; D. Idaho) alleging misleading demand/supply statements Mar 2023–Dec 2024 — **dismissed Feb 3, 2026** with leave to amend. Two **derivative suits** (Feb 2025) alleging fiduciary breach and insider trading over the same period remain pending. Standard post-drawdown litigation; the dismissal is favorable. Monitor any amended complaint.
2. **Combined Chairman/President/CEO roles** in Mehrotra — concentration of power, standard flag.
3. **Tone watch:** on CNBC (June 30, 2026) Mehrotra attributed the shortage partly to customers: "Certain customers drove pricing significantly down in our industry. In 2023, our prices came down to one-third of what they were." Factually defensible, but externalizing blame during a period of record pricing power is the kind of language to track under our integrity screen.
4. **Clean elsewhere:** no restatements; CFO Mark Murphy stable in seat since April 2022; no abrupt finance-org departures; no related-party issues found.

## 5. What this team would do in a downturn

The FY2023 playbook is on record and was executed early and hard: cut capex >40% and WFE >50%, cut wafer starts 25-30%, cut executive pay before cutting the dividend, take underutilization charges ($382M in FY23) rather than flood the market. Expect the same sequence next cycle: supply cuts first, capex deferral second, token dividend maintained, buybacks suspended. Two caveats: (a) mid-$40B FY27 construction capex and 3–5-year LTAs reduce flexibility versus 2022; (b) history says any buyback dollars will have been spent near the top, not the bottom.
