# KLA Corporation (KLAC) — Economic Moat Assessment

**Analyst:** 菲比斯 (Moat Analyst, Dept. 2 Equity Research)
**Reference date:** 2026-07-19
**For:** CIO / Investment Committee; margin-of-safety input for 𢦀鳩仔 (Valuation)

---

## Moat Rating

**WIDE · WIDENING (with a long-dated China tail risk that could turn this to "stable")**

This is one of the few genuinely wide-moat semis-cap-equipment names, and unlike most
"wide moat" labels it survives the ROIC test decisively — including through downcycles.
The rating is not based on popularity; it is based on a 25%+ ROIC floor at the cyclical
trough and a ~7x market-share lead that is still expanding.

---

## The ROIC Test (the non-negotiable gate)

A moat only counts if the business earns well above its cost of capital *through the
cycle*, not just at the peak. KLA passes emphatically.

| Metric | Evidence |
|---|---|
| ROIC, 2021–2025 average | ~43% (NOPAT $1.31B in FY20 → $3.85B in FY25; invested capital $6.59B → $9.24B) |
| ROIC, recent | ~42–43% (GuruFocus, Sep 2025 / Mar 2026) |
| ROIC at the **trough** (FY19/20 WFE downturn) | ~25% return on investment |
| Estimated WACC | ~9–13.5% (GuruFocus 13.47% Mar 2026; historically lower) |
| **Spread (ROIC − WACC)** | **Positive by 12–30+ points every year, including the trough** |

The load-bearing fact: even in the *worst* year of the cycle KLA earned ~25% on capital —
roughly double its cost of capital. That is the signature of a real moat, not operating
leverage flattering a good year. Popularity does not produce a 25% trough ROIC; structural
advantage does.

Corroborating margin evidence (pricing power + cost advantage):
- Gross margin ~61% (FY25); operating margin ~41.7% (recent).
- 10-year operating-margin band: **min 30.3% / median 36.9% / max 41.8%** — the *floor*
  of the range is a level most industrials never reach at their peak. Margins have been
  stable-to-rising, consistent with price increases that do not cost share.

## Evidence by Moat Source

### 1. Intellectual property / technology lead — **WIDE**
- **Strongest evidence:** ~56% share of the entire process-control market; >85% in optical
  wafer inspection; ~60% brightfield / >50% darkfield inspection; ~45% CD metrology / ~40%
  overlay. Nearest competitor (Applied Materials) ~9.8% and *declining* in 2025 — roughly a
  7:1 lead that is widening, not holding.
- **Counter-evidence:** Share this high is a ceiling; incremental gains are hard and invite
  antitrust/customer-diversification pressure. Leading-edge inspection R&D intensity is
  rising, so the lead must be continuously repurchased.

### 2. Switching costs — **WIDE**
- **Strongest evidence:** Tools are embedded in qualified fab process lines with recipes
  tuned per node; ripping out KLA means re-qualifying yield-critical steps. This shows up in
  service: **$2.7B service revenue FY25 (+15% YoY), 52 consecutive quarters of YoY growth**,
  ~22% of revenue, >75% of revenue "subscription-like"/recurring. Customers keep paying to
  keep the installed base running.
- **Counter-evidence:** Switching costs protect the *installed base*, not necessarily the
  *next* greenfield fab's tool-of-record decision, which is where a domestic Chinese
  challenger could enter at mature nodes.

### 3. Cost advantage / scale — **WIDE (derived)**
- **Strongest evidence:** Largest installed base in the category funds the largest applications
  and service organization, which in turn spreads R&D over the most units — a self-reinforcing
  scale loop reflected in the ~61% gross margin no sub-scale rival matches.
- **Counter-evidence:** Chinese entrants price 30–50% below KLA; at mature nodes where
  "good enough" is acceptable, KLA's cost structure is a liability, not an asset.

### 4. Brand / mission-critical role — **NARROW-to-WIDE**
- **Strongest evidence:** KLA is the yield-management standard at the leading edge; being
  "tool of record" is a reputational lock-in at the most advanced nodes (where AI-chip
  economics make yield the single most valuable variable).
- **Counter-evidence:** Brand is downstream of technology; it erodes the moment a rival
  matches capability at a node. Not an independent moat source.

### 5. Regulatory / network effects — **NONE (as a moat)**
- US export controls currently *help* KLA vs. Chinese rivals at the leading edge, but this
  is a policy accident, not a durable moat — and it is a double-edged sword (see threats).
  No genuine network effect exists.

## Durability vs. Applied Materials / Lam (adjacent moats)

KLA's moat is **narrower in scope but deeper** than AMAT's or Lam's. AMAT and Lam have broad
deposition/etch franchises but face 2–3 credible competitors each (Lam vs. AMAT vs. TEL in
etch/dep). KLA competes in a category where its nearest rival has ~1/7th its share and is
losing ground. The trade-off: KLA's total addressable market is smaller and more concentrated,
so it is more exposed to a single WFE line item being cut and to customer concentration. Net:
KLA's *competitive* moat is the widest of the three; its *diversification* is the weakest.

---

## Threat Assessment (steel-manned disruption case)

**Threat 1 — China domestic tool development (top structural threat).**
Steel-man: Beijing has unlimited patience and capital, a captive domestic demand base, and
explicit localization mandates. SMEE, Wuhan Jingce (JingCe), and RSIC are already gaining in
mature-node unpatterned-wafer inspection and mask inspection at 30–50% lower price. Export
controls that block KLA from selling advanced tools into China are simultaneously *forcing*
Chinese fabs to fund and qualify domestic alternatives — the single most effective way to
destroy a switching-cost moat is to remove the incumbent from the market by fiat, which is
exactly what US policy is doing at the leading edge in China. If domestic tools climb the node
ladder from mature to advanced over 10 years, KLA loses the ~33% of revenue tied to China and,
worse, faces a scaled competitor with reference installations.
Assessment: real and rising, but today confined to trailing nodes where reliability/throughput
still lag. Time horizon 5–10+ years for leading-edge parity. This is the erosion vector to watch.

**Threat 2 — WFE cyclicality + customer concentration (top near-term threat).**
Steel-man: TSMC/Intel/Samsung drive ~65–75% of revenue and individual customers can each
exceed 10% of sales. A capex pause by 1–2 leading-edge foundries compresses revenue fast; China
revenue already fell from 41% (FY24) to ~30% (FY25), with a $300–350M export-control hit
guided over five quarters. This is not a moat threat per se, but it means the moat's *cash
flows* are lumpy and the entry price matters enormously.
Assessment: certain to recur; does not impair the moat (trough ROIC ~25% proves it) but does
impair predictability and thus valuation.

Honorable mention: antitrust risk from >50% share, and the secular risk that AI-chip yield
economics eventually attract a well-funded new optical-inspection entrant.

## Ten-Year Test — what must stay true

For KLA to be *stronger* in 2036 than today:
1. **Leading-edge process control stays a distinct, KLA-dominated discipline** — inspection/
   metrology does not get commoditized or absorbed into litho/dep/etch platforms.
2. **The installed-base/service flywheel keeps compounding** — recurring revenue continues its
   double-digit, 50+ quarter growth streak, deepening switching costs faster than rivals can
   qualify in.
3. **Chinese domestic tools remain confined to mature nodes** — no leading-edge parity from
   SMEE/Jingce/RSIC; KLA is not structurally excluded from the fastest-growing demand pool.
4. **KLA out-invests in R&D at each node transition** — the technology lead is re-earned, since
   this moat is rented, not owned.

If (1)–(3) hold, KLA is wider in ten years. The swing factor is (3): a China-localization
success at advanced nodes is the one scenario that converts this from widening to eroding.

## Implication for the Margin of Safety (input to 𢦀鳩仔)

- **Moat quality justifies a premium multiple, not an unlimited one.** The wide moat + 40%+
  through-cycle ROIC supports paying up for quality; this is a compounder, not a cigar butt.
- **But demand a *cyclical* margin of safety, not a moat discount.** The right time to buy is a
  WFE downturn, when revenue and multiple compress together despite the moat being intact
  (trough ROIC ~25% is the anchor that says the business is fine even when the stock is not).
- **Haircut the China third.** Model a scenario where ~30% of revenue (the China book) is
  structurally impaired over 10 years by localization + export controls, and require the entry
  price to still clear a hurdle in that case. Recommend 𢦀鳩仔 run base / bear(China-lost) /
  bull cases and demand **~25–30% discount to base-case intrinsic value** given customer
  concentration and cyclicality — wider than the ~15% one might demand for a diversified
  wide-moat compounder, precisely because the cash flows are lumpy and geopolitically exposed.
- **Erosion-call trigger for this desk:** first evidence of a Chinese domestic inspection/
  metrology tool qualified as "tool of record" at an advanced logic/DRAM node. That is the
  leading indicator that would move my rating from *widening* to *eroding* — before it shows
  up in KLA's share or margins.
