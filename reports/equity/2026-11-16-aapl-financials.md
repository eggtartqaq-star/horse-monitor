## 1. Summary & grade

**Grade: A− · Financial score 78/100** (80 on the statements, minus 2 because key data could not be verified: cash balance, SBC, capex).

The statements are excellent. FY2026 gross margin was 48.8%, operating margin 33.0% (derived), and Services gross margin 76.2%. Free cash flow was roughly equal to net income or slightly above it. Debt is about $80bn against $158bn of operating income. Recent EPS growth is mostly real: in FY2026, EPS rose 20.4%, of which about 18 points came from net income growth and only about 2 points from a smaller share count.

What holds the grade below A is durability, not quality. FY2026 is an iPhone up-cycle year (iPhone +21%, Greater China +28%), and Q4 got a one-off boost from tariff refunds. Over five years the picture is more modest. Net income grew from $94.7bn (FY2021) to $132.2bn (FY2026), a 6.9% CAGR. EPS CAGR was 9.9%, and about 29% of that came from buybacks. The FY2022–FY2024 earnings were flat. A ~40x P/E is pricing the last two years as the new normal. The five-year record supports durability, not ~20% growth.

**Quality vs price of entry.** The 78 rates the statements only, with no valuation input. At 39.7x TTM EPS (2.5% earnings yield, about 2.7% FCF yield, derived), the entry price is a separate and material negative. That belongs to 𢦀鳩仔 (Valuation), not this score.

**The 3 numbers that matter**
1. **Services gross margin 76.2%, 40.2% of company gross profit on 25.8% of revenue (FY2026, derived).** This is the quality argument. The prior-year split was NOT OBTAINED, so I cannot show the trend.
2. **EPS +20.4% in FY2026 (≈18pt net income, ≈2pt share count), but the 5-year EPS CAGR is 9.9%.** Whether the 40x multiple is justified depends on which of those two numbers persists.
3. **The Google payment: about $20bn a year (widely reported estimate), roughly 16% of Services revenue and possibly ~13% of operating income.** It survived the September 2025 remedy ruling, but it is under appeal (single-source report) and the judge said he could revisit it.

## 4. The Google payment

- **Size.** About **$20bn a year** paid to Apple is "the figure most outlets use" [S13, S14, S15]. Apple has not disclosed it. It is an **estimate** and may be stale relative to FY2026, since it originates in antitrust-trial evidence. One source puts Google's total default payments to all partners above $26bn [S13].
- **Scale against FY2026 Services** (derived, estimate-based):
  - $20bn / $123.3bn = **≈16% of Services revenue**.
  - It is likely near-100% margin, but that is an assumption because Apple does not disclose the cost. On that basis it is ≈21% of Services gross profit ($93.9bn), ≈8.6% of company gross profit, and **≈12.7% of operating income** ($158.1bn).
  - Per share: $20bn / 14.72bn ≈ **$1.36 pre-tax**. The tax rate was NOT OBTAINED. At an illustrative 15–20% rate it would be about $1.09–1.16 after tax, roughly 12–13% of FY2026 EPS. This is a labeled sensitivity, not a forecast.
- **What the 2 Sep 2025 remedy ruling (US v. Google, Judge Mehta) did** [S13, S14, S15, S16]:
  - Google may **keep paying** for default placement. A payment ban was rejected.
  - **Exclusive** distribution deals are prohibited. Revenue share cannot be conditioned on keeping Google as the default for more than one year.
  - Apple may promote or integrate other assistants and chatbots alongside Google.
  - Apple shares rose on the decision [S15].
  - The judge said he is "prepared to revisit a payment ban (or a lesser remedy) if competition is not substantially restored."
  - **Appeal:** DOJ and states reportedly filed notices of appeal on 13-Apr-2026, challenging the rejection of a payment ban [S17]. This comes from a single, less-established source and is **unverified**.
- **Net effect.** The most likely outcome is that the payment continues. The worst case (a payment ban) would remove about 12–13% of operating income in one step, at nearly 100% margin. It has not been eliminated, only deferred. Annual renegotiation also probably weakens Apple's pricing power in the deal over time, but that is my inference.

## 5. Buybacks, share count and EPS decomposition

**Share count** (weighted diluted, [S10, S11]): FY2019 18.596bn → FY2020 17.528bn → FY2021 16.865bn → FY2023 15.813bn → FY2024 15.408bn → FY2025 15.005bn → FY2026 ≈14.72bn (derived). FY2021→FY2026 is **−12.7%**, about **−2.7%/yr**. The decline is slowing: −2.6%, −2.6%, then −1.9% in FY2026.

**EPS decomposition** (derived, multiplicative):

| Period | EPS growth | From net income | From share count | Buyback share of EPS growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2026 | +20.4% | +18.0% | ×1.019 (≈+1.9pt) | **≈9%** |
| FY2025 | +22.7% | +19.5% | ×1.027 (≈+2.7pt) | ≈12% |
| FY2021→FY2026 (5 yr) | +60.1% (9.9% CAGR) | +39.6% (6.9% CAGR) | ×1.146 (2.8%/yr) | **≈29%** (log basis) |

**Buyback spend vs FCF, FY2026** [S6] and derived:
- Repurchased **297m shares for $85.8bn**, an average of **≈$289/share** (derived). That is about 19% below the current $356.
- Dividends paid: $15.7bn. Total returned: **$101.5bn**.
- Against FCF of ≈$136.1bn (capex unverified, see §6), returns were **≈75% of FCF**. Buybacks alone were ≈63%. Apple returned less than its FCF in FY2026. Apple has also **dropped its formal "net cash neutral" target** [S6], so cash is likely accumulating. That is unverified because the balance sheet cash figure was NOT OBTAINED.
- New $100bn authorization, April 2026 [S6].

**Buyback accretion is shrinking.** At the current market cap of ≈$5.08tn (derived: $356.27 × 14.27bn), an $85.8bn buyback retires only ≈1.7% of the company a year. The higher the multiple, the less EPS each buyback dollar buys. At 40x, buybacks cannot carry EPS growth the way they did between FY2019 and FY2023.

**Share count discrepancy (flag).** The caller's Webull figure is 14.27bn shares outstanding. The implied FY2026 weighted diluted count is 14.72bn, and MarketBeat showed a Q3 basic count of ≈14.7bn [S6 search]. A 3% gap between a period-end basic count and the full-year weighted diluted count is possible but large. **Not reconciled.** It affects market-cap-based ratios (P/B, P/S, buyback yield) but not P/E.

## 10. Strongest case for / against

**For.**
- Each Services dollar carries 76% gross margin, and Services make up 40% of gross profit.
- Group operating margin is 33%, FCF is about equal to net income, and capex is light.
- Debt is covered roughly 2x by one year's operating income, and Apple could repay all of it out of less than one year's FCF.
- Growth has re-accelerated to double digits (revenue +15%, net income +18%), and about 90% of FY2026 EPS growth was organic, not buyback-driven.
- Apple is generating FCF without the AI-capex burden hitting MSFT and other hyperscalers.
- It returned only about 75% of FCF and dropped net-cash-neutral, which creates optionality.

**Against.**
- FY2026 looks like a cyclical peak: iPhone +21%, China +28%, and a tariff-refund boost in Q4. All three tend to mean-revert.
- The five-year record is a 6.9% net income CAGR, with net income flat from FY2021 to FY2024. A 40x P/E capitalizes the peak.
- About 13% of operating income (estimate) depends on a Google payment that is under court supervision, limited to one-year terms, and reportedly on appeal.
- Buyback accretion fades as the multiple rises (about 1.7% of market cap a year now).
- iPhone at 53% of revenue makes Apple a single-product company.
- Apple's AI position relies on lower spending, which helps FCF today but could become a strategic gap the statements will not show until it appears in revenue.
