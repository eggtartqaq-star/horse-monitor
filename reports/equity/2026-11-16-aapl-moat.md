## 2. The question

Everyone agrees Apple has a wide moat, so confirming that tells the Owner nothing. The useful
question is **which of Apple's two businesses carries the moat, and which one is under attack.**

1. **The device franchise.** About 2.5bn active devices, iPhone at 50% of revenue, hardware
   gross margin around 37%. Its moat is brand, integration and ecosystem switching costs.
2. **The toll franchise.** Services: App Store commission, the Google default-search payment,
   iCloud, subscriptions. 76% gross margin. Its moat is **control of distribution** on a
   2.5bn-device installed base.

In the MSFT report the key distinction was preference versus distribution: preference moats last,
and distribution moats last only until a regulator takes the distribution away. **For Apple the
device franchise is a preference moat and the toll franchise is a distribution moat.** Courts and
regulators in the US, the EU and Japan are attacking the second. Generative AI threatens both, but
the second more.

### 3.2 The caveat the committee must hear — the denominator is being shrunk on purpose

The brief is right that buybacks make ROE meaningless. **They also flatter ROIC whenever a
vendor builds invested capital from book equity plus debt.** Apple has spent years returning
cash so that its equity base shrinks. *[recall, unverified: Apple's stated goal of becoming
"net cash neutral" over time.]* Stockrow's own 10-year range runs from **21.8% (FY2017) to 78.3%
(FY2024)**. A business with a stable operating model does not triple its operating return in
seven years. **Much of that rise is the denominator shrinking, not the moat widening.**

**What I can and cannot claim:**

- **CAN:** on every vendor definition, Apple earns far more than its cost of capital, and the
  ratio has not declined. **Apple is not having to invest more each year to defend its returns**
  — the specific failure I found at MSFT.
- **CANNOT:** that ROIC has *risen* because the moat widened. I have no operating-only ROIC
  (excess cash removed, NOPAT over operating invested capital) and will not make one up (§13,
  item 1).

| Android switchers as a share of new iPhone buyers, US | **12%** (Mar 2026 qtr); **11–15%** historically | [SQ Magazine / CIRP](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/apple-customer-loyalty-statistics/) |

**What the data support:**

1. **Captivity is very high and has been for five years.** About 9 in 10 US iPhone buyers
   stay on iOS. That is consistent with real switching costs: iMessage and FaceTime, iCloud
   photos, purchased apps, Watch and AirPods pairing, family sharing.
2. **It is not widening.** Loyalty sits inside a five-year 87–93% band, and Android switchers
   have held at 11–15% of buyers. **The 87% "record" headline compares against only three years
   of a narrower series.** I am not treating it as a trend.
3. **Counter-evidence I will not bury.** The CIRP post is titled ***"iPhone Loyalty Remains Very
   High, Android Even Higher."*** If Android loyalty is equal or higher, then **high retention is
   a feature of a two-platform smartphone market, not proof of an Apple-specific moat.** Leaving
   any platform costs the user. Apple's advantage shows up in **which** users it keeps (the
   premium tier) and what they pay, not in a uniquely high retention rate.
4. **Limits:** CIRP is US-only, does not publish sample size or margin of error, and measures
   the platform rather than the brand. I found **no attach-rate data for Watch or AirPods** and no
   non-US loyalty data (§13).

**Why I still rate this WIDE:** loyalty near 90% in the world's most profitable smartphone market,
held for five years while Android flagships matched or beat iPhone on specifications, shows the
lock-in does not rest on Apple having the best product in any given year. Like Microsoft's,
**this moat survives Apple having a weaker product for a year or two.** §6 tests whether it
survives AI.

### 5.3 Has the toll rate actually fallen? The honest answer

- **I cannot measure the effective App Store take rate.** Apple does not disclose it.
- **What can be observed:** Services is growing **~10%** at a **76.0%** gross margin with a record
  quarter. **That is not what a toll in visible collapse looks like.** If link-outs were moving
  large amounts of US App Store revenue off-platform at zero commission, a 76% blended margin and
  10% growth would be hard to sustain. I do not have a sourced prior-year Services gross margin to
  confirm the trend (§13, item 4).
- **So: no measurable erosion yet in what Apple reports.**

**My erosion call, and why I make it before the numbers move:**

1. **The legal direction is settled even though the rate is not.** The EU (DMA, fined), the US
   (Ninth Circuit: "some" commission, not the commission Apple chose) and Japan *[recall]* have all
   rejected Apple's right to set its own toll unilaterally. **No jurisdiction is moving the other
   way.** The open question is how far the rate falls, not whether it falls.
2. **The US decision matters most,** because the US is the core App Store profit pool and the
   anti-steering remedy reaches the largest developers (games, streaming, dating, AI subscriptions).
   A court-set "reasonable" link-out commission becomes a **ceiling** that every large developer
   will use.
3. **Why the effect is lagging.** A large developer re-plumbs payments only once the legal rate is
   final. A final Supreme Court ruling in 2027 would show up in Services growth in FY2027–28.
   **That is the window in which I expect the numbers to confirm this call.**
4. **Steel-man for Apple.** Convenience is a moat inside the toll. Users trust Apple Pay and
   one-tap subscription management, and conversion falls at every extra step in a link-out. If
   most users still pay in-app even where a cheaper link exists, the legal cap on the link-out fee
   hardly matters. **I have no link-out conversion data to settle this (§13, item 5).** It is the
   single most valuable data point the committee could get on Apple.

### 6.2 The disruption case at full strength (threat)

**Thesis: the smartphone moat rests on apps, and AI agents make apps unnecessary.**

The App Store toll, and much of the ecosystem lock-in, assumes that **the unit of interaction
is the app.** You open Uber and pay inside it. You open Spotify and subscribe. Apple sits on every
one of those transactions. Now suppose the unit of interaction becomes **an agent you instruct by
voice or text**, which books, buys, summarises and plays on your behalf across services:

- **The app grid becomes plumbing.** The user no longer chooses apps. The agent chooses services.
  **No storefront means no toll**, because an agent calling a service's API is not an in-app
  purchase.
- **Whoever owns the agent owns the customer.** In 2026 the brain inside Siri is **Google's**.
  Apple shows the interface; Google provides the intelligence. For the most important new layer
  of personal computing, **Apple has moved from platform owner to distributor of someone else's
  model.** That is weaker than MSFT's position. Microsoft owns ~27% of OpenAI and is building its
  own MAI models. Apple rents.
- **Google is on both sides of Apple's profit pool.** It pays Apple for default search (inside
  Services) and is paid by Apple for Gemini. If AI answers replace search queries, the search
  payment shrinks, and Apple's AI is supplied by the firm with the most to gain from that.
  **Few counterparties have that much leverage over a wide-moat business.**
- **Switching costs could flip.** Today the switching cost is "my photos, messages and apps live
  in iOS." If a cross-platform assistant (Gemini, ChatGPT) holds your memory, preferences and
  context, **that memory goes with you to any device.** In this scenario a phone is a camera,
  a screen and a radio, which is the market Android OEMs already win on price.
- *[recall, unverified: OpenAI bought Jony Ive's hardware start-up "io" in 2025 to build an
  AI-native device.]* A device with no app grid built by a model owner is exactly this scenario.

**This is a serious case.** It needs no technology breakthrough, only a gradual change in user
habits, and the in-house AI delay that pushed Apple to Google is the first evidence for it.

### 6.3 The deepening case at full strength (deepener)

**Thesis: models are becoming commodities, distribution plus personal context is the scarce
asset, and Apple owns more of both than anyone.**

- **Apple chose its model supplier — the supplier did not choose Apple.** A reported ~$1bn a year
  is about **0.2% of FY26 revenue** (~$479bn; my arithmetic, using the unconfirmed $1bn). Apple
  could swap or add vendors (one source says ChatGPT remains an opt-in inside Siri). **When the
  model layer has several credible suppliers bidding for 2.5bn devices, the buyer has the pricing
  power.** By this reading Apple is the best-placed company in AI because it does not have to
  spend on the frontier race. That also explains §3: **its ROIC is not being diluted by AI capex
  the way MSFT's is.**
- **The device holds the context.** An agent is useful only if it knows your messages, calendar,
  health, location, payments and photos, and that data lives on the iPhone under Apple's
  permission system. Google is contractually barred from training on Siri queries. **Apple keeps
  the customer relationship and the data and buys the computation.**
- **The evidence from 2026 so far.** iPhone revenue rose **+17.5%** in the September quarter, a
  September-quarter record ([Vested Finance](https://vestedfinance.com/blog/us-stocks/apple-q4-2026-earnings-decoded/)).
  US loyalty is **90%**. Apple **gained share in China** (§8.1) **in a quarter when the new Siri
  was reportedly not available in China at all.** If AI capability were driving phone choice,
  Apple's AI delay should have cost it buyers. **There is no sign it has.**

### 6.4 Which does the evidence support?

**Near term (1–3 years): the deepener case, clearly, on the device.** Hardware demand, loyalty and
China share all held through the period when Apple's AI was visibly behind. Users are not choosing
phones by their assistant.

**Long term (5–10 years): the threat case, on the toll.** The finding is the same as for MSFT:
**AI is turning Apple's moat into a pure distribution moat.** Apple's AI position rests on owning
the device that delivers someone else's model. Distribution moats last only until (a) a regulator
removes the distribution, which is happening to the App Store (§5), or (b) the user's context
moves somewhere portable, which is what a cross-platform assistant is built to do.

> **My adjudication, as a claim the committee can hold me to:** AI is not a threat to iPhone
> unit demand in this decade. It is the most credible long-run threat to the **App Store toll and
> the Google search payment** — the 43.7% of gross profit with the highest margin. **The device
> moat is safe from AI. The rent collected on top of the device is not.**

both are gaining share from Xiaomi, vivo, OPPO and HONOR
([TelecomLead](https://telecomlead.com/smart-phone/china-smartphone-market-q3-2026-huawei-and-apple-gain-share-as-xiaomi-vivo-oppo-and-honor-decline-128351)).
**Huawei grew 17.3%**
([TelecomLead](https://telecomlead.com/smart-phone/china-smartphone-market-q3-2026-huawei-grows-17-3-as-apple-oppo-vivo-and-xiaomi-decline-128170))
and is the only vendor with double-digit growth on IDC's count.

**Reading:** China is where the device moat is **narrow, not wide**. Huawei is a domestic premium
brand with its own operating system (HarmonyOS), so it is the one competitor that can win
Apple's premium customers **with an ecosystem that has its own lock-in**. That makes it a real
platform competitor, not just another phone maker. Apple held up in Q3, but it holds second place
**without** its new AI features in that market. **These are preliminary estimates, and IDC often
revises its China numbers.**

### 8.2 Product cadence

**The standard iPhone 18 has been moved to 2027** ([Counterpoint via search extraction](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/china-smartphone-shipments-hit-14-year-low-in-q3-2026)).
IDC partly attributes Apple's −4.3% China shipments to the smaller number of models launched.
This is a scheduling choice, not a moat failure, but it is the first break in the annual iPhone
cycle I am aware of, and the committee should know about it.

### 8.3 Failed or unproven categories

- **In-house frontier AI: failed to ship on time.** Licensing Gemini is the evidence. Of the items
  in this section, it is the only one that bears on the moat.
- *[recall, unverified — not scored]* Vision Pro has not become a mass-market category, and the
  car project was cancelled in 2024. Neither weakens the existing moat; both show **the moat does
  not carry over into new hardware categories just because Apple enters them.** That parallels
  MSFT §8: deep protection inside its scope, none outside it.
- **Wearables:** I obtained no FY26 figures. **NOT OBTAINED** (§13, item 8). I make no claim
  either way.

## 10. Ten-year view — what must stay true

For Apple to be **stronger** in 2036 than today, all four must hold:

1. **The phone stays the main personal-compute device through which AI is used, and Apple keeps
   the user's context on it** — under Apple's permission system, not in a third party's assistant
   memory.
2. **Apple keeps more than one credible model supplier.** If one model vendor (Google or anyone
   else) becomes indispensable, the toll runs in reverse. Watch whether Apple ships its own or a
   second-source foundation model by about 2028.
3. **The post-litigation US link-out commission stays well above payment-processing cost** —
   high enough that the App Store remains a toll rather than a payment processor.
4. **Premium share in China stays in the high teens or better** against a Huawei with its own
   ecosystem.

**My probability-weighted view, stated qualitatively because I have no model:** condition 4 is
the likeliest to hold. Condition 3 will hold **partly**: the toll survives at a lower rate.
Conditions 1 and 2 are the open bets, and the Gemini dependency is evidence **against** them as of
2026. **In ten years Apple is very likely to remain a wide-moat device business. Whether it
remains a wide-moat toll business is genuinely uncertain, and most of the current premium in
the valuation sits in that part.** The last clause needs 𢦀鳩仔's model to confirm — it is a
hypothesis, not a finding.

**What makes Apple stronger than MSFT:** one thing, and it is the variable this desk weights

## 12. Implication for the margin of safety 𢦀鳩仔 should demand

**Required margin of safety: 20%** below 𢦀鳩仔's central intrinsic value, **conditional on
the following:**

1. **Model Services in two parts, not one.** Device-related services (iCloud, AppleCare,
   subscriptions) can be valued on today's economics. **The App Store commission and the Google
   payment must be stress-tested**: one case with a court-set lower US link-out rate from FY28,
   and one with the search payment declining as AI answers replace queries. I do not specify the
   haircuts. That is valuation work, and choosing them here would mean inventing numbers.
2. **If the central value holds the Services take rate or the Services gross margin flat for ten
   years, require 25%, not 20%.** That assumption is exactly what §5 and §6 dispute.
3. **Do not reward the ROIC headline.** Use an operating ROIC with excess cash removed (§3.2). A
   vendor ROIC of 70–105% inflated by buybacks should not justify a terminal multiple.

**Why 20% rather than lower, given the score is above MSFT:** a higher score would normally earn
a smaller margin of safety. Two things cancel that here. (a) **The largest single risk to the
moat has a dated legal milestone** — the Supreme Court argument reportedly in January 2027, about
two months away. (b) **The stock is at an all-time high.** The Owner's cost of $357.60 is about
0.4% above the ~$356 reference, and the market is plainly not pricing any toll risk. When the
largest risk has a near-term date and the price shows no discount for it, I would not lower the
margin of safety.

**What this report does not say.** It does not say sell, hold or switch. The Owner holds AAPL at
$357.60. Whether that price is inside or outside a 20% margin of safety depends entirely on
𢦀鳩仔's valuation, **which does not exist yet.** The moat is wide. Whether the price is right is
a separate question I have not answered.
