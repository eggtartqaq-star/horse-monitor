## 2. What a moat means for an oncology company

The generic Buffett checklist does not transfer, and applying it produces nonsense. Physicians
have no brand loyalty to a BTK inhibitor; there is no network effect; the switching cost is a
prescription pad. For a drug company the moat is three things and only three:

| Source | What it actually is | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Patent + regulatory exclusivity** | A state-granted monopoly. The only true legal barrier. | **Dated. It expires on a known day.** |
| **Clinical differentiation** | Evidence a physician cannot argue with — ideally a randomised head-to-head win. Converts the monopoly into *share*. | Durable while the label holds |
| **Commercial reach** | Salesforce, country filings, reimbursement dossiers, distribution. Expensive and slow to build. | **Survives the patent — it carries the next molecule** |

The structural point that governs this entire report: **the first two are wasting assets; only
the third compounds.** A pharma moat is not a fortress, it is a *relay race*. The question is
never "how wide" but **"does the commercial platform reliably hand the baton to the next
protected molecule before the current one expires?"** §7 is therefore not a bonus section — it
is where the moat either continues or ends.

**Base-rate precision (per the CEO's instruction).** The industry report's **9.6% cumulative
Phase I → approval rate** (BIO, 9,985 phase transitions across 7,455 programmes,
[BIO/Biomedtracker](https://diyhpl.us/~nmz787/pdf/Clinical_development_success_rates_for_investigational_drugs.pdf))
applies to BeOne's **pipeline**, and *only* to the pipeline. BRUKINSA, TEVIMBRA and now
BEQALZI are approved, marketed and generating cash; they carry **zero** approval risk. Anyone
applying a 10% survival probability to BeOne as a whole has made a category error. Anyone
valuing the unapproved pipeline near face value has made the opposite error. Roughly **80% of
current revenue sits in the risk-free-of-approval bucket**; the disagreement should be about
the other 20% and the terminal value.

## 3. Moat source 1 — Clinical differentiation: ALPINE and the head-to-head question

### 3.1 Versus ibrutinib: a genuine head-to-head win. This is real, and it is rare.

**ALPINE is a randomised, open-label Phase III head-to-head trial of zanubrutinib versus
ibrutinib in relapsed/refractory CLL/SLL, 652 patients, 113 sites worldwide, published in the
*New England Journal of Medicine*.**

| Endpoint | Zanubrutinib | Ibrutinib | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall response rate (primary) | **86.2%** | 75.7% | Non-inferiority **and superiority** met |
| PFS | — | — | **HR 0.65** — 35% reduction in progression/death |
| 24-month PFS | **79.5%** | 67.3% | +12.2pp |
| Discontinuation for toxicity | **13.0%** | 17.6% | Favours zanubrutinib |
| Atrial fibrillation / flutter | Lower | Higher | Favours zanubrutinib |

Sources: [NEJM — *Zanubrutinib or Ibrutinib in R/R CLL*](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2211582);
[JCO interim analysis](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.22.00510);
[*Blood* (2024) — final comparative analysis of ALPINE](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/144/26/2706/517956/Sustained-benefit-of-zanubrutinib-vs-ibrutinib-in);
[OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/final-response-analysis-of-alpine-trial-shows-superior-orr-with-zanubrutinib-vs-ibrutinib-in-cll);
[Targeted Oncology](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/alpine-trial-of-zanubrutinb-improves-pfs-vs-ibrutinib-in-cll-and-sll).

**"ALPINE is the only study to demonstrate PFS superiority in a head-to-head comparison of BTK
inhibitors"** ([Targeted Oncology](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/alpine-trial-shows-zanubrutinib-outperforms-ibrutinib-in-cll-sll)).

I want to be emphatic, because this is exactly the kind of finding a chronically sceptical
research house talks itself out of: **this is not marketing. It is a prospective randomised
superiority result against the category originator, in the highest-impact journal in
medicine.** It is one of a small number of instances in modern oncology where a challenger beat
the incumbent prospectively rather than by inference. AstraZeneca ran the equivalent trial for
acalabrutinib (**ELEVATE-RR**) against the same comparator and achieved only
**non-inferiority** on PFS ([context via *Blood Cancer Journal*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41408-024-01044-4)).
**Zanubrutinib did strictly better against ibrutinib than its closest rival did.** That is a
durable, label-embedded, physician-facing asset.

### 3.2 Versus acalabrutinib: no head-to-head exists. This is the honest limit of the claim.

**There has never been a randomised trial of zanubrutinib versus acalabrutinib.** Every
zanubrutinib-vs-acalabrutinib claim rests on **matching-adjusted indirect comparison (MAIC)** —
statistical reweighting across ALPINE and ELEVATE-RR
([PMC MAIC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12246662/);
[ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/march-2024/zanubrutinib-vs-acalabrutinib-in-relapsed-or-refractory-cll-new-efficacy-analysis/)).

And the MAIC literature is **contested and sponsor-directional**: an analysis favourable to
zanubrutinib reports significantly improved PFS and an OS trend versus acalabrutinib
([Pharmacy Times](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/zanubrutinib-outperforms-acalabrutinib-in-adjusted-head-to-head-analysis);
[Targeted Oncology](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/zanubrutinib-offers-clinical-and-cost-benefits-over-acalabrutinib-in-r-r-cll)),
while a separate MAIC in the *American Journal of Hematology* runs the comparison the other way
([AJH](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ajh.27110)). Independent methodologists
note the two trials are **structurally non-comparable** — ELEVATE-RR enrolled *only*
del(17p)/del(11q) high-risk patients, ALPINE did not restrict — so an anchored indirect
comparison carries **high risk of bias**
([*Blood Cancer Journal*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41408-024-01044-4)).

BeOne also cites **real-world Medicare data on >10,500 patients showing BRUKINSA reduced risk
of death by 24% vs acalabrutinib and 36% vs ibrutinib**
([Q2 2026 earnings call, 5 Aug 2026, via GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9029559/beone-medicines-ltd-onc-q2-2026-earnings-call-highlights-revenue-surges-30-to-17-billion-guidance-raised-on-brukinsa-momentum)).
**Treat this as supportive, not decisive.** Retrospective claims-database comparisons carry
channelling bias — the two drugs are not prescribed to identical patients — and a 24% mortality
difference between two agents of the same class and mechanism is, on its face, larger than
mechanism plausibly supports. It is a marketing-grade asset, not an evidence-grade one.

### 3.3 Verdict on the central claim

BeOne's differentiation claim is **half proven and half asserted.** Proven, prospectively,
against **ibrutinib** — the drug that is losing share anyway. Asserted, via reweighted
cross-trial statistics, against **acalabrutinib** — the drug that competes for the marginal
prescription. A moat analyst must not let the strength of the first launder the weakness of the
second. **The committee should not be told "BRUKINSA is proven best-in-class." It should be
told "BRUKINSA is the only BTKi with a randomised superiority win, and its lead over the No. 2
is inferred, not measured."**

### 3.4 Did the evidence convert into share? Yes — this is the confirming number

Clinical differentiation is only a moat if physicians act on it. They have:

- **BRUKINSA FY2025 global revenue $3.9bn, +49% YoY**, now the **global revenue leader in the
  BTK class** ([Vision Lifesciences](https://visionlifesciences.com/insights/beigene-china-biotech-partnerships-pipeline)).
- **Q2 2026: BRUKINSA global revenue >$1.2bn, +31% YoY**, with **"the highest level of
  sustained new patient starts since launch"**; **US BRUKINSA $893m, +31%**
  ([BeOne Q2 2026 results, 5 Aug 2026, via StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/ONC/be-one-medicines-announces-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-and-mrtkxm4ku2ly.html)).
- Company states it **maintains leading new-patient share across the BTKi class**
  ([BeiGene Q3 2025 earnings release, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1651308/000162828025049821/exhibit991-q32025earningsr.htm)).
- The top three BTKis (Imbruvica, Calquence, Brukinsa) hold **>60% of class share**
  ([Expert Market Research](https://www.expertmarketresearch.com/reports/brutons-tyrosine-kinase-btk-inhibitors-market)) —
  BRUKINSA is the *newest* of the three and the *revenue leader*.

A late entrant taking class revenue leadership from a franchise as entrenched as Imbruvica —
while *still growing new-patient share seven years post-launch* — is precisely the share
durability evidence my mandate demands. **Rating for this moat source: WIDE, time-boxed by §4.**

## 4. Moat source 2 — Patent life: when does BRUKINSA lose exclusivity?

**This is the most important section in the report, and it is also where my sourcing is
weakest. Read the confidence caveat before quoting any date.**

### 4.1 United States

| Protection | Expiry | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition-of-matter (compound) patent, US** | **April 2034** | BeiGene SEC filings, via [DrugPatentWatch](https://www.drugpatentwatch.com/p/tradename/BRUKINSA) and [Synapse patent landscape](https://synapse.patsnap.com/article/when-does-the-patent-for-zanubrutinib-expire) |
| Oral dosage form; method-of-use (BTK modulation) | 2034 | same |
| Crystalline forms; specific-indication use patents | **2037, 2043** | same |
| **MSN Pharmaceuticals settlement — negotiated generic entry** | **no earlier than 15 June 2037** (conditions may move it either way) | [BeiGene 8-K, 14 Nov 2024 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1651308/000165130824000157/bgne-20241114.htm); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/sec-filings/beigene-settles-patent-litigation-extends-brukinsa-exclusivity-93CH-3729614); [Pharma Manufacturing](https://www.pharmamanufacturing.com/industry-news/news/55245034/beigene-settles-patent-case-over-b-cell-malignancy-treatment) |
| Earliest modelled generic entry, all protections considered | **15 August 2037** | [DrugPatentWatch](https://www.drugpatentwatch.com/p/tradename/BRUKINSA) |

**This is the finding that most changes the shape of the asset.** The brief framed it as "2030
cliff or 2038 cliff." **On the available evidence the US answer is far closer to 2037 than to
2030 — roughly eleven to thirteen years of runway from today, not five.**

The *mechanism* matters more than the list. The **first ANDA filer settled, and settled on a
date three years past the composition-of-matter expiry.** A first filer accepting 2037 rather
than litigating for a 2034 launch is a market-priced statement, by a counterparty with real
money at risk, that the secondary estate (crystalline form, indication patents) is strong
enough not to be worth attacking. **That is better evidence than the patent list itself.**

**Caveats, stated plainly:**
1. The settlement binds **MSN only**. Other ANDA filers are unbound and may challenge the
   post-2034 estate independently.
2. Secondary patents (polymorph, method-of-use) are materially more vulnerable to invalidation
   than composition-of-matter patents. A 2034 outcome is a live downside scenario.
3. I did not open the FDA Orange Book. §15.

**Working assumption for the committee: US cliff in the window 2034–2037; 2037 as the
settled-case, 2034 as the downside case.**

deal to Western pharma — the industry report documents cross-border licensing rising from 42

(`reports/industry/2026-08-07-biotech-landscape.md`). **BeOne is the one that refused to be a
licensor and built the commercial organisation instead.** That decision cost roughly a decade
of losses and it is now the asset that cannot be replicated quickly by any of the 18A cohort.
It is also the asset that will still be standing in 2037.

**Rating: WIDE. This is the highest-conviction section in the report.**

## 6. Moat source 4 — Cost of development as a structural edge

**The claim.** "BeOne recognized early that countries like China, Korea, Australia and Poland
offered structural advantages in clinical development with a large patient population, faster
recruitment and lower trial costs," and it re-engineered development around a "Fast to
Proof-of-Concept" model, given that clinical development is **as much as 90% of total drug
development cost** ([Baillie Gifford, Q2 2026](https://www.bailliegifford.com/en/usa/non-professional-investor/insights/ic-article/2026-q2-finra-ltgg-reflections-beone-10062863/)).

**Is it real?** Yes, in the sense that the throughput is verifiable: **>24,000 patients across
>150 trials in 45 countries with a ~3,700-person internal team** (same source). Running trials
in-house rather than through a CRO is a genuine cost and cycle-time advantage, and it is
consistent with the observed speed of BeOne's pipeline (§7).

**Is it durable? No — and this is the weakest of the moat sources.** Three reasons:

1. **It is copyable and being copied.** The entire 18A cohort and every Western pharma with a
   China development arm now runs the same playbook. The industry report's finding that China
   accounts for **~90% of global ADC licensing activity** is evidence that the China
   development-cost edge has become an *industry* condition, not a *company* asset.
2. **Redomiciling to Switzerland and building US/EU manufacturing arbitrages the edge away.**
   You cannot simultaneously claim "we are a Swiss global oncology company" and "our advantage
   is cheap Chinese trials." The cost edge and the geopolitical repositioning are in tension.
3. **Regulatory acceptance of China-only data is not guaranteed.** FDA has previously rejected
   China-single-country oncology datasets. Multi-regional trials — which BeOne now runs — cost
   more, which is precisely the point of the tension above.

**Rating: NARROW, and eroding by design. Do not underwrite this as a source of terminal value.**

## 7. Moat source 5 — Pipeline depth and self-cannibalisation (the relay-race test)

Per §2, this is where the moat either continues past 2037 or ends. **The evidence here is
better than I expected, and it is the reason this report is not a Narrow-and-stable.**

### 7.1 The baton is visibly being passed — twice

**BEQALZI (sonrotoclax) was approved by FDA on 13 May 2026** — accelerated approval, adults
with relapsed/refractory mantle cell lymphoma after ≥2 prior lines including a BTK inhibitor;
**the first and only BCL2 inhibitor approved in R/R MCL**
([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-sonrotoclax-relapsed-or-refractory-mantle-cell-lymphoma);
[BeOne 8-K, 13 May 2026 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001651308/000165130826000012/bgne-20260513.htm);
[Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/beone-venclexta-challenger-beqalzi-sonrotoclax-nets-fda-approval-first-bcl-2-mantle-cell)).
Continued approval is contingent on the confirmatory **CELESTIAL-RRMCL** trial (NCT06742996).
Two Phase 3s are running in **previously untreated CLL**, the first pitting
**BRUKINSA + BEQALZI against Gazyva + Venclexta**, with MRD data expected in 2026 that could
support a filing (same Fierce source).

**BGB-16673 (catadegbrutinib) is the most advanced BTK degrader in the clinic, with >1,100
patients dosed**, discovered in-house on BeOne's CDAC platform, and is now **preparing Phase 3
in combination with sonrotoclax in R/R CLL**
([ApexOnco/OncologyPipeline](https://www.oncologypipeline.com/apexonco/beone-looks-degrader-combo);
[PatSnap Eureka, EHA 2026](https://eureka.patsnap.com/blog/life-science/eha-2026-bgb-16673-competitive-landscape-analysis/);
[BeOne Medical Affairs](https://beonemedaffairs.com/us/molecules/bgb-16673/)).

Beyond haematology, management reported on the Q2 2026 call that **five solid-tumour programmes
have achieved proof of concept and are moving to pivotal trials** (CDK4, B7-H4 ADC,
GPC3 × 4-1BB) ([GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9029559/beone-medicines-ltd-onc-q2-2026-earnings-call-highlights-revenue-surges-30-to-17-billion-guidance-raised-on-brukinsa-momentum)).
The 9.6% base rate applies here, and should be applied here.

### 7.2 Is it cannibalising itself on schedule?

**Yes — it is the only company doing so across all three relevant mechanisms.** BeOne holds
next-generation covalent BTKi (zanubrutinib) **+** next-generation BCL2 (sonrotoclax) **+** the
leading BTK degrader (BGB-16673), spanning first-line, relapsed/refractory and post-BTKi-failure
salvage ([PatSnap Eureka](https://eureka.patsnap.com/blog/life-science/eha-2026-bgb-16673-competitive-landscape-analysis/)).
Management's own framing on the Q2 call — "only BeOne is equipped to provide the best-in-class
therapies as monotherapy or in combination for every CLL patient... regardless of stage, risk
status or treatment preference" — is self-serving but, on the portfolio facts, substantially
accurate.

**This is the correct structure.** The company whose franchise is threatened by BTK degraders
owns the leading BTK degrader. That is what disciplined self-cannibalisation looks like, and it
is the opposite of what Intel did (see `reports/equity/2026-07-28-intc-moat.md`).

### 7.3 The steel-man: the disruption case, at full strength

Per my standing rule, here is the strongest version of the bear case, not a straw one.

**(a) The degrader race is not won, and a very well-funded competitor is in it.**
**Roche paid ~$700m for Nurix's BTK degrader bexobrutideg (NX-5948)**
([AllSci](https://allsci.com/news/licensing-deals/roches-usd-700m-deal-for-nurixs-btk-degrader-clears-antitrust-review/)).
NX-5948 has a **Phase 3 running against pirtobrutinib — the first BTK degrader Phase 3 in the
industry** — and a positive readout "redefines the post-covalent BTKi market"
([PatSnap Eureka, Nurix](https://eureka.patsnap.com/blog/life-science/eha-2026-nurix-therapeutics-competitive-landscape-analysis/)).
Degradation eliminates **both the kinase and the scaffolding function of BTK**, potentially
overcoming resistance beyond C481S — a mechanistic step-change, not an incremental one. If
Roche wins the degrader class, BeOne's covalent franchise is disrupted by someone else's drug
*and* its own degrader is second. **Roche's commercial reach is larger than BeOne's.** This is
the single most credible technological threat and I do not want it softened.

**(b) The revenue model itself is the thing being disrupted — fixed duration versus
continuous.** BRUKINSA is **continuous therapy**: revenue per patient is a function of *time on
drug*. Fixed-duration BCL2-based regimens (12–24 months, MRD-guided stop) are where CLL
treatment is going, and **BeOne's own CELESTIAL-TNCLL programme is pushing it there.** A
successful BRUKINSA + BEQALZI fixed-duration regimen could win share while **shortening the
average revenue-generating treatment episode**. Winning the clinical argument and losing
revenue-per-patient is a real and under-modelled outcome. **This is my highest-conviction
non-consensus concern and I have not seen it priced anywhere.**

**Rating for this moat source: NARROW–WIDE, WIDENING. Execution to date has been genuinely
good; the outcome is not yet determined.**

implications for European capital access and geopolitical scrutiny
([Vision Lifesciences](https://visionlifesciences.com/insights/beigene-china-biotech-partnerships-pipeline);
[Bloomberg, Nov 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-11-15/china-origin-drugmaker-beigene-to-rename-itself-amid-global-push)).

**My mandate is the competitive question, not the legal one** (The dictator, Dept 4, covers
legal mechanics). Competitively:

**What genuinely works.**
1. **The manufacturing answer is substantive, not cosmetic.** US manufacturing at **Hopewell,
   New Jersey**, plus expanding European capacity, is cited as providing supply-chain
   resilience and **BIOSECURE Act compliance for US government contracts**
   ([Vision Lifesciences](https://visionlifesciences.com/insights/beigene-china-biotech-partnerships-pipeline)).
   A US-manufactured, Swiss-domiciled product is a categorically different procurement object
   from a China-manufactured one. This is capex, not branding.
2. **The revenue mix is doing the real de-risking.** China fell to ~29% of Q2 2026 revenue and
   is the slowest-growing geography (§4.2). Political risk attaches to *revenue dependence*,
   and BeOne's dependence is shrinking every quarter.
3. **Contrast with 2359.HK (WuXi AppTec)**, which the firm already covers: WuXi was **added to
   the DoW Section 1260H list on 8 June 2026** and has **~72% US revenue exposure** with no
   ability to diversify away from the risk (`reports/industry/2026-08-07-biotech-landscape.md`).
   **BeOne has not been named on 1260H and is not a designated biotechnology company of concern
   on any evidence I found.** Structurally, BIOSECURE targets *service providers* handling US
   patient genomic data — a CRO/CDMO problem, not a drug-owner problem.

**What does not work.**
1. **A Swiss address does not relocate R&D, manufacturing, or the founding team.** The company
   "retains deep operational roots in China," reflecting its origins as a Sino-American
   enterprise ([Vision Lifesciences](https://visionlifesciences.com/insights/beigene-china-biotech-partnerships-pipeline)).
   A hostile political actor does not need the legal domicile to make the argument.
2. **Legislative risk can be re-scoped at will.** The industry report identifies as the sector's
   biggest unpriced tail risk that BIOSECURE-style restrictions could be extended **from
   services to in-licensed assets** (`reports/industry/2026-08-07-biotech-landscape.md`, §10).
   A statute written around "molecules originating in China" catches BeOne regardless of Basel.
3. **A dual HK/Nasdaq/STAR listing keeps the stock inside the China-risk trade.** 6160.HK is a
   **Hang Seng Biotech constituent** ([HSI, May 2026 index review](https://www.hsi.com.hk/static/uploads/contents/en/news/pressRelease/20260522T174500.pdf)).
   Index flows will treat it as China exposure whatever the articles of association say.

**Competitive verdict: the redomiciliation converts the liability to roughly *neutral for
commercial purposes* and *unresolved for political purposes*.** It removes friction in EU
procurement and US government contracting; it does not remove the possibility of a
purpose-built statute. **Score it as a partial success — worth 2 of a possible 5 points.**
It is not a *source* of competitive advantage; it is the removal of a discount.

### 9.2 What I can and cannot compute

**My calculations, explicitly marked as estimates:**

- **Pre-tax operating return on total assets ≈ 12.3%** — FY2026 guided operating income
  midpoint $1.05bn ÷ total assets $8.55bn. *Estimate; uses guidance, and a period-start asset
  base.*

- **True ROIC is higher than the ROA figure** because total assets include ~$3.79bn of
  liabilities, much of it non-interest-bearing payables. **I could not obtain the debt
  breakdown and therefore cannot compute a defensible invested-capital denominator (§15).**

**Two accounting caveats that cut in opposite directions and largely offset:** R&D is expensed,
so the accounting asset base *understates* real investment (flattering ROIC); but a large
accumulated deficit *understates* book equity (also flattering ROIC). Neither is a reason to
adjust the headline; both are reasons not to over-read it to a decimal place.

### 9.3 The judgement

**Gross margin in the high-80% range with revenue growing 30%+ is unambiguous pricing power** —
the specific evidence my mandate demands, and it is present. Operating income guided to
**more than double**, from $447m to $1.0–1.1bn, on 28% revenue growth, is operating leverage of
the kind that only appears when a fixed commercial platform is being amortised over a rising
revenue base. **That is the numerical signature of the §5 moat.**

**But: BeOne has been GAAP-profitable for roughly six quarters.** My rule says *sustained* ROIC
above cost of capital. **Six quarters is not "sustained."** It is a promising start on a record
that does not yet exist. I am not going to grade a moat wide on eighteen months of returns
however good the trajectory, and the committee should hold me to that if I am tempted later.

**Rating: the ROIC evidence is POSITIVE IN LEVEL AND DIRECTION, INSUFFICIENT IN DURATION.**
This costs BeOne 6 points and is the main reason the score is 68 rather than 78.

## 10. Threat assessment — the top 2 credible threats

### Threat 1 (technology shift): Roche/Nurix win the BTK degrader class — **Medium probability, High impact**

Detailed in §7.3(a). Roche's **~$700m deal for bexobrutideg (NX-5948)** and the **first BTK
degrader Phase 3 in the industry, running against pirtobrutinib**, put a competitor with
greater commercial reach on a credible path to owning the mechanism that supersedes covalent
BTK inhibition ([AllSci](https://allsci.com/news/licensing-deals/roches-usd-700m-deal-for-nurixs-btk-degrader-clears-antitrust-review/);
[PatSnap Eureka, Nurix](https://eureka.patsnap.com/blog/life-science/eha-2026-nurix-therapeutics-competitive-landscape-analysis/)).

**Why it is only Medium probability:** BeOne's BGB-16673 has **>1,100 patients dosed** and is
described as the most advanced degrader in the clinic. BeOne is not being ambushed; it is in a
two-horse race it may well win.
**Why the impact is High anyway:** if BeOne comes second in degraders while its covalent
franchise is being superseded, it loses the mechanism *and* the franchise in the same decade —
and the 2034–2037 patent runway becomes irrelevant because the drug is obsolete before it is
generic. **The patent cliff is the *floor* on the moat's life, not the ceiling. Obsolescence
can arrive first, and usually does in oncology.**

**Leading indicator to monitor:** NX-5948 Phase 3 readout timing versus BGB-16673 Phase 3
initiation. Whoever files first probably takes the class.

### Threat 2 (regulation/pricing): US Medicare negotiation asymmetry — **High probability, Medium impact**

This is my **erosion call made before the numbers show it**, and it is the one I most want on
the record.

**Sourced facts:** Medicare negotiation eligibility requires >9 years on market for
small-molecule drugs with no generic. **Imbruvica (ibrutinib) was selected in cycle 1.**
**Acalabrutinib was selected in cycle 2, among the 15 Part D drugs for IPAY 2027.**
**Zanubrutinib has not been selected in either cycle**
([OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/second-cycle-of-medicare-drug-price-negotiations-includes-4-oncologic-agents);
[Managed Healthcare Executive](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/medicare-drug-price-negotiation-has-arrived-now-what-);
[IPAY 2027 selected-drug analysis, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12467749/)).

**My inference — clearly labelled as inference, not source:** BRUKINSA was FDA-approved in
November 2019, so it becomes selection-eligible on the 9-year small-molecule test around
2028–2029, with a maximum fair price applying in the early 2030s. **That means acalabrutinib
gets a mandated lower Medicare price roughly two to four years before zanubrutinib does.**

**Why this matters and why nobody is discussing it.** CLL is overwhelmingly a disease of the
elderly; Medicare is the dominant payer. **A negotiated Medicare price on Calquence while
Brukinsa remains at list inverts the cost-effectiveness argument in BeOne's largest and most
important patient segment** — the very argument BeOne currently uses to sell the drug
([Targeted Oncology on zanubrutinib's cost benefit](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/zanubrutinib-offers-clinical-and-cost-benefits-over-acalabrutinib-in-r-r-cll)).
Payer-driven share loss does not require any clinical event. **Watch US BRUKINSA
new-patient-start share in the Medicare channel from 2027; it will move before revenue does.**

This sits on top of the sector-wide pricing regime documented in the industry report: the
**2 April 2026 Section 232 proclamation imposing 0–100% tariffs on patented pharmaceuticals**,
with the 0% rate conditional on an MFN agreement plus onshoring and **expiring 20 January 2029**
(`reports/industry/2026-08-07-biotech-landscape.md`, §6.2). BeOne's Hopewell NJ manufacturing is
directly relevant to that calculus and is a genuine mitigant.

### Threats considered and ranked below the top two

- **China NRDL price erosion** — real, but already in the numbers (China +17% vs group +30%)
  and shrinking as a share of revenue. Known, visible, diminishing.
- **BIOSECURE / 1260H extension to BeOne** — low probability on current evidence (§8), but the
  impact would be severe. This is a tail, not a base case, and Dept 4 owns it.
- **Fixed-duration therapy compressing revenue per patient** — see §7.3(b). Arguably belongs in
  the top two; I placed it below only because BeOne controls the timing of its own transition.

## 13. Margin of safety required

**I require a 30% margin of safety to 𢦀鳩仔's central intrinsic-value estimate.**

**Derivation, and why 30% and not 60%:**

| Factor | Effect on required MoS |
|---|---|
| Approved, cash-generating products carrying ~80% of revenue — **no approval risk on the base business** | Reduces |
| Demonstrated pricing power (high-80s gross margin) and 30%+ growth | Reduces |
| Six quarters of GAAP profitability only — short earnings history to anchor a DCF | **Increases** |
| **A terminal value that must be argued, not extrapolated** — the moat has a dated expiry | **Increases materially** |
| Binary technology race (degraders) with a well-funded rival | **Increases** |
| Geopolitical tail risk that cannot be hedged inside the business | **Increases** |
| EU/China exclusivity dates unknown to me today | **Increases** |

**Guidance to the valuation team (𢦀鳩仔):**

1. **Do not run a perpetuity growth model on BRUKINSA.** Model it as an annuity terminating in
   the 2034–2037 window with an explicit erosion curve, and value the platform separately.
   Any DCF whose terminal value exceeds ~40% of the total is not describing this business.
2. **Run the cliff as a scenario pair, not a point estimate:** 2037 base case (settled), 2034
   downside. My rough sense is that the difference between them is a large fraction of
   enterprise value, which is precisely why §4 is flagged as the report's weakest sourcing and
   its highest-value open question.
3. **Value the commercial platform (§5) explicitly and separately from the molecule.** It is the
   part that survives 2037 and it is what justifies any terminal value at all.
4. **Apply the 9.6% base rate to the pipeline, and only the pipeline.** The five solid-tumour
   programmes at proof of concept should carry a probability-weighted value well below face.
5. **The Owner's HK$219.60 cost is irrelevant to this analysis.** At HK$211.20 the position is
   3.8% underwater; that fact tells us nothing about the moat and must not influence sizing.

**Position implication (my view, not a recommendation — committee decides):** the moat supports
**holding and, at a sufficient discount, adding.** It does not support a valuation that
capitalises current growth into perpetuity. If the valuation team's central estimate is not at
least ~43% above the current price (i.e. price ≤ 70% of value), the moat does not close the gap.
