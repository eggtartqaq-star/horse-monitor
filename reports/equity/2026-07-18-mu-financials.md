## Summary & Grade

Micron is printing the best numbers in the history of the memory industry: FQ3 FY26 revenue of **$41.46B** (+345% y/y), GAAP gross margin **84.9%**, operating margin **81.2%**, GAAP net income **$28.24B** ($24.67 diluted EPS), operating cash flow **$25.39B**, and record FCF **$18.3B** — with guidance to **$50B revenue / ~86% GM / ~$31 EPS** for FQ4. The balance sheet flipped to a large net-cash position ($24.4B) after a $4.4B debt paydown. Sixteen take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs) claim **$100B of minimum multi-year revenue** with **$22B of customer deposits/commitments**.

The caveat is the reason this is not an automatic A: these are **peak-cycle economics in the most cyclical large-cap business in tech**. Three fiscal years ago (FY2023) this same company lost money and wrote down $1.83B of inventory; FY2024 GAAP EPS was $0.70. An 85% gross margin in a commodity-rooted industry is unprecedented and mean-reverting by nature; the SCA floor prices and deposits mitigate but do not repeal the cycle. Also note that a large share of the headline "net cash" is refundable customer deposits (financing inflows), not earned cash.

**Fundamentals quality grade: B+** (current statements: A; through-cycle durability and peak-margin sustainability: C — blended B+).

**The 3 numbers that matter:**
1. **84.9% GAAP gross margin (FQ3-26) vs ~22% GM in FY2024** — the entire thesis lives or dies on where mid-cycle margin settles; the amplitude of this cycle is unprecedented.
2. **$100B minimum take-or-pay SCA revenue with $22B of deposits/commitments ($18B cash + $4B LCs)** — the structural claim that "this cycle is different"; watch enforcement and floor-price levels.
3. **FQ3-26 FCF $18.3B vs FY2026 capex plan ~$27B** — for the first time, Micron self-funds an aggressive HBM buildout with room to spare.

- Equity: $54.165B at FY25 end (2025-08-28) → **$100.724B at 2026-05-28** [FQ3-26 10-Q]. Equity nearly doubled in 9 months from retained earnings.
- **ROE:** FY2025 ≈ 15.8% on ending equity (8.539/54.165). FY26 YTD annualized ≈ **80%+** on average equity (calc). FQ3-26 annualized ≈ 120%+ (calc). These are peak-cycle artifacts, not sustainable run-rates.
- **ROIC (calc):** FQ3-26 NOPAT ≈ $33.7B × (1–15%) ≈ $28.6B on invested capital ≈ equity $100.7B + debt $5.7B – cash/investments $30.2B ≈ $76.2B → **annualized ROIC well above 100%**. Through-cycle ROIC (FY21–FY25 average) is more honestly in the **high single digits to low teens**, since FY23–24 returns were near zero/negative.

## Cash Generation

- **OCF:** FY24 $8.51B → FY25 $17.53B → FY26 YTD $45.70B (8.41+11.90+25.39, calc).
- **Capex intensity (HBM buildout):** FY25 net capex $13.80B (adj. FCF only $3.72B — capex ate ~79% of OCF). FY26 plan **~$27B** (~$10B in FQ4 alone). Despite this, FY26 YTD adj. FCF ≈ **$29.1B** (3.9+6.9+18.3, calc). The model has crossed from capex-consuming to strongly self-funding.
- **FCF conversion (FQ3-26):** FCF $18.3B / GAAP NI $28.24B ≈ 65% (calc) — depressed by capex, not by working-capital games; OCF/NI ≈ 90%.
- **Critical nuance:** the **$22B of SCA customer deposits are NOT in FCF or OCF** — CFO Mark Murphy explicitly stated deposits flow through **financing** activities and will be **returned to customers in the latter half of the agreement terms**. This is honest presentation (a plus for accounting quality), but it means reported cash balances overstate Micron's own money (see below). A further ~$10B of deposits is expected in FQ4.
- Dividend: raised 30% to $0.15/quarter (announced with FQ2-26) — trivial payout; capital return optionality is large but unexercised at scale.

## Balance Sheet & Debt

- **Cash & investments:** record **$30.2B** at 2026-05-28.
- **Debt:** reduced by **$4.4B in FQ3-26** (incl. $4.3B senior-notes tender), ending at **$5.7B** total debt → **net cash $24.4B**. For reference, total debt was $14.577B at FY25 end ($0.56B current + $14.017B long-term) [FY25 10-K]. Remaining maturity/coupon schedule of the residual $5.7B: **unverified** this session (10-Q detail inaccessible); immaterial to solvency given interest expense is a rounding error against $33.7B quarterly operating income. Coverage is effectively infinite.
- **Deposit-adjusted view (my calc):** if ~$18B of the quarter-end cash is refundable SCA cash deposits, Micron's *own* net cash is closer to **~$6B**, not $24.4B. The deposits are cheap financing with demand-visibility attached — good — but they are a liability, and the committee should not count them as distributable cash.
- **Equity:** $100.724B (common stock $128M, additional capital $14.442B, retained earnings $94.682B, treasury stock –$8.502B) at 2026-05-28 [FQ3-26 10-Q].

## Accounting Red Flags Screen

| Check | Finding |
|---|---|
| Earnings vs OCF divergence | Minor and benign: FQ3 OCF $25.39B vs NI $28.24B (~90%); gap consistent with receivables growth when revenue nearly doubles q/q. FY26 YTD OCF broadly tracks NI. **No smoothing signature.** |
| Inventory (the classic memory landmine) | FQ3-26 inventory **$8.6B, ~120 days** — flat-to-down in dollars while revenue exploded; low writedown risk *today*. History says watch this line first when the cycle turns (FY23: $1.83B writedowns). Days computed on COGS, which is thin at 85% GM — a demand shock would balloon DIO fast. |
| Receivables vs revenue | Level **unverified** (10-Q inaccessible). Mechanically rising with revenue; no third-party flags found. Follow-up item. |
| Customer deposits classification | **Clean**: $18B cash deposits booked as financing inflows, excluded from FCF, disclosed as refundable. Transparent treatment; but headline cash is inflated by customer money — adjust in any per-share cash math. |
| Adjusted-figure addbacks | GAAP vs non-GAAP gap is small (FQ3: $24.67 vs $25.11, ~1.8%) — no aggressive addback culture. |
| Share-count creep | Diluted shares ≈ 1,145M (calc: 28,240/24.67) vs ≈ 1,111M in FY24 (calc: 778/0.70) — ~1%/yr SBC creep, partially offset historically by buybacks ($8.5B treasury stock). Modest, monitor. |
| Capitalization games | None evident; capex is genuinely heavy and disclosed ($27B FY26 plan). Depreciation will step up hard into FY27 — a real (non-scandalous) margin headwind. |
| Revenue quality | Take-or-pay SCAs with floor prices covering ~20% of DRAM / ~one-third of NAND volume and $100B minimum revenue: genuine structural improvement, but enforceability in a glut and the actual floor-price levels are undisclosed — **the single biggest "trust me" item in the story.** |

**Net: no accounting red flags found. All risks here are cycle risks, not integrity risks.**

**Rationale:**
- Profitability (current): 20/20 — record everything; 84.9% GM, 81.2% OM, guided higher.
- Cash generation: 18/20 — $18.3B quarterly FCF, self-funded $27B capex; slight deduction for deposit-flattered optics and imminent depreciation step-up.
- Balance sheet: 18/20 — net cash, debt tendered down to $5.7B; deduction because ~$18B of cash is refundable customer deposits.
- Accounting quality: 14/15 — clean screen, conservative deposit treatment, small GAAP/non-GAAP gap; receivables detail unverified.
- Through-cycle durability: 12/25 — the heavy penalty. FY23 loss + writedowns is 3 years old, not 30. Peak margins in memory have never held; SCAs are promising but untested through a downturn, and FY26 economics should not be extrapolated as mid-cycle.
