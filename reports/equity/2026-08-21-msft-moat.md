## 2. The trap: why "wide moat" is not an answer

The brief is explicit and correct. Everyone agrees Microsoft has a wide moat. A report that
concludes "wide moat, high score" transfers no information to the Owner.

So state the question properly. **The interesting question about Microsoft in 2026 is not
whether the moat is wide. It is whether the moat is being *bought* rather than *earned*.**

A moat, in this house's definition, is a structural feature that lets a business earn returns
far above its cost of capital *without* proportionate reinvestment. That is the whole point:
the fortress is already built. What Microsoft is doing right now — $116bn of capex in FY2026,
roughly **$175bn of calendar-2026 capital spending** planned — is the behaviour of a company
that must *purchase* its competitive position annually, in cash, in a capital-intensive,
physically-depreciating, commodity-input-exposed asset class.

That is a *different kind of business* from the one that earned 45% ROIC on software licences
in 2021. **The software moat and the AI-infrastructure moat are not the same moat**, and the
second is structurally worse than the first. §3 shows this is already visible in the returns.

The rest of this report is organised around one question: **is the enterprise software
franchise (high-return, moat-like) subsidising an AI infrastructure land-grab (low-return,
scale-like), and if so, is the Owner being asked to pay a software multiple for a business that
is becoming a hybrid?**

## 3. The ROIC gate — no moat without it

My standing rule: no moat without ROIC evidence. Applied to Microsoft, the level passes easily
and **the trajectory is the single most important finding in this report.**

### 3.1 The level

| Metric | FY2026 (yr to 30 Jun 2026) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | **$331.8bn, +18%** (+16% cc) | [Microsoft FY26 Q4 press release](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast); [8-K exhibit 99.1 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000789019/000119312526323632/msft-ex99_1.htm) |
| Operating income | **$155.2bn, +21%** (+19% cc) | same |
| Operating margin | **~46.8%** (my calculation: 155.2 ÷ 331.8) | derived |
| Net income | **$133.7bn, +31% GAAP** (+22% non-GAAP) | same |
| Q4 FY26 revenue | $90bn, +18%, above consensus $87.72bn | [Futurum](https://futurumgroup.com/insights/microsoft-q4-fy-2026-ai-demand-accelerates-azure-and-enterprise-growth/) |
| Q4 FY26 operating income | $40.6bn, +18% | same |
| **Azure and other cloud services** | **surpassed $100bn annual revenue for the first time**; Q4 growth **+43%** | [Microsoft FY26 Q4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast); [Tickeron](https://tickeron.com/earnings/MSFT/) |

A 46.8% operating margin on $332bn of revenue is not a normal corporate outcome. It is prima
facie evidence of pricing power on a scale almost nothing else in this coverage universe
matches. **The level test passes.**

### 3.2 The trajectory — this is where the report earns its keep

| Fiscal year | ROIC | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2020 | **43.4%** | [ValueSense ROIC history](https://valuesense.io/ticker/msft/roic) |
| 2021 | **45.6%** | same |
| 2022 | 36.6% | same |
| 2023 | 28.7% | same |
| 2024 | 25.7% | same |
| 2025 | 23.8% | same |
| **2026 (TTM)** | **20.5%** (GuruFocus, as of Jun 2026) / 23.3% (Alpha Spread) / 26.1% (ValueSense, Mar 2026) | [GuruFocus ROIC](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/roic/MSFT); [Alpha Spread](https://www.alphaspread.com/security/nasdaq/msft/profitability/ratio/return-on-invested-capital); [ValueSense](https://valuesense.io/ticker/msft/roic) |

**Microsoft's return on invested capital has roughly halved in five years.** The three vendors
disagree on the 2026 point estimate by six percentage points (20.5% / 23.3% / 26.1%) — I do not
have a primary invested-capital reconciliation and will not pretend to one (§15) — **but all
three agree on the direction, and the direction is down every single year since FY2021.**

Set that against the cost of capital. GuruFocus computes **WACC 12.06% as of June 2026, versus
a 10-year median of 9.51% and a 10-year range of 5.61%–12.06% — i.e. Microsoft's cost of
capital is at its decade high** ([GuruFocus WACC](https://www.gurufocus.com/term/wacc/MSFT)).

**The spread is therefore compressing from both ends simultaneously.**

| | FY2021 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|
| ROIC | ~45.6% | ~20.5–26.1% |
| WACC (est.) | nearer the ~9.5% median | **12.06%, decade high** |
| **Spread** | **~36pp** | **~8–14pp** |

*The FY2021 WACC figure is my inference from the stated 10-year median and range, not a sourced
point estimate — marked as an estimate.*

**A spread of 8–14 points is still a moat.** It is emphatically not nothing; most businesses
never earn their cost of capital at all. But it is a **quarter to a third** of the spread the
same company earned five years ago, and the compression is monotonic.

### 3.3 The cause is not mysterious. It is the denominator.

| Metric | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| FY2026 capital expenditure | **~$116bn** ($115.95bn), **up ~80%** from **$64.55bn** in FY2025 | [Hallucination Yield capex tracker](https://www.hallucinationyield.com/research/microsoft-capex/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/07/30/microsoft-earnings-q4-26-cloud-brings-revenue-rain/5280798) |
| Property and equipment (gross) | **$313.1bn**, from **$205.0bn** a year earlier | same |
| Q4 FY26 capital spending | **record ~$41bn** | [CNBC, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/microsoft-msft-q4-earnings-report-2026.html) |
| Q1 FY27 capex guidance | **>$50bn** | [Hallucination Yield](https://www.hallucinationyield.com/research/microsoft-capex/) |
| Calendar-2026 capital spending plan | **~$175bn** (after a datacentre lease reclassification; an earlier figure of ~$190bn was cited in April 2026 on soaring memory prices) | [Converge Digest](https://convergedigest.com/microsoft-plans-175-billion-in-2026-capital-spending/); [CNBC, 29 Apr 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/29/microsoft-msft-q3-earnings-report-2026.html) |
| Finance leases FY26 | $5.6bn, primarily large datacentre sites | [Hallucination Yield](https://www.hallucinationyield.com/research/microsoft-capex/) |

**Note the accounting change and treat the capex headline with care.** Microsoft has shifted
more future datacentre leases from *finance* leases to *operating* leases; finance leases are
included in capex, operating leases are not. That reclassification is why the calendar-2026
number reads $175bn rather than ~$190bn. **The economic commitment did not fall by $15bn; the
reported capex line did.** Anyone tracking "capex is moderating" needs to check the lease mix
before believing it. I flag this as a presentation risk, not an accusation.

### 3.4 It is also showing up in the numerator — margins

| Metric | Q4 FY26 | Q4 FY25 | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Microsoft Cloud gross margin** | **65%** | 68.6% | [Microsoft FY26 Q4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast); [Encyclopedia of Stocks](https://encyclopediaofstocks.substack.com/p/microsoft-corporation-msft-q4-fy26) |
| **Company gross margin** | **~67.6%** — *lowest since 2022* | 68.7% | same |

Management attributes the compression to "sales mix shift to Azure as well as continued
investments in AI infrastructure and growing product usage, partially offset by ongoing
efficiency gains" (FY26 Q4 commentary, via the sources above).

**Read that attribution carefully, because it is an admission.** My mandate asks for
*stable-or-rising* gross margins as moat evidence. Microsoft's are falling, and management's own
explanation is that **the fastest-growing part of the business is the lower-margin part.** That
is the definition of negative mix. Growth is real; it is arriving at a structurally worse gross
margin than the franchise it is diluting.

### 3.5 Verdict on the ROIC gate

**PASSES ON LEVEL. FAILS THE "STABLE-OR-RISING MARGIN" TEST OUTRIGHT. TRAJECTORY NEGATIVE ON
BOTH ROIC AND GROSS MARGIN, MONOTONICALLY, FOR FIVE YEARS.**

This is the numerical spine of the report and I want it stated without hedging: **on the two
quantitative tests my own mandate specifies — sustained ROIC above cost of capital, and
stable-or-rising gross margins — Microsoft passes the first and fails the second.** The moat is
wide. It is also, measured the only way this desk permits it to be measured, **narrower than it
was in 2021 and getting narrower each year.**
