## 4. How the mandated items are handled

### 4.1 GAAP, not adjusted EPS
The DCF is built on **GAAP operating income, with SBC treated as an operating cost** (A13). Adjusted operating income is 59% above GAAP on a TTM basis, and SBC is about 76% of that gap [F]. Using adjusted margins is worth **+US$52 per ADS** (§8.3, step 3). That is the single largest difference between this model and a street model that capitalises adjusted EPS. **P/E comparisons below use GAAP EPS only.**

### 4.2 Dilution of 3–4% a year, without double counting
Expensing SBC **and** projecting 3–4% share growth would charge the same cost twice. The approach:
- **Observed:** diluted ADSs are up ~3.4% YoY [F].
- **Already charged:** SBC expense of ~US$0.52bn a year ÷ a US$49bn market cap ≈ **1.1%** of the equity a year [C]. This sits inside GAAP EBIT.
- **Residual ~2.3%:** this is partly a **treasury-stock-method artefact.** The ADS is up ~37% YTD [F technical], which pulls more in-the-money options into the diluted count. It is also partly old low-strike options vesting and exercising (for example, Oyler's exercise at US$9.23 per ordinary share [F management]). The overhang that already exists is captured by using the **diluted** count of 118M today.
- **Forward excess dilution:** **1.0% a year for 10 years in the base**, the full residual 2.3% in the bear, 0% in the bull. Value is divided by the growing ADS count year by year. This departs from 巴爺爺's caution 4 ("model about 3% a year of ADS-count growth"; financials update §11). The departure raises value (+US$14 against a 2.3% excess path; §9.4), and it is the main bullish-leaning discretionary choice in this model. *(audit V6-4)*
- **Effect:** base US$201 vs **US$213 with no excess dilution** (−6%). The full 2.3% gives US$187 (−7%).
- **The HKEX basic count of 1,469M at 30 Jun 2026 is not used.** It may include depositary-reserve shares [F §6].

### 4.3 The one-off tax gain is stripped
Management has flagged a possible material GAAP benefit from releasing valuation allowances [F Flag 8]. It is **non-cash and non-recurring.**
- **The DCF works off EBIT and cash tax, so a release changes nothing in it.**
- The P/E inputs (TTM US$7.88; FY26E US$9.08; FY27E US$10.53) are built from operating income × NI/EBIT conversion of 0.82, between TTM 0.78 and 9M 0.855 [C]. **They exclude any release.** If Q4'26 reported EPS jumps by billions of dollars, **ignore it for multiples.**
- The **economic** value of the tax attributes (lower future cash tax) is real but unconfirmed. Partial credit sits in A11. The upside is quantified in §3.

### 4.4 BRUKINSA concentration (73.6%) and BTK competition
Concentration is modelled **structurally**, not by adding to the discount rate:
- BRUKINSA is a **separate revenue line with a dated LOE and an erosion curve** (A2, A3).
- The **sum-of-the-parts split** (§6) shows how much of the value it carries.
- The competition items enter through dated deceleration in A2: **pirtobrutinib's frontline label** (2 Oct 2026); **Calquence's Medicare negotiated price from 2027**, which inverts BRUKINSA's cost argument (moat Threat 2); **tacabrutideg and fixed-duration regimens** cannibalising before LOE (−2% and −3% in 2035–36). In the bear, **Roche/Nurix winning the degrader class** shows up as the platform growing only 3–8% a year.

### 4.5 The unquantified US pricing-agreement cost
Nobody has quantified it [F Flag 9; news A3.1]. I therefore model it as an **explicit, visible haircut** rather than ignoring it:
- **−5% on platform revenue from 2028 in the base** (future launches priced to developed-market levels); −10% in the bear; 0% in the bull.
- A **−3% BRUKINSA price haircut** in the bear only.
- **Each 5 points of platform haircut is worth about ±US$9–10 per ADS** (one-at-a-time: 0% gives US$210, 10% gives US$191).

### 4.6 Net cash of US$3.7–4.8bn
Added at face value: **US$4.8bn (base and bull), US$3.7bn (bear).** The difference is **US$9.3 per ADS** (−5%). The aggregator's US$2.05bn total-debt figure, which probably includes leases, is the reason for the conservative case [F §6].
