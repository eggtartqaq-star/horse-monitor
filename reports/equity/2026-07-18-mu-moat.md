# MU (Micron Technology) — Economic Moat Analysis

**Analyst:** 菲比斯, Moat Analyst, Dept. 2 (Equity Research) · **Date:** 2026-07-18
**Reporting to:** CIO · **For:** Investment Committee / 𢦀鳩仔 (Valuation)

---

## Moat rating: NARROW, WIDENING — with an explicit through-cycle asterisk

Micron historically had **no moat** by our ROIC test. The HBM era plus the 3-player DRAM
oligopoly has created a **narrow** moat that is currently **widening**, but the moat has
not yet been proven through a single full downcycle. Current peak-cycle economics
(85% gross margin) are shortage economics, not moat evidence, and must not be capitalized.

## 2. Evidence by moat source

### a) Oligopoly / capital-intensity & scale barriers — **Narrow, stable-to-widening**
- Three players (Samsung, SK hynix, Micron) control ~90%+ of DRAM revenue. Shares have
  been durable across cycles: Micron ~25–26% of DRAM revenue through 2025 (Q1-25: SKH 36%,
  Samsung 34%, MU 25%; Q3-25: 34/33/26; Q1-26: Samsung 38%, SKH 29%).
- Leading-edge DRAM requires ~$15–25B fabs, EUV, and decades of process learning;
  Micron's FY2026 capex is ~$27B (FQ4 guide ~$10B). No Western/Japanese entrant has
  succeeded in 25 years — the only credible entrant is state-funded CXMT (see threats).
- **Strongest evidence:** share persistence across the 2023 trough (Micron kept ~25%
  despite negative margins). **Counter-evidence:** CXMT went ~4% → ~8% of DRAM share in
  a year and approaches Micron's wafer capacity (~350k vs ~385k wspm by end-2026).

### b) Switching costs / customer qualification lock-in (HBM) — **Narrow, widening; strongest source**
- HBM is qualification-gated (thermal, signal-integrity, base-die co-design with the
  GPU/ASIC customer) and sold under multi-year, price-and-volume-locked contracts —
  structurally different from spot-traded commodity DRAM.
- Micron's **entire calendar-2026 HBM output is sold out under binding contracts**,
  including HBM4; the company cites 16 strategic customer agreements (SCAs) giving
  multi-year visibility. HBM4 (>11 Gbps, 1-beta) is shipping in volume to the lead
  customer, with the 12-high ramp tracking 2x the speed of HBM3E.
- Industry-wide, HBM4 pricing is set to roughly double (~$2/Gb → $4–5/Gb) with
  expanding LTAs — genuine contracted pricing power, at least for now.
- **Strongest evidence:** sold-out 2026 supply at locked prices; HBM share ~21%
  (Q2–Q3 2025) vs near-zero in 2023 — share gained on merit against incumbents.
- **Counter-evidence:** qualification lock-in lasts one product generation (~18–24
  months); every HBM generation is a re-bid. Samsung passed NVIDIA HBM4 qualification
  and achieved **price parity with SK hynix** (after selling HBM3E ~30% cheaper),
  and NVIDIA is dual/tri-sourcing Rubin-generation HBM4.

### c) Cost advantage / process technology — **Narrow, stable**
- Micron's 1-beta/1-gamma nodes are competitive; FY2025 DRAM margin gains were driven
  partly by "manufacturing cost reductions from product and process technology"
  ([FQ4-25 remarks](https://investors.micron.com/static-files/5ea95475-639b-4cfc-91fd-b9b4a2bb5e63)).
  HBM4 at >11 Gbps is industry-leading spec. But this is leadership *within* an
  oligopoly of peers who leapfrog each other every node — an arms race, not a durable
  cost gap. Rated narrow at best.

### d) Intellectual property — **Narrow contributor, not standalone**
- Deep patent estate and decades of DRAM process know-how are a real entry barrier
  (CXMT's struggles under export controls partly reflect this), but IP has never
  protected memory pricing between the Big 3. Cross-licensed, litigated, and leapfrogged.

### e) Brand / network effects — **None.** Memory buyers qualify parts, not logos.
Popularity of the AI trade is not a moat.

### f) Regulatory / geopolitical barriers — **Narrow, two-sided**
- US export controls slow CXMT (EUV denial) — a barrier that protects Micron at the
  leading edge. But China banned Micron from key infrastructure (2023) and CXMT enjoys
  state subsidy (~$4.3B+ backing) — regulation cuts both ways for MU.

## 3. Threat assessment (steel-manning the disruption case)

**Threat 1 — CXMT floods commodity DRAM (probability: high for legacy; medium for leading edge).**
CXMT is near Micron's wafer capacity by end-2026 (~350k vs ~385k wspm), targets 17% of
global DRAM supply by 2028, has demonstrated DDR5-8000/LPDDR5X, and will price
aggressively into PCs/phones/consumer once DDR5 yields mature (~late 2026). This
re-commoditizes the non-HBM ~50–60% of Micron's bit output and could turn the next
downcycle vicious exactly where Micron has no contract protection. Micron's mitigation
is mix-shift up the stack — which every competitor is also doing, concentrating the
Big 3's non-HBM capacity into the same premium niches.
([SemiAnalysis](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/chinas-cxmt-is-set-to-challenge-dram) ·
[Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/dram/chinas-banned-memory-maker-cxmt-unveils-surprising-new-chipmaking-capabilities-despite-crushing-us-export-restrictions-ddr5-8000-and-lpddr5x-10667-displayed))

**Threat 2 — HBM premium compression: Samsung catch-up + supply race + AI capex normalization (probability: medium-high by 2027–28).**
Samsung has passed NVIDIA HBM4 qualification, reached price parity, and is scaling
capacity alongside SK hynix for 2026 ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/samsung-and-sk-hynix-to-scale-up-memory-production-capacity-in-2026-to-meet-ai-demand/)).
Three qualified suppliers all adding capacity into one demand source (AI accelerators)
is the classic memory setup for an HBM-specific glut. An 85–86% gross margin is an
invitation for customers (NVIDIA, hyperscaler ASIC programs) to fund alternatives:
custom base dies, near-memory compute, or eventual post-DRAM architectures. If AI
infrastructure capex plateaus while 2026-committed capacity lands in 2027, HBM pricing
reverts hard. LTAs delay, not prevent, this.

**Secondary threats:** customer concentration (NVIDIA-linked demand); a technology
shift in memory hierarchy (CXL pooling, storage-class memory) diluting HBM's role.

## 4. Ten-year test — what must stay true for Micron to be stronger in 2036

1. HBM (and successors) stays **qualification-gated and co-designed** with customers,
   so it never trades like a commodity — and Micron re-wins qualification every
   generation (HBM4E, HBM5...).
2. The Big 3 maintain **capex discipline**; nobody buys share with capacity. (History:
   discipline held 2013–2021 only loosely; broke in 2022–23.)
3. **CXMT stays confined to legacy/mid-range** — i.e., export controls hold and China
   does not reach HBM-class packaging + leading-edge DRAM at scale.
4. Micron sustains node parity or leadership (1-gamma and beyond) without a process
   stumble (cf. Samsung's HBM3E stumble — it can happen to anyone).
5. AI memory demand proves secular, not a one-time datacenter build-out.
Failure of any of 1–3 likely returns Micron to sub-WACC through-cycle returns. I judge
the joint probability of all five holding for ten years at well under 50% — which is
why this is not a wide moat.

## 5. Implication for margin of safety (for 𢦀鳩仔)

- **Do not capitalize current earnings.** FQ3-26's 84.9% GM and FQ4 guide of ~86% GM /
  $50B revenue are shortage-cycle peaks. Value on **normalized through-cycle
  economics**: I suggest normalizing to mid-cycle gross margins in the 35–45% band
  (FY2025 = 41% is a reasonable anchor for "good but not euphoric") and a mid-cycle
  ROIC of ~10–15%, not FY2026's level.
- **Demand a large margin of safety: 40–50% below intrinsic value on normalized
  earnings.** Rationale: narrow moat + extreme cyclicality + two credible, dated
  threats (CXMT yield parity late 2026; HBM4 tri-source supply landing 2027) + a
  historical base rate of median ~4% ROIC.
- If the market price already embeds peak margins persisting past 2027, the margin of
  safety is negative regardless of the moat improving. The moat call here supports
  *survivability and higher normalized mid-cycle returns than history* — it does not
  support paying for 85% gross margins as a steady state.
- **Erosion tripwires to monitor** (flag to CIO before the numbers show it): CXMT DDR5
  yield reports; HBM contract price direction for 2027 supply (set ~Q4 2026); Micron's
  HBM share slipping below ~20%; Big 3 capex announcements exceeding demand growth.
