# INTC — Economic Moat Assessment
**菲比斯, Dept. 2 Equity Research · Reference date 2026-07-28 · Post-Q2'26 (reported 23 Jul 2026)**

## Verdict up front — TWO ratings

| Business | Moat rating | Trajectory |
|---|---|---|
| **Intel Products (x86 CCG + DCAI)** | **NARROW** | **ERODING** — masked by a cyclical shortage |
| **Intel Foundry** | **NONE** | **STABLE** (losses narrowing from a moatless base; no customer-derived advantage yet) |

A blended rating would be dishonest. The product business earns real money and has real switching costs; the foundry has negative returns and zero committed leading-edge external customers. They must be valued separately.

## 1. The ROIC test — the mandate's gate

| Metric | Franchise era | Decline | Now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 62.6% (2015), 58.6% (2019) | 55.5% (2021) → 32.7% (2024) → 34.8% (2025) | **40.4% GAAP / 41.8% non-GAAP (Q2'26)** |
| Operating margin | ~30%+ | **−22.0% (2024)** → −0.04% (2025) | positive, shortage-aided |
| **ROIC** | high-teens/20%+ | 14.9% (2021) → 2.4% (2022) → 0.0% (2023) → **−0.8% (2024)** → 0.5% (2025) | **2.45% TTM (Mar 2026)** |

**Estimated WACC: 9–11%** (estimate; equity-dominant structure, elevated beta).

**Finding: Intel has earned below its cost of capital for five consecutive years and remains ~7 points below it today.** Five-year average ROIC 3.4%. That is not a narrowing moat — it is outright value destruction. On the mandate's own test the *consolidated* company currently has no moat.

Two aggravating notes on the "recovery":
- **Impairments flatter the denominator.** $3.3B of charges in 2024; a **$3.9B non-cash Mobileye goodwill impairment in H1'26**. Writing down capital mechanically raises future ROIC without improving competitive position.
- **Capex is re-inflating the base.** 2026 capex guided **>$20B**, 2027 "significantly above 2026," against ~$8–9B annualised non-GAAP net income. ROIC recovery is capped by construction.

## 2. Evidence by moat source

**A. Switching costs / x86 ecosystem — NARROW, real, but the buyer changed.** Intel holds ~70% of consumer PC share and 67.4% of total x86 (Mercury, Q1'26); enterprise estates are genuinely expensive to port. But the switching cost protects **x86, not Intel** — AMD is x86 and takes share at zero porting cost. And the customer who *can* pay the porting cost already has: **Arm reached ~50% of hyperscaler CPU compute in 2026, up from ~18% in mid-2024**, with Arm servers taking >45% of data-centre CPU revenue. When your top customers employ their own CPU design teams, ISA lock-in is a legacy tax, not a moat.

**B. Share durability — FAILING, still failing in the latest print.** Total x86: 72.9% (Q1'25) → 68.6% (Q4'25) → **67.4% (Q1'26)** — ~5.5 points in a year, the fastest in the series. Server units: AMD 27.2% → 30.0% → **33.2%**. **Server revenue: AMD 46.2% vs Intel 53.8%** — near parity in the profitable half, and that measure excludes Arm entirely. Killer detail: **industry server CPU units grew >10% YoY while Intel's server shipments were flat.** Intel is not participating in the volume growth; its DCAI growth is price, not units.

**C. Pricing power — PRESENT, but shortage rent, not moat rent.** Q2'26 looks spectacular: revenue **$16.1B, +25% YoY** (fastest since 2011, despite the Altera deconsolidation); **DCAI $6.3B, +59% YoY, operating margin 16.1% → 39.5%**; GAAP GM 27.5% → 40.4%. Industry context: server CPU prices **+10–20% since March 2026**, consumer +5–10%, another **8–10% guided for 2H26**, and Intel reportedly **sold out of server CPU capacity for 2026** amid an acute AI-driven shortage. **A monopolist raises price and keeps share. Intel raised price and kept losing share.** Q1'26 commentary attributes part of AMD's record gain to Intel's supply constraint. The test arrives in 2027–28 when capacity loosens.

**D. Cost advantage / manufacturing scale — INVERTED.** Credit where due: **18A is real and shipping** — Q2 output ran ~25% above target and +50% QoQ, and **Xeon 6+ "Clearwater Forest," the first 18A server product, launched in the quarter.** After a decade of missed roadmaps this is the first hard evidence of manufacturing recovery. But the fabs are a **−36.2% operating margin** business: owning capacity is an advantage only if it is cheaper than renting from TSMC, and on current unit economics it is dramatically more expensive. TSMC must be matched twice consecutively (N2 → A16) before "parity" means anything durable.

**E. Foundry as a business — NO MOAT. The clearest call in the report.** Q2'26 Foundry: revenue **$5.8B (+31% YoY)**, operating loss **−$2.1B (−36.2%)**, improved from −$3.2B/−71.7% a year ago. That revenue is overwhelmingly **internal transfer**; external leading-edge revenue is immaterial. 18A external commitments: Amazon, Microsoft, US DoD — meaningful but small, two of three strategically motivated. **14A committed external customers: ZERO**; two prospects have early PDK access, decisions expected 2H26–1H27. Break-even targeted exiting 2027. **Loss-narrowing from internal volume and packaging is operating leverage, not competitive advantage.** Moves to *narrow* only on two committed high-volume 14A logos at second-source scale.

**F. Sovereign barrier — REAL, but a subsidy, not a moat.** The US government holds **433.3M shares at $20.47 (~9.9%, $8.9B)**, funded by $5.7B of unpaid CHIPS grants plus $3.2B Secure Enclave; passive, no board seat. Plus **Nvidia $5B @ $23.28** and **SoftBank $2B @ $23.00**. Intel is the only US-headquartered leading-edge logic manufacturer — genuine strategic scarcity. **But sovereign support raises the floor on survival, not the ceiling on returns**, and it imports political capital-allocation risk — pressure to build ahead of demand is exactly the disease that produced −22% operating margin in 2024.

**G. AI accelerators — ABSENT where the profit pool is.** Crescent Island enters customer testing 2H26; Jaguar Shores 2027. Management's claim that "AI-driven businesses grew >70% YoY and are ~70% of revenue" is a **definitional stretch** counting all Xeon and AI-PC silicon. Marketing, not accelerator position.

## 3. Accounting flag for the Committee (important)

The **$11.0B GAAP net loss / −$2.16 EPS is non-cash and perverse**: driven by a **$12.529B mark-to-market loss on Escrowed Shares** held for the Dept. of Commerce under the CHIPS Secure Enclave agreement, accounted for as a derivative liability. **The loss gets bigger when the stock goes up.** Non-GAAP net income was **+$2.2B / $0.42**.

Consequence: **Intel's GAAP EPS is now anti-correlated with its share price.** Any screen, model or trailing-P/E built on GAAP EPS is garbage until this unwinds. 巴爺爺 must normalise it explicitly; 賭馬狗 should exclude INTC from GAAP-EPS factor screens.

## 4. Threat assessment — steel-manning the disruption case

**Threat 1 — Arm + in-house custom silicon permanently removes the socket (structural, already ~half done).** Graviton, Axion, Cobalt and Grace are vertical integration by the buyer, not a cyclical shift. Arm at **50% of hyperscaler compute from 18% in two years** is the fastest architectural displacement in the category's history. The steel-manned bear case: x86 becomes the enterprise-legacy and edge architecture — a large, profitable, *shrinking-share* annuity — while growth accrues to Arm and accelerators. Nothing in Q2 contradicts it: **units flat while the market grew 10%**.

**Threat 2 — TSMC's scale is unbridgeable AND Intel's structure makes it un-fixable.** TSMC's advantage is compounding learning-curve volume plus **neutrality**. Intel Foundry's every credible large customer — Apple, Qualcomm, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom — competes with Intel Products. That conflict is structural and cannot be engineered away while one company owns both. TSMC need not out-execute Intel; it merely has to not be Intel's competitor. Zero committed 14A customers this late is consistent with this being the binding constraint, not process quality.

**Threat 3 — funding and dilution.** Share count ~**5.04B**, up from ~4.23B in 2023 (~19% dilution) at issue prices of $20.47–$23.28. With capex >$20B in 2026 and higher in 2027 against ~$9B of non-GAAP earnings, the funding gap is structural. Assume more dilution or asset sales.

## 5. Ten-year test

1. **14A lands ≥2 committed, high-volume, non-strategic external customers by 2027.** Binary; without it the foundry is a permanent capital sink and separation is the correct answer.
2. **Process leadership sustained across two consecutive nodes**, not one.
3. **x86 stabilises above ~50% of data-centre CPU revenue** and the category holds >50% of datacentre compute against Arm. Both trending the wrong way.
4. **A credible accelerator position** — or explicit acceptance of being a host-CPU-and-wafer supplier into a profit pool it does not own, valued accordingly.
5. **Capital discipline survives government ownership.**
6. **ROIC crosses WACC on a normalised (non-shortage) basis.** Until then every other bullet is narrative.

Probability judgement: (1) and (3) are each roughly coin-flips and largely independent. Compounded, "clearly stronger in ten years" is a minority case. The modal outcome is a structurally profitable but share-losing x86 annuity attached to a subsidised, roughly break-even foundry.

## 6. Asset-value verdict and margin of safety

**This is an asset-value / special-situation case, NOT a quality compounder.** With TTM ROIC of 2.45% against a ~10% WACC there is **no franchise return to discount**; a DCF on Intel is a DCF on management's promises. The correct frame is **sum-of-parts**: Products (normalised mid-cycle, *not* shortage-peak margins) + Foundry (fab replacement value, heavily haircut for purpose-specificity and −36% margins) + **49% Altera** (Silver Lake bought 51% for ~$3.3B, implying ~$8.75B whole) + residual **Mobileye** stake (note the $3.9B impairment as a realisable-value signal) − net debt − funding gap, **plus a separately-priced call option on 14A**.

**The punchline the Committee must hear: the market has already stopped treating it as a cigar butt.** INTC trades around **$92** (52-week range **$18.97–$142.35**) on ~5.04B shares — roughly **$413–464B** market cap, at **~105x forward earnings and ~7.9x sales**, on 2.45% ROIC. The stock is up ~4–5x from where the government, Nvidia and SoftBank were issued shares nine months ago.

An asset-value framework can defend an order-of-magnitude figure around **$150–250B** (rough estimate; 𢦀鳩仔 must build the real SOTP and 巴爺爺 must supply the Q2 balance sheet). That is **one-half to one-third of the current price.**

**A cigar butt bought at 100x forward earnings is not a cigar butt. It is a momentum position wearing a value costume.**

### Margin of safety demanded: **50%**
A binary, government-entangled, capital-intensive turnaround with a five-year record of sub-WACC returns and an unresolved foundry warrants the widest discount in our framework — **50% below a conservatively-built SOTP**, implying a buy zone around **$15–25/share**, approximately where the sovereign, Nvidia and SoftBank were issued stock. **At ~$92 the margin of safety is negative.**

Recommendation: **no moat-based position.** If Morris or 老詹 want exposure to the CPU shortage cycle, that is a *trading* thesis to be sized and stopped by Zac and John — it must not be booked as a quality compounder or long-term moat holding, because on the evidence it is neither.

*Erosion call logged for the KPI record: I call the Q2'26 margin and revenue improvement cyclical shortage rent rather than moat repair, before the numbers show it. Falsification test is 2027–28 — if DCAI holds ~40% operating margins and server revenue share stops falling once capacity loosens, I am wrong and the product moat should be re-rated to narrow/stable.*
