## 1. Summary & Grade

**Grade: C+ (improving from D)** — a genuine operating inflection sitting on top of a still-broken capital structure.

Q2 2026 is the first quarter in five years where Intel's income statement looks like a real semiconductor company again: revenue $16.1B (+25% YoY, fastest growth since ~2011), GAAP gross margin 40.4% vs 27.5% a year ago, DCAI revenue +59% at a 39.5% operating margin, and non-GAAP EPS of $0.42 against a $0.21 consensus. This is the seventh consecutive quarter of beating guidance.

Three things stop this from being a B:

1. **Intel Foundry still lost $2.1B in the quarter** on $5.8B of (overwhelmingly internal) revenue — a −36.2% operating margin. It is improving ($3.2B loss → $2.1B YoY) but it is not close to breakeven and it consumes the entire profit of the product groups.
2. **The capital structure was rescued by ~22% dilution, not by cash generation.** Share count is up from roughly 4.15B three years ago to 5.08B, with a further 143M escrowed shares and 241M $20-strike warrants outstanding. Any per-share recovery starts from a much wider base.
3. **True free cash flow remains negative and capex is going up, not down** — 2026 gross capex guidance was raised from $18B to **over $20B**, with 2027 higher still.

Reported GAAP results are unusable at face value: Q2 GAAP EPS was **$(2.16)** — an $11.0B net loss — driven almost entirely by a **$12.5B non-cash mark-to-market loss on Escrowed Shares** held for the U.S. Department of Commerce, a liability that *grows as the stock rises*. Per firm rule, I weight cash flow and segment operating results over the GAAP bottom line here — but I do **not** dismiss the escrowed-share charge as cosmetic (see §5).

**Financial score: 55 / 100.**

**The single most important cash-flow fact: H1 2026 operating cash flow exceeded capital spending on PP&E by $1.9B, versus a $5.9B shortfall in H1 2025 — a $7.8B year-over-year swing.** On a pre-partner basis, Intel has stopped burning cash on operations plus capex. That is the survival question answered, at least for now.

**But the headline "adjusted free cash flow of $(8,419)M" in Q2'26 needs unpacking:** it was driven by **net partner outflows of $12,216M** — payments relating to the semiconductor co-investment programme (SCIP) partner structures, not operating deterioration. This is a balance-sheet/ownership transaction, not a P&L burn. Investors reading the headline as "Intel burned $8.4B this quarter" are misreading it. Conversely, investors reading FY2025's "$(1.6)B adjusted FCF" as near-breakeven are also misreading it: **on a plain OCF-minus-gross-capex basis FY2025 burned $8.0B.** Intel's "adjusted FCF" adds back government incentives and partner contributions and is a materially flattering non-GAAP construct.

**Capex is going the wrong way for FCF:** 2026 gross capex guidance was **raised from $18B to over $20B**, and management guided 2027 capital spending **higher still**. At $20B+ against ~$40B of annualized OCF-if-margins-hold, Intel can self-fund — but only just, and only if the Q2 margin level persists. There is no error budget.

## 7. Accounting Red Flags

**1. Escrowed Shares derivative — the dominant GAAP distortion (SEVERE, but disclosed).**
Intel accounts for shares held in escrow for the U.S. Department of Commerce under the CHIPS Act Secure Enclave agreement as a **derivative liability marked to fair value through earnings**. Liability: **$2.7B at 27 Dec 2025 → $15.6B at 27 Jun 2026.** Charge: **$12.5B in Q2'26, $13.6B in H1'26.**
*This creates a perverse mechanic: the better the stock does, the larger the reported loss.* The accounting is technically correct and fully disclosed — no manipulation. **But do not dismiss it as "fake."** The liability measures a real transfer of ~$15.6B of economic value from existing shareholders to the U.S. Government. It is non-cash, but it is not costless. Analysts excluding it entirely are understating true shareholder dilution. Expect continued large, volatile, stock-price-driven swings in GAAP EPS every quarter — GAAP EPS is now essentially uninterpretable for Intel.

**2. Chronic "one-off" impairments — the multi-year pattern (HIGH).**
- FY2024: record $18.8B net loss, GAAP EPS $(4.38) vs non-GAAP $(0.13) — **a $4.25/share gap**.
- FY2025: **$950M** of asset impairments and accelerated depreciation on manufacturing assets "determined to have no remaining operational use."
- H1 FY2026: **$3.9B goodwill impairment at the Mobileye reporting unit.**
When impairments recur every single year for three consecutive years, they are **run-rate, not exceptional.** The FY2024 GAAP/non-GAAP gap of $4.25/share is one of the widest I have seen at a mega-cap. Mobileye was a ~$15.3B acquisition; a further writedown is a live risk.

**3. Non-standard "adjusted free cash flow" (HIGH).**
Intel's adjusted FCF adds back government incentives, partner contributions and finance-lease payments. FY2025: **adjusted FCF $(1.6)B vs true FCF (OCF $9.7B − gross capex $17.7B) of $(8.0)B** — a **$6.4B flattering gap**. Any committee member using Intel's headline FCF number is understating the burn by ~80%. Use OCF minus gross capex.

**4. Intersegment inventory reserves and Foundry transfer pricing (MEDIUM-HIGH).**
FY2025 carried **$849M of higher intersegment inventory reserves** (lower-of-cost-or-NRV) on the early 18A ramp. ~95% of Foundry revenue is intersegment. The split of profit between Intel Foundry and the product groups is therefore an internal transfer-pricing policy decision, and the reported narrowing of the Foundry loss is not fully independent of that policy. **Monitor:** if Foundry losses shrink while CCG/DCAI margins compress with no external explanation, the "improvement" is a reclassification. So far Q2'26 shows *both* improving simultaneously, which is reassuring.

**5. Foundry as a separate legal/reporting subsidiary (MEDIUM — watch item).**
Intel Foundry operates as a separate entity with standalone financials. Combined with the DOC's 241M-share warrant that triggers if Intel drops below 51% ownership, this structure both (a) enables future external investment/IPO of Foundry and (b) makes a full separation extremely expensive. Watch for any change in the consolidation or segment definition that resets the loss baseline.

**6. Government funding accounted for as equity/derivative, not grant income (POSITIVE).**
The U.S. Government's investment sits in equity with the escrow portion as a derivative liability. It does **not** flow through the P&L as grant income. This is the conservative treatment and is a credit to Intel's accounting. Government incentives that offset capex are, however, netted into the adjusted FCF metric — see #3.

**7. Share-count creep (HIGH).** +17.0% YoY basic share count in Q1'26; ~+21–22% over three years; up to ~+30% fully loaded. Quantified in §5. This is the largest sustained dilution by a US mega-cap outside of a bankruptcy reorganization in recent memory.

**8. Earnings vs cash-flow divergence — CLEAN this quarter (POSITIVE).** Q2'26 GAAP net loss of $(11.0)B against **+$7.0B of operating cash flow**. The divergence runs in the *safe* direction: cash far exceeds accounting earnings, which is the signature of non-cash charges, not of aggressive revenue recognition. Per firm rule I weight the cash. **I was unable to verify receivables and inventory growth versus revenue growth this session — [UNVERIFIED]. This is a gap and should be checked in the full 10-Q; a +25% revenue quarter is exactly when channel stuffing would hide.**

The FY2026 Q3 and Q4 prints — specifically whether gross margin holds above 41% and whether external foundry revenue holds above ~$290M/quarter — will determine whether this becomes a B or reverts.

**Fundamentals quality grade: C+ (trajectory positive).**

## 10. The Three Numbers That Matter Most

1. **Intel Foundry quarterly operating loss: $(2.1)B in Q2 FY2026** (vs $(3.2)B in Q2 FY2025 and $(2.4)B in Q1 FY2026; $(10.3)B for FY2025). This is the burn rate that decides whether IDM 2.0 works. It must reach roughly $(1.0)B/quarter by mid-2027 to keep the 2027-ish breakeven story alive.

2. **Intel Foundry external revenue: $293M in Q2 FY2026 — versus $307M for the whole of FY2025.** The only hard evidence that Intel is becoming a merchant foundry rather than an internal cost centre. If this holds above ~$290M/quarter and grows, the thesis is real. If it was a one-off prepayment or a single customer, it isn't.

3. **GAAP gross margin: 40.4% in Q2 FY2026** (vs 27.5% in Q2 FY2025 and ~32.7% in FY2024; guided to 41.0% for Q3). This is the direct read on 18A yields and mix. Sustained expansion past 45% would validate the process-node recovery; a stall below 41% would mean the Q2 print was mix, not manufacturing.

*Watch-item (4th):* **share count 5,083M basic (Q1'26) versus ~4.15B three years ago, with 241M $20-strike DOC warrants outstanding.** Every per-share target must be built on this base.
