## 1. Situation summary

### China revenue exposure — larger than MU's, but mostly "ship-to"
- **[F]** Per the FY2026 Form 10-K (fiscal year ended Jan 31, 2026, filed Mar 11, 2026): China was **36% of net revenue (~$2.97B)**, down from **43%** in FY2025. A **substantial majority of China shipments are to non-China-based customers** whose factories/contract manufacturers sit in China (ship-to, not end-demand); only a small portion of product ships into China and stays there (MRVL 10-K FY2026, SEC).
- **[F]** The 10-K flags that some China sales **require export licenses**, that licenses have in the past been **delayed or denied**, and that existing licenses **may be revoked** on policy changes. It also flags the risk that a **revenue-share fee on China-derived sales** (the mechanism floated in 2025 and formalized for advanced GPUs in Jan 2026 — see below) could erode gross margins or prompt Marvell to curtail China sales if extended to its products (MRVL 10-K FY2026).
- **Delta vs. MU:** Micron's China demand shock is *behind* it (2023 CAC ban → Oct 2025 data-center exit; residual ~low). Marvell's China number is *current and large* (36%), but its character is different — mostly ODM/contract-manufacturing ship-to that can migrate with electronics assembly (Vietnam/Malaysia/Mexico) over time rather than sovereign Chinese end-demand. The genuinely at-risk slice (products consumed in China: carrier, enterprise networking, some data-center interconnect) is much smaller than 36% but not publicly quantified — flag for 巴爺爺 to pin down from filings.

### Export-control regime for data-center silicon — thaw with strings (Jan–Jul 2026)
- **[O] Jan 15, 2026:** BIS published a **revision to the license review policy for advanced computing commodities**: exports of **NVIDIA H200-class and below** to China/Macau moved from presumption-of-denial to **case-by-case**, implementing the administration's **Dec 8, 2025** announcement of H200 sales in exchange for a **25% export fee**. Conditions include: US supply sufficiency, exporter certification that China production **"will not divert global foundry capacity"** from US end users, security procedures, third-party performance testing, and a cap: China sales of a model may not exceed **50% of its cumulative US end-use sales** (Federal Register, Jan 15, 2026; Covington, Jan 2026; IAPS analysis).
- **[F]** Washington has approved H200 sales to **~10 Chinese firms** (Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, JD.com among them), **up to 75,000 chips each**, via NVIDIA or authorized distributors (IAPS/Introl summaries, 2026). **[F] early Jul 2026:** Reports that Chinese authorities told Alibaba/ByteDance/DeepSeek they may receive permission to buy **<200k H200s** for training lifted semis including MRVL on Jul 9 (StockStory/FinancialContent, Jul 9, 2026).
- **Read-through for MRVL:** Marvell's crown-jewel custom AI silicon (AWS Trainium-family, Microsoft Maia 200) is designed **for US hyperscalers deploying in US/allied data centers** — it is not itself exported to China, so the direct export-control cut on the AI franchise is limited. The exposure is **second-order**: (a) MRVL networking/interconnect/optical-DSP content that ships into China systems; (b) any future BIS move to control high-end **networking and interconnect** silicon (a repeatedly discussed gap in the controls) would touch Marvell directly; (c) the "no foundry diversion" certification language shows BIS is now explicitly policing **TSMC/CoWoS allocation** as a policy object.
- **[F]** No Marvell-specific **Entity List** action, Chinese **SAMR antitrust** probe, or MOFCOM measure found as of Jul 19, 2026. Context: the US side said at the **May 14–15, 2026 Trump–Xi Beijing summit** that China agreed to suspend antitrust probes of NVIDIA/Qualcomm/Intel; MOFCOM said semiconductor-supply-chain investigations "would be terminated," but **no formal SAMR case closures have been published** (TechTimes, May 22/27, 2026). China's antitrust/anti-dumping toolkit remains loaded and has been used against US chipmakers (Qualcomm probe, Oct 2025; anti-dumping probe into US legacy-chip suppliers) — Marvell is a plausible future target if the truce breaks, given its share in networking silicon.

### TSMC dependence — the defining structural exposure (worse than MU)
- **[F]** Marvell is **fabless**. Its leading-edge data-center products (custom AI ASICs, 800G/1.6T optical DSPs, switching) are built at **TSMC on 5nm/3nm**, with the announced 2nm platform also at TSMC; advanced packaging is TSMC **CoWoS** (MRVL 10-K risk factors; Silicon Analysts, Q1 2026). There is **no Marvell-owned fab, no second source at the leading edge, and no CHIPS-funded self-hedge**.
- **Explicit contrast with our MU finding (2026-07-18):** MU's Taiwan risk is *geographic* — it owns its Taiwan fabs and is actively diversifying (Idaho 2027, Hiroshima, Singapore; $6.1B CHIPS + ~$200B US plan). **MRVL's Taiwan risk is contractual *and* geographic and is effectively 100% at the leading edge**, with diversification not in Marvell's own control (TSMC Arizona helps only as TSMC chooses to allocate US capacity, and CoWoS remains overwhelmingly in Taiwan). MRVL is therefore **more TSMC/Taiwan-dependent than MU** in any strait-disruption scenario, and additionally exposed to **TSMC pricing power**: **[F] Jul 16, 2026** TSMC posted record Q2 revenue ($39.3B, above guidance; FY growth >30–40% signaled) but warned of **higher prices**, which chilled the chip complex and hit MRVL ~−8% (TechTimes, Jul 16, 2026; 24/7 Wall St, Jul 16, 2026).

### CoWoS advanced-packaging allocation — the chokepoint within the chokepoint
- **[F]** TSMC CoWoS capacity is heading to roughly **120,000–140,000 wafers/month by end-2026** (from ~13k/month end-2023), with lines **fully booked** against 2026 demand estimated near **1.0–1.15M wafers**; the supply-demand gap is narrowing from ~20% toward ~10% by year-end (TechTimes Jul 11, 2026; FinancialContent/36kr, Feb 2026).
- **[F]** **NVIDIA has secured ~60%** of CoWoS capacity; roughly 40% is split among **Broadcom, AMD, Marvell, and Google's TPU program**. Marvell is reported to have secured **~55,000 wafers for 2026** for its AWS (Trainium 2) and Microsoft (**Maia 200**, N3E) custom programs (Astute Group; 36kr, 2026). Broadcom + Marvell control **~95% of the ASIC co-design market** (Tom's Hardware, May 2026).
- **Implication:** CoWoS is a single-vendor, single-island chokepoint. Marvell's growth ceiling in FY2027 is partly set by TSMC allocation decisions in which NVIDIA is the anchor customer — and, per the new BIS certification language, allocation is now also a **US policy variable**. Any China-bound production Marvell's ecosystem touches must be certified as non-diverting.

### US–China truce status (shared frame with MU report, updated)
- **[O] Oct 30, 2025:** Busan one-year truce (tariff relief for pause of China's Oct 9, 2025 rare-earth regime; US suspended the 50% subsidiary rule for a year); **reaffirmed at the May 2026 Beijing summit**. **Key elements expire late 2026 / end-2026** (Coface; CFR; FEE).
- **[F] Jun 22, 2026:** China added **10 US firms (incl. MP Materials, USA Rare Earth)** to its export-control list — the most significant escalation since Busan (Al Jazeera, Jun 22, 2026; SCMP). **[F] Jul 2026:** Rare-earth curbs continue to disrupt chip supply chains despite the truce (Astute Group, Jul 2026; SCMP). The truce is **holding but fraying**; expiry ~Oct–Dec 2026 is the dominant dated catalyst, as for MU.

### Taiwan Strait posture (unchanged from 2026-07-18 MU assessment)
- **[F]** Coercive-but-status-quo: near-constant 5–6 PLA warships around Taiwan; **134 ADIZ air incursions in June 2026**; no blockade/quarantine; insurers increasingly pricing blockade risk (AEI China & Taiwan Update, Jul 2, 2026; CFR Conflict Tracker). **[O] Jun 9, 2026:** Taiwan tightened its own regulation of AI-chip exports to China.

### Tariffs (Section 232) — a live cost watch item
- **[O] Jan 15, 2026:** 25% tariff on a narrow set of advanced logic semiconductors (AI accelerators; HTSUS 8471.50/8471.80/8473.30), with a Phase 2 broader tariff recommended and a **tariff-offset program favoring US-investing companies** (White House proclamation; EY, Feb 2026 — per our MU file). Unlike MU (only US memory maker, $200B US buildout, CHIPS recipient), **Marvell has no fab investment to earn offsets with**; its imported TSMC-made silicon (or its customers' imported systems) is on the wrong side of this policy design. Whether custom hyperscaler ASICs are captured depends on HTSUS classification of the imported articles — unresolved; flag for The dictator (Regulatory) to track the Commerce data-center-semiconductor report triggered Jul 1, 2026.

## 3. Transmission to markets

1. **Revenue channel:** China 36% ship-to → truce collapse or Chinese retaliation disrupts contract-manufacturing flows and the in-China end-demand slice; license revocations directly cut carrier/enterprise sales.
2. **Growth-ceiling channel:** CoWoS allocation and TSMC N3/N2 wafer supply cap the custom-ASIC ramp (AWS, Microsoft) that carries the entire bull thesis; NVIDIA's ~60% anchor share makes MRVL a residual claimant.
3. **Cost channel:** TSMC price increases (flagged Jul 16, 2026) plus potential Section 232 Phase 2 tariffs on imported advanced silicon compress margins with no offset program to shelter a fabless firm.
4. **Policy-linkage channel:** the BIS "no foundry diversion" certification makes TSMC capacity a bargaining chip in US-China talks — headlines here now move MRVL even when Marvell isn't named.
5. **Sentiment channel:** after +175% YTD, MRVL trades as a high-multiple AI proxy; single-day moves of 6–8% on macro/capex headlines (Jul 15–16, 2026) — geopolitical headlines will be amplified similarly.
6. **Severe channel:** strait disruption halts both wafer starts and CoWoS packaging with no substitute on earth for either; unlike memory (fungible spot market), custom ASICs have zero alternative supply — output goes to zero for the duration.

## 5. Scenarios with tripwires (12-month, prospective LONG)

### Base — "Managed competition, allocation-constrained growth" (~55–60%)
Truce messily renewed late 2026; H200-style case-by-case licensing persists (China AI demand thaw continues); no new controls on networking silicon; MRVL keeps its ~55k-wafer CoWoS allocation as capacity grows to 120–140k wpm; TSMC price hikes nibble margins; strait stays coercive-but-status-quo. Geopolitics is a volatility source; the stock trades on hyperscaler capex and Maia 200/Trainium execution.
**Tripwires confirming base:** truce extension headlines Oct–Dec 2026; no Federal Register action on networking/interconnect ECCNs; TrendForce/DigiTimes 2027 CoWoS tables showing MRVL share held or grown; SAMR publishes formal case closures for US chipmakers; PLA activity within 2025–26 ranges (ADIZ ~100–150/mo, no live-fire closures near main ports).

### Adverse — "Truce lapses, controls widen, fabless pays the tariff" (~30%)
Truce expires without renewal → China re-activates the rare-earth regime and the 50% subsidiary rule snaps back; Beijing retaliates against US semis (entity-list additions, SAMR probe or procurement/"domestic substitution" directives touching Marvell's networking share); BIS extends controls to high-end interconnect/optical DSPs; Section 232 Phase 2 lands on advanced-silicon imports with no offset for fabless firms; NVIDIA/TSMC dynamics squeeze MRVL's CoWoS share or price.
**Market impact (estimate):** 25–40% drawdown from mid-July levels — larger than MU's adverse band because the starting multiple is higher and the China revenue at stake is current, not residual.
**Tripwires:** (1) no truce extension by ~Nov 1, 2026, or new Chinese export-control/entity-list additions targeting US semis; (2) BIS proposed/interim rule covering networking, switching, or optical-interconnect ECCNs; (3) MOFCOM/SAMR document naming Marvell, or a Chinese ministry "secure and controllable" networking-procurement directive; (4) Phase 2 proclamation covering MRVL-relevant HTSUS lines without fabless offsets (watch the Commerce data-center report follow-through from Jul 1, 2026); (5) credible reports of MRVL CoWoS allocation cuts or TSMC price increases above ~mid-single-digit %; (6) an AWS/Microsoft program cancellation framed around supply security (overlaps with Elon Musk's industry coverage — coordinate).

### Severe — "Strait crisis" (~5–8%)
Blockade, quarantine, or kinetic incident. All MRVL leading-edge wafer supply and CoWoS packaging halts; custom-ASIC programs stop with no alternative foundry; China ship-to revenue seizes simultaneously. **This scenario is categorically worse for MRVL than for MU** — MU at least owns fabs in Japan/Singapore/US-in-progress and sells a fungible commodity; MRVL owns no capacity and sells single-source custom silicon.
**Market impact (estimate):** 50%+ drawdown amid broad risk-off; recovery timeline tied entirely to TSMC restoration.
**Tripwires (early warnings — same watchlist as MU report):** PRC "quarantine"/customs-inspection language toward Taiwan shipping; live-fire zones enclosing Kaohsiung/Taipei approaches; ADIZ incursions sustained >300/mo or unbounded exercises; US non-combatant evacuation advisories; sharp war-risk insurance repricing for strait transits; PLA reserve mobilization.

### Black-swan candidates
- **Taiwan quarantine short of war** — halts fab logistics while markets debate whether it "counts"; MRVL has zero buffer.
- **Weaponized CoWoS allocation** — US policy (via the Jan 2026 certification regime) or TSMC commercial choices reorder packaging priority in a crisis; MRVL as the smallest of the big four allocants is most squeezable.
- **Hyperscaler in-sourcing shock** — AWS or Microsoft moves co-design in-house or to Broadcom; geopolitically flavored if framed as supply-chain security. Low probability near-term (Maia 200 just ramped) but thesis-defining.
- **Rare-earth/gallium full embargo** on truce collapse — input shock to TSMC itself; sector-wide, MRVL fully exposed via its foundry.

- **Pairing note:** in tariff/industrial-policy scenarios MU outperforms MRVL (offset-eligible vs. fabless importer); holding both partially internalizes that hedge while doubling the Taiwan tail — sizing must reflect the shared factor.

**Overall geopolitical risk rating for a prospective LONG: MEDIUM-HIGH** — one notch above MU's MEDIUM. Actionable 12-month policy risk is comparable (same truce clock, and MRVL's custom-ASIC core is not directly export-controlled; no entity-list or Chinese action names Marvell today). But three structural facts push MRVL above MU: (1) **China is 36% of current revenue** (mostly relocatable ship-to, but live) versus MU's already-realized exit; (2) **effectively 100% leading-edge dependence on TSMC wafers *and* CoWoS packaging with no owned capacity and no self-hedge in motion**; (3) US industrial policy (tariff offsets, CHIPS) is a tailwind for MU and a **neutral-to-headwind for a fabless importer**. The Taiwan tail is the same event for both names — but it hits MRVL harder.
